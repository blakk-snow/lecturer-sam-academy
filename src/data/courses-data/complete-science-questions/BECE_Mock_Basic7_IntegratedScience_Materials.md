### Question 1 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- P - **solid** (1 mark).
- Q - **liquid** (1 mark).
- R - **gas** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: One mark for each correct state; no half marks.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- P - **the particles are closely packed in a regular pattern and the material keeps its own shape** (1 mark).
- Q - **the particles are close together but irregular and there is a flat surface; the material takes the shape of the bottom of the jar** (1 mark).
- R - **the particles are far apart, moving, and spread through the whole jar** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The reason must come from the figure; 'because it is a solid' earns no mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- (i) **It escapes and spreads out into the room** (1 mark).
- (ii) **The particles of a gas move freely in all directions, so a gas spreads to fill the space available** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'It finishes' alone earns no mark in (i).*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- P - any solid, e.g. **salt, sugar, charcoal, stone, cooking pot** (1 mark).

- Aluminium - **Al** (1 mark).
- Helium - **He** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A symbol written wholly in capitals, such as NA or AL, earns no mark.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **Place the sample in a simple circuit with a cell and a bulb** (accept: hammer it gently, or rub it) (1 mark).
- **If the bulb lights, the sample conducts electricity and is a metal** (accept: it flattens into a sheet instead of breaking; it is shiny) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A test with no stated result earns 1 mark only.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO differences, each stated for both sides: **metals conduct heat and electricity, non-metals generally do not**; **metals are shiny, non-metals are dull**; **metals can be beaten into sheets and drawn into wires, non-metals are brittle**; **most metals are solids with high melting points, non-metals may be solids, liquids or gases** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: A statement about metals only, with nothing said about non-metals, earns no mark.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- This is the first sub-strand of Basic 7 and it sets the language of the whole course. Insist from the beginning on the words matter, material, state, solid, liquid, gas, element and symbol, used exactly.
- In Question 1(b) the marks are for evidence taken from the figure. Candidates who merely repeat the answer to (a) in other words earn nothing; teach them to point to the spacing, the pattern and the movement of the particles.
- Question 2(b) is the classic table of shape and volume. The liquid row, no fixed shape but a fixed volume, is the one most often lost, and it is worth demonstrating by pouring the same water through three differently shaped containers.
- Chemical symbols are a mark-earning skill with a strict rule: a capital first letter and a small second letter. NA, CL and AL are wrong and earn nothing. Give a two-minute symbol drill at the start of every lesson until the first twenty are secure.
- In Question 5 the letters W, X, Y and Z replace magnesium, sulphur, argon and potassium, and the atomic numbers of those four boxes have been removed on purpose, so candidates must read the position in the table rather than recall a number.
- The periodic table at this level is about pattern, not theory. Candidates should be able to say that the elements are arranged in order of atomic number, that a group is a vertical column whose members behave alike, and that a period is a horizontal row.
- Follow-up work: let each group assemble twelve materials from the school compound, record texture, appearance, colour and shape in a table, classify them, and then make one useful article from a solid material and report on it. The table and the article together cover the first three indicators of this sub-strand.
