### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- (i) Jupiter - the **largest planet** in the solar system, with the **great red spot** (a giant storm)
- (ii) Saturn - famous for its **bright, wide rings** of ice and rock
- (iii) Uranus - **rotates on its side**, so that it appears to roll along its orbit; it looks blue-green
- (iv) Neptune - the **farthest planet from the Sun**, deep blue, with the strongest winds and the longest orbit
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each (4 marks). The property must be peculiar to that planet. Do NOT award a mark for ‘it is big and cold’, which is true of all four.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- They are **very far away from the Sun** compared with the Earth (1 mark).
- So **very little of the Sun's heat and light reaches them** - the heat spreads out and is very weak at that distance (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. Do NOT accept ‘they are covered with ice’ as the reason - the ice is a result of the cold, not its cause.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Neptune is **much farther from the Sun** than Jupiter (1 mark).
- So its **orbit (path round the Sun) is very much longer**, and it has a far greater distance to cover to complete one journey (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. Accept the additional point that it also travels more slowly along its orbit. Do NOT accept ‘it is lazy’ or ‘it is heavier’.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- A moon (natural satellite) is a **natural body that moves round a planet** (1 mark).
- The outer planets are **very large and have a strong pull (force of gravity)**, so they can hold many moons in orbit round them (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. Accept ‘satellite’ for moon. Do NOT accept ‘a moon is a small star’.*

### Question 5 (9 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **Air (oxygen) to breathe**
- **Water**
- **Food** and a **suitable, moderate temperature** (not extremely hot or cold)
- A **suitable surface or habitat** to live on
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct conditions (3 marks).*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- It is **extremely cold**, because it is very far from the Sun.
- It has **no solid surface** and **no liquid water** - it is made up mainly of gases.
- Its **atmosphere has no oxygen** to breathe (it is mainly hydrogen and helium, with poisonous gases), and **violent storms** blow there constantly.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct reasons (3 marks). The reasons must be about conditions on the planet. Do NOT accept ‘it is too far to travel to’ - that is about reaching it, not about life existing there.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Saturn is made up **mainly of gases and has no solid surface**, so there is nothing firm to stand on and the astronaut would **sink into the planet** (1 mark).
- Besides, the **extreme cold, the crushing pressure and the absence of oxygen** would make survival impossible (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. The absence of a solid surface must appear for the first mark.*

**(d)** [1 marks]

- The **journey takes many years and the conditions would kill a human being**; a probe needs **no air, food or water**, is cheaper and safer, and can send back pictures and readings without returning.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for any one sound reason.*

### Question 6 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- **Balls of different sizes** (or moulded clay, paper mâché, calabash pieces) - to **represent the four planets and the Sun**.
- **Wire, string or thin sticks and a cardboard or wooden base** - to **hold the planets in position** at their different distances; **paint and paper labels** to colour and name them.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for each material correctly named WITH its use, maximum 2 marks. A material named without a use earns no mark. Accept any local material that would really work.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- **Find out the correct order, relative sizes and distances** of Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune from books or the internet.
- **Make or choose balls of the right relative sizes** - the biggest for Jupiter, a slightly smaller one for Saturn, and two smaller ones for Uranus and Neptune.
- **Paint each planet in its colour** and add a card ring round Saturn.
- **Fix the planets with wire or string to the base in the correct order**, spacing them out from the Sun, then **label each one, write a title and display the model** for the class to discuss.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR sensible steps in a workable order (4 marks). This is the ‘construct a model and display it for discussion’ exemplar, so award method marks. The correct ORDER of the planets must appear somewhere for full marks.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- The **correct order** of the planets from the Sun, with each in **its own separate orbit**.
- The **relative sizes** - Jupiter the largest, then Saturn, with Uranus and Neptune much smaller - and the **rings round Saturn**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct points (2 marks).*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- The **sizes and the distances are not to true scale** - the real distances are far too vast to show on a table.
- The planets in the model are **held by wire and do not move**, whereas the real planets **move freely in orbit and rotate**; the model also cannot show the gases, the storms, the many moons or the extreme cold.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct limitations (2 marks). This item rewards candidates who understand that a model is only a representation; accept any sound limitation.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- This sub-strand has ONE content standard and ONE indicator, so the blueprint separates the items by exemplar instead, showing which of the five exemplars each item serves.
- Question 2 depends on Fig. 1 and Question 3 on Fig. 2. The same labels I to IV are used in both figures and refer to the same planets, so a candidate who names them correctly in 2(a) can carry the names into Question 3.
- Teach candidates to read the clues in Fig. 1: I is drawn largest (Jupiter) and II carries the rings (Saturn). The figure is deliberately marked ‘not drawn to scale’, and Question 3(f) asks them to explain what that means.
- There are EIGHT planets. Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006 and is not counted; if a candidate writes nine, mark it wrong but explain why in class.
- In Question 4(a) the property must be PECULIAR to that planet. ‘It is large and cold’ is true of all four and earns no mark; look for the great red spot, the rings, the sideways rotation and the greatest distance from the Sun.
- In Question 4(b) the cold is caused by the great distance from the Sun. Candidates who answer ‘because they are covered with ice’ have reversed cause and effect and earn no mark.
- Question 6 is the modelling exemplar. Award method marks generously for any workable procedure, but the correct ORDER of the four planets must appear for full marks in 6(b). Part 6(d) rewards pupils who understand that a model is only a representation.
- If your class actually built a model, accept it as evidence for Question 6 and let the pupils present it; the exemplar asks for the model to be displayed for discussion.
- Encourage the use of charts, pictures and digital content for this sub-strand, as the first exemplar directs. Where there is no internet, a drawn chart on manila card works just as well.
- The two figures are hand-drawn sketches, matching the style pupils are expected to produce in their own answers (the exemplar asks them to draw the outer planets); redraw them on the chalkboard if you wish.
