### Question 3 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **Hold the handle firmly with both hands, stand with the feet apart** and **swing the hoe so that the blade enters the soil** (1 mark).
- **Pull the loosened soil towards you, turn it over and break the lumps**, removing roots and stones (1 mark).
- **Work backwards in strips across the plot** until the whole bed is dug, then **draw the soil together into a level bed or into ridges** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks as shown. Accept any sound, well-ordered description of the digging, the turning of the soil and the shaping of the bed. This is the curriculum exemplar on explaining how the tools are used to perform specific activities.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Fill the can with clean water, fit the rose and hold the can by the handle** (1 mark).
- **Walk slowly along the bed, tilting the can so that the water falls over the seedlings**, moving it to and fro so that every plant is reached and the soil is wetted evenly (1 mark).
- **The rose breaks the water into many fine drops**, so it **does not wash away the soil or beat down and damage the young seedlings** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks: 2 marks for the method and 1 mark for the reason. The reason mark needs the gentle, fine drops and the protection of the seedlings or the soil.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- The work is **done more quickly and with less effort**, and the job is **done neatly and properly**.
- The **crops, the soil and the tool itself are not damaged**, and there is **less risk of injury** to the user.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO sound reasons (2 marks).*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **The injury:** a **cut on the hand or the foot from a cutlass or a hoe** (accept a blister, a wound from a tool left blade upwards, or poisoning from a sprayer) (1 mark).
- **The prevention:** **keep a safe working distance from other pupils, carry the cutlass with the blade pointing down and away from the body, wear boots and gloves, pass a tool handle first and never play with tools** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 mark for a genuine injury and 1 mark for a prevention that matches it.*

- **Y - watering can** (1 mark).
- **Z - secateurs (pruning shears)** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each (4 marks). Accept 'sickle' for W and 'pruning knife' for Z. Do not accept the hoe for W, since the weeds must first be cut down.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- **W:** the weeds are **tall and woody**, and a **sharp cutlass cuts them down quickly at ground level with a swinging stroke** (1 mark); a small hand tool such as a trowel could not do this work, and the plot must be cleared before it can be tilled (1 mark).
- **Z:** the hedge must be **cut evenly on the top and the sides** (1 mark); **secateurs or shears cut the small branches cleanly without tearing them** or damaging the rest of the hedge (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks for each reason (4 marks). Award the second mark of each pair only where the candidate goes beyond repeating the name of the tool - a comparison with an unsuitable tool, or the quality of the cut, earns it.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **The wheelbarrow** (accept the rake or the watering can) (1 mark).
- It would be needed in **more than one area**: for example, to **cart away the cut weeds from W, bring manure to the ridges in X and carry off the trimmings from Z** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 mark for the tool and 1 mark for a reason that covers at least two of the four areas. A tool named with only one use scores 1 mark.*

### Question 5 (9 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Wash or scrape off the soil after use and dry the tool well.**
- **Rub the metal parts with oil or grease** so that they do not rust.
- **Sharpen the cutting edges regularly with a file**, and **tighten or replace loose or broken handles**.
- **Store the tools in a dry store or shed**, hung up or arranged neatly, and never leave them lying in the field.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR correct ways (4 marks).*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **They last longer**, so the school **saves the money it would spend replacing them**.
- **They work well**, so the work is **easier, faster and neater**.
- **They are safe to use** - blunt blades and loose handles slip and cause injuries - and they are **always ready for the next lesson**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE sound reasons (3 marks).*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Carry a cutlass or any sharp tool with the blade pointing downwards and away from the body**, and **pass a sharp tool handle first**.
- **Keep a safe distance from other pupils when swinging a tool**, never play with or run while carrying one, and **never leave a tool lying with its blade or prongs facing upwards** (accept: wear boots and gloves).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO genuine safety rules (2 marks).*

  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE suitable headings (3 marks).*

- Checking: **compare the information with a second source or the textbook**, or **show it to the teacher, an experienced farmer or the agricultural extension officer** for confirmation (1 mark).

### EXAMINER NOTES

- This paper closes Strand 4. It is practical by nature: if your class worked in the school garden this term, mark their own records and tool-handling against Questions 3, 4 and 5 as well.
- Accept the local and common names of the tools. 'Machete' for cutlass, 'shears' for secateurs and 'digging fork' for garden fork all earn the mark; a vague 'knife' or 'spade' does not.
- In Question 2(a) the candidate must read the sketches. Tell the class beforehand that the rake has teeth on a bar while the garden fork has long prongs at the end of a handle - this is the commonest confusion in the paper.
- Question 4(b) is the discriminating part: a candidate who only repeats the name of the tool scores half the marks. The second mark of each pair needs a real reason - the nature of the weeds or the quality of the cut.
- Do not accept the hoe as the first tool for area W. Tall weeds must be cut down with a cutlass before the plot can be tilled.
- In Question 3(a) and 3(b), the method marks are for a clear, ordered description. A candidate who writes only 'use the hoe to dig' scores 1 mark at most.
- Question 5 carries the safety teaching of the sub-strand. Read the answers to 5(c) aloud in class; these are the rules that prevent accidents during practical lessons.
- Question 6 is the curriculum's album exemplar. If your groups actually produced albums, mark their work against part (a), display the best of them in the garden store, and let the groups present at assembly.
- The two figures are hand-drawn sketches, in the style pupils are expected to produce in their own answers; redraw them on the chalkboard if you wish.
