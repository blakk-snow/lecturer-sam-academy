### Question 1 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **A state of complete physical, mental and social well-being** (1 mark), **and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Any condition that upsets the normal working of the body or mind** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (i) all three kinds of well-being are needed for the first mark.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **Disease lowers the level of health, and the removal or prevention of disease restores health** (1 mark).
- **Freedom from disease alone is not full health, since mental and social well-being are also needed** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept any clear statement of the inverse relationship.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Cholera, measles and tuberculosis** (2 marks for all three; 1 mark for any two).
- Mode of spread of any one named: **cholera - contaminated water or food**; **measles or tuberculosis - droplets in the air from a patient** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A non-communicable disease named among the three loses the first mark.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **He is not completely healthy** (1 mark).
- Reason: **his mental and social well-being is poor, and health is more than the absence of disease** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: No mark for the judgement without the reason.*

- (i) **Malaria** (1 mark); **the female Anopheles mosquito** (accept mosquito) (1 mark).
- (ii) **Drinking or eating water or food contaminated with faeces** (accept dirty hands, flies on food, poor toilets) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'mosquito' alone for the insect.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO actions each with a reason: **drain stagnant water and clear bush and gutters** - removes the breeding places of the mosquito (1 mark); **sleep under treated nets and screen windows** - prevents biting (1 mark); **report and treat fever early** - removes the source of the parasite (1 mark). Maximum 2 marks.
  - *Marking note: No mark for the action without its reason.*

### Question 3 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- (i) **Diseases that are not passed from one person to another** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Kwashiorkor** (accept marasmus, anaemia, rickets, goitre, night blindness) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Any named deficiency disease is acceptable in (ii).*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **too much salt in the food**; **fatty or fried food**; **sugary drinks**; **lack of physical exercise (sitting all day)**; **being overweight** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: The risk factor must come from the passage.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- (i) Any TWO of: **frequent passing of urine**, **great thirst**, **loss of weight in spite of eating**, **tiredness**, **blurred vision**, **sores that heal slowly** (1 mark each).
- (ii) **Stroke, heart failure, kidney damage or damage to the eyes** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A cause written in place of a symptom earns no mark.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **reduce salt, fat and sugar and eat more fruits and vegetables**; **take regular exercise and reduce weight**; **avoid alcohol and tobacco**; **attend the clinic regularly and take prescribed medicines** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: The advice must be practical and related to her condition.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- (i) **A disease in which body cells divide in an uncontrolled way to form a growth that may spread** (1 mark).
- (ii) Any ONE of **lung, breast, cervical, prostate, liver or skin cancer** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The idea of uncontrolled growth of cells is essential in (i).*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **a lump or swelling**; **a sore that does not heal**; **unusual bleeding or discharge**; **a change in a mole or wart**; **a lasting cough or hoarseness**; **difficulty in swallowing**; **a change in bowel or bladder habit**; **unexplained loss of weight** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: Three distinct signs are required.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- (i) Tobacco smoke contains **chemicals that damage the cells of the lungs and air passages**, which then grow uncontrollably (1 mark).
- (ii) **Ultraviolet rays damage the skin cells**, which may grow out of control as skin cancer (1 mark).
- (iii) Mouldy grain carries **poisons (aflatoxins) from fungi that damage the liver** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The idea of damage to cells must appear in (i) and (ii).*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **At an early stage the growth is small and has not spread to other parts of the body** (1 mark).
- **It can then be removed or treated successfully, while a cancer that has spread is much harder to treat** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The idea of spreading must appear.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Ringworm** (accept tinea or athlete's foot for the toes) (1 mark).
- (ii) **Fungi** (1 mark).
- (iii) **Candidiasis (thrush)**, **nail infection** or **fungal eye infection** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Do not accept 'worm' or 'germ' for the group of organisms.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- W - **a shared comb carries infected hairs and skin scales from one scalp to another** (1 mark).
- X - **a shared towel rubs the fungus and skin scales on to another pupil's damp skin** (1 mark).
- Y - **a shared cap passes the fungus from head to head and keeps the scalp warm and sweaty** (1 mark).
- Z - **skin scales with the fungus are left on the wet floor and picked up by bare feet** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Each mark needs the idea of transfer of the fungus from one person to another.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **no sharing of combs, towels, caps, clothing or footwear**; **early treatment of infected pupils at the clinic**; **daily cleaning of bathrooms and wearing slippers in them**; **washing, sun-drying and ironing of clothing, towels and bedding**; **keeping the body clean and dry, especially between the toes**; **avoiding overcrowding and keeping the dormitory airy** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: Three distinct measures; repeated ideas score once.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Personal cleanliness, no sharing of personal articles and early reporting for treatment** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Keeping the surroundings and public bathhouses clean, supporting education and encouraging treatment** (1 mark).
- (iii) **Providing clinics, medicines, trained workers, school screening and public health rules** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The role must match the level named.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **a short bold heading naming the disease**; **a clear drawing of the signs**; **short simple points on how it spreads**; **the steps to prevent it**; **where to go for treatment**; **large letters and few words, in a language the people read** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: Three distinct features of a good poster.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Danger: **the real cause is not treated, so the disease spreads, and sand and hot leaves wound the skin and may cause further infection or scarring** (1 mark).
- Advice: **ringworm is caused by a fungus spread by contact and shared articles, and it is cured with antifungal medicine from the clinic** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The answer must name the fungus as the cause to earn the second mark.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- The published curriculum prints the code B9/JHS3.5.2.2 twice on consecutive pages, once for the content standard on health, disease and common diseases and again for the content standard on fungal diseases, and the indicator code B9/JHS3.5.2.2.1 likewise appears under both. The codes have been reproduced exactly as published and are distinguished in the blueprint as I2 and I4. Teachers should expect to meet the same code twice in the official document.
- The WHO definition must be learnt word for word. Candidates who write only 'absence of disease' lose both marks, because the definition says expressly that health is more than that.
- The commonest error in this sub-strand is to confuse a cause, a symptom and a risk factor. Teach the three words together: a cause produces the disease, a symptom is what the patient feels or shows, and a risk factor is a habit or condition that makes the disease more likely.
- In Question 2(b)(ii) candidates must first decide which of the five diseases on the chart are non-communicable. High blood pressure and diabetes are the two; ringworm, although it is not spread by an insect, is communicable, and this is worth discussing in class.
- Question 5(b) is the heart of the fungal-disease indicator. Every mark turns on the transfer of the fungus from one person to another, so answers such as 'the comb is dirty' earn nothing.
- Question 6(c) touches a real belief in many communities. Candidates should be encouraged to answer with respect but with science: the fungus is the cause, the clinic has the cure, and cleanliness prevents it.
- Follow-up work: let the class carry out its own survey of the diseases reported at the nearest clinic for one month, draw the bar chart, calculate the percentages and classify each disease as communicable or non-communicable. Then let groups design and display posters on ringworm, candidiasis and nail infection, which is exactly the activity the curriculum asks for.
