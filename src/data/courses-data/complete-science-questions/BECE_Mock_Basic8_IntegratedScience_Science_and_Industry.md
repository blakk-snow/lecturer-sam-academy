## MARKING SCHEME - SECTION B (60 marks)

*General rules: (1) Accept any other correct and relevant answer. (2) Award marks for correct points, not for repeating the question. (3) Where a candidate gives more answers than are asked for, mark only the first ones up to the number required. (4) In the design question, mark the reasoning and not the drawing; a labelled sketch and a clear explanation earn full marks. (5) Do not deduct marks for spelling unless the meaning is changed. (6) Where a candidate names a real Ghanaian example that the scheme does not list, award the mark if it fits the point being made.)*

### Question 1 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **P - science** (the careful, orderly study of nature to find out how and why things happen) (1 mark).
- **Q - technology** (the use of that knowledge to make tools, machines and methods) (1 mark).
- **R - innovation** (a new or improved product, method or idea that is actually put to use) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks, 1 mark each. The single word is enough; the explanation in brackets is not required. Do not accept 'invention' for P or Q.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Science supplies the knowledge, and technology puts that knowledge to work** to meet a human need (1 mark).
- **Technology in turn supplies science with instruments** - the microscope, the thermometer, the computer - so that scientists can observe and measure what they could not reach before, and so discover more (1 mark).
- **A correct example** (1 mark), for example: the scientific finding that an electric current produces a magnetic field was used to build the electric motor and the fan; the study of germs led to vaccines and to the refrigerator used to store them; the study of light led to the solar panel.
  - *Marking note: 3 marks: 1 for science feeding technology, 1 for technology feeding science back, 1 for a correct example. A candidate who gives only one direction of the relationship, however well explained, earns at most 2 marks with the example.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **The needs and the problems of the people decide what is studied and what is made.** Where malaria is common, scientists work on malaria; where there is no mains electricity, inventors work on solar lamps (1 mark).
- **So the relationship works in both directions**: science and technology change the way people live, and the way people live, their needs, their money and their government's choices, decide what science and technology do next (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks. The first mark is for society setting the agenda; the second for recognising that the relationship is two-way. An answer that merely repeats 'it goes back to P' earns nothing.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Mobile money** - people send and receive money, pay school fees and buy goods **without travelling to a bank**, which many villages do not have.
- Also accept: **the solar lantern or solar home system** (light and charging where there is no mains electricity); **the solar-powered vaccine refrigerator** at a rural clinic; **the cassava or palm-fruit processing machine** (less of the harvest is wasted); **the solar fish dryer** (clean, fast drying, free of flies); **the drone used to deliver blood and medicines**; **plastic waste recycled into pavement blocks**; **the borehole with a hand pump**.
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 for a real innovation, 1 for the problem it solves. The two must match. A machine named with no problem stated earns 1 mark only.*

### Question 2 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Agriculture** - the tractor, the irrigation pump, the knapsack sprayer, improved seed.
- **Health care** - the X-ray machine, the solar-powered vaccine refrigerator, the laboratory microscope.
- **Communication** - the mobile telephone, the radio, the internet.
- **Transport** - the lorry, the motor bicycle, the aeroplane.
- Also accept: **industry** (processing and packaging machines); **the home** (the refrigerator, the gas cooker, the electric iron); **education** (the computer, the projector); **banking and trade** (mobile money, the ATM); **water supply** (the borehole and pump).
  - *Marking note: 4 marks: 1 mark for each area that is correctly named AND matched with a suitable example. An area with no example, or an example that does not belong to the area, earns nothing for that item.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Work is done faster and with less effort**, so one person can do in a day what once took a week.
- **More food and more goods are produced**, and less of the harvest is wasted, so there is food to sell as well as to eat.
- **Illness is found and treated earlier**, and vaccines, clean water and light at night have lengthened life.
- Also accept: **messages and money move in seconds**; **new jobs, trades and businesses have been created**; **pupils can learn from sources far beyond the school**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE benefits (3 marks). The benefit must be to people, not merely a description of the machine.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Pollution** - smoke and fumes in the air, waste water and chemicals in the rivers, oil and refuse on the land.
- **Destruction of the natural environment** - forests cleared, farmland dug up and rivers silted by mining and building.
- **Mountains of waste** - plastic sachets and bottles that block the gutters and cause floods, and old phones, televisions and refrigerators that nobody can safely destroy.
- Also accept: **some workers lose their jobs to machines**; **accidents and injuries from machines and vehicles**; **noise from mills, generators and loudspeakers**; **less exercise and more time wasted on screens**; **dependence on imported machines, spare parts and fuel**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE problems (3 marks). The problem must follow from the technology; 'people are lazy' alone is too vague unless it is tied to machines doing the work.*

### Question 3 (12 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **X - smoke, or fumes, rising from the burning cables** (1 mark).
- **Y - the stream, or river, running past the yard** (1 mark).
- **Z - the heap of electronic waste (e-waste)** - old televisions, computers, refrigerators and telephones (accept 'scrap' or 'old electrical appliances') (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks, 1 mark each. For Z accept any answer that shows the candidate recognises discarded electrical or electronic goods; 'rubbish' alone is too vague.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **It is breathed in.** The smoke carries poisonous gases and very fine particles that irritate the nose, throat and lungs, causing coughing, chest pain, asthma and, over years, lasting damage to the lungs.
- **It carries metal poisons such as lead.** These enter the blood and can damage the brain and the nerves, and they are especially harmful to young children and to unborn babies.
- Also accept: **the smoke settles on food, water and washing** in the nearby houses; **it causes burning eyes and headaches**; **it hides the air pollution that worsens heart disease**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO effects (2 marks). The answer must say what the smoke does to the body, not merely that it is 'bad'.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Rain washes acids, oils and metal poisons out of the broken appliances** and the run-off carries them down into the stream (1 mark).
- **The stream becomes polluted**: the water is no longer safe to drink, cook or bathe in; fish and other water life are killed; and the poisons pass into people who drink the water, eat the fish or eat crops watered from it (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 for the poisons reaching the water, 1 for the consequence to the water life or to the people. 'It makes the water dirty' alone earns 1 mark at most.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **The gain is small and private, but the harm is large and shared.** A few workers earn a little, while the smoke, the polluted stream and the poisoned soil damage the health of the whole neighbourhood, including children who earn nothing from the yard.
- **The damage lasts far longer than the income.** Metal poisons stay in the soil and the water for many years and the cost of treating illness falls on the families and on the state.
- **The choice is not between this yard and no work at all.** The same metals can be recovered safely at a proper recycling plant, with tools, gloves, masks and no open burning, so the jobs can be kept while the harm is removed.
  - *Marking note: 2 marks for any TWO sound points (2 marks). The best answers weigh the benefit against the cost rather than simply condemning the workers. An answer that blames the workers alone, with no reasoning, earns nothing.*

**(e)** [3 marks]

- **Provide, or licence, a proper recycling plant** where the metals are recovered by machine instead of by open burning.
- **Ban open burning on the site and enforce the ban**, through the Environmental Protection Authority and the assembly's by-laws.
- **Train the workers and supply protective equipment** - gloves, boots, masks - and register them so that their health is checked.
- Also accept: **move the yard away from houses and from the stream**; **require importers and sellers of electrical goods to take back old appliances**; **provide collection points and public education so that e-waste is separated from ordinary refuse**; **support the workers with other work while the site is cleaned up**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE measures (3 marks). The measure must be within the power of the assembly or the government. 'The workers should stop' is not a measure and earns nothing.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- **A single excavator moves in one day what a hundred people with shovels could not move in months**, so a far larger area of forest and farmland is cleared and dug up in a very short time.
- **The machines work in and beside the river itself and use chemicals such as mercury on a large scale**, so the water is muddied and poisoned far faster than the river can clean itself.
- Also accept: **machines reach deep and remote places hand tools never could**, and **the pits and heaps they leave behind are too large for the people to fill in again**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO reasons (2 marks). The answer must compare the scale or the speed with hand mining.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **The rivers become muddy and are poisoned with mercury**, so the water is unsafe and costs far more to treat for town supply.
- **Farmland and forest are destroyed** and left as pits and heaps of sand, so cocoa and food crops are lost and farmers lose their livelihood.
- **People fall ill** - mercury poisoning, skin and stomach disease from dirty water - and the open pits fill with water in which children drown and mosquitoes breed.
- Also accept: **fish and other water life are killed**; **the soil is no longer fertile**; **school attendance falls as children are drawn into the pits**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE effects (3 marks). Mark only the first three. Accept effects on land, water, health or livelihood.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Minerals are produced in quantity** and sold abroad, earning foreign exchange and taxes that pay for roads, schools, clinics and electricity.
- **Jobs and incomes are created** for miners, drivers, mechanics, traders and food sellers, and skills are learnt.
- Also accept: **raw materials for industry**, and **development of roads, power and water in mining districts**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO benefits (2 marks). This part exists to keep the answer balanced; a candidate who refuses to admit any benefit earns nothing here.*

**(d)** [3 marks]

- **Licence, inspect and police the mines**, so that only registered miners work and the law against mining in river beds and forest reserves is enforced.
- **Require every miner to restore the land** - to refill the pits, replace the topsoil and replant trees or crops when the gold is finished - and to lodge money in advance to pay for it.
- **Treat the waste water in settling ponds and ban the careless use of mercury**, replacing it with safer recovery methods.
- Also accept: **train and equip small-scale miners with cleaner methods**; **monitor the rivers regularly and publish the results**; **keep a buffer of untouched land along every river**; **involve the chiefs and the community in watching over the land**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE workable measures (3 marks). 'Stop all mining' is not a way of using the technology and earns nothing; 'stop illegal mining' does earn a mark, because the law is being enforced.*

### Question 5 (8 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- **The problem is stated plainly and is a real one** in a school or a community (1 mark).
- **The goal is stated**: what the device or method must actually do - for example, *it must sound a warning when the tank is full*, or *it must dry fifty kilogrammes of maize in two days without the rain touching it* (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks. This part carries the competency CP 6.1, the ability to define a goal towards solving a problem. A candidate who describes a gadget but never says what problem it solves earns nothing here.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **A list of suitable materials that can really be obtained locally** - for example empty bottles, a float, wire, a bell or buzzer, an old dry cell, bamboo, wire mesh, a polythene sheet, scrap wood (1 mark).
- **A simple labelled sketch** showing the main parts and where each material goes (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks. Award the sketch mark for any drawing with at least two sensible labels. No marks are taken off for untidy drawing; this is a science paper, not an art paper.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **The explanation follows a clear order** - what happens first, next and last (1 mark).
- **It rests on a correct scientific idea** (1 mark): the float rises because it is lighter than water and closes the circuit, so the buzzer sounds; the glass traps heat so the air inside the dryer becomes hotter than the air outside and carries the moisture away; the dark surface absorbs more radiation than a shiny one.
  - *Marking note: 2 marks. The science need not be advanced, but it must be correct. A design that could not possibly work as described earns the first mark only, for the orderly explanation.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **One benefit** (1 mark): water, money, time, food or fuel saved; work made safer or easier; health improved; waste reduced.
- **One difficulty** (1 mark): the cost of the materials; parts that wear out or rust and must be replaced; the device may fail in rain or harmattan dust; somebody must be trained to use and repair it; it may be stolen or damaged.
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 each. Reward honesty: a candidate who can name a real weakness in his or her own design is thinking like an engineer. A candidate who writes 'there is no difficulty at all' earns nothing for the second mark.*

### Question 6 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **The science teacher, a laboratory technician or a qualified engineer or electrician** in the community.
- **The science textbook, an encyclopaedia or the school or public library.**
- **The website or the office of a recognised body** - the Ghana Standards Authority, the Energy Commission, the Food and Drugs Authority, the CSIR, a university or the Ministry of Energy.
- Also accept: **a published scientific article or report**, and **the manufacturer's own documents, read critically alongside an independent source**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE sources (3 marks). 'Social media', 'the internet' on its own, or 'my friends' earn nothing, because they name no responsible author.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Who wrote it, and what do they know?** Is the author a scientist, an institution or an unnamed person forwarding a message?
- **Does any other independent source agree?** A claim that appears in two or three trusted places separately is far stronger than one that appears everywhere in exactly the same forwarded words.
- **Is evidence given?** Are there measurements, a test anyone can repeat, or only promises and pictures?
- Also accept: **how recent the information is**; **whether the writer gains money if you believe it**; **whether the claim contradicts well-established science** - energy cannot be created out of nothing, so a machine that gives out more energy than it takes in is impossible; **whether the language is calm and exact or excited and exaggerated**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE checks (3 marks). Award a mark generously to any candidate who notices that the claim breaks the law of conservation of energy, which was taught in Sub-Strand 4.3.*

**(c)** [4 marks]

- **(i) Two helps** (1 mark each): quick contact with parents and friends, especially in an emergency; access to information, dictionaries, past questions and lessons; sending and receiving money for fees and provisions; a torch, a calculator, a clock and a camera in one pocket.
- **(ii) Two harms** (1 mark each): hours lost from study, chores and sleep; false and harmful information believed and passed on; bullying, insults and contact with strangers; the cost of data and the risk of theft; eye strain, poor posture and less exercise and play.
  - *Marking note: 4 marks in all: 2 + 2. Mark only the first two answers in each part. A candidate who writes only that the phone 'is good' or 'is bad' without saying how earns nothing. This part carries CC 8.2 - look for ideas set out in a clear order.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- This sub-strand is about connections, not about new facts. Reward the candidate who can link a scientific idea to the thing it made possible and to the change it brought to people's lives, even where the wording is simple.
- Question 1(c) is the heart of the indicator. Many candidates see only that science changes society; press the class afterwards to see that society also decides what science is done.
- Question 3 is drawn from the scrap yards that exist on the edge of several Ghanaian towns. Teach it as a problem to be solved, not as a scandal; the workers in the sketch are earning a living in conditions nobody should have to work in.
- Question 4(c) deliberately asks for the benefits of mining. Do not let a class fall into the habit of condemning a technology without weighing it; that is not scientific thinking.
- Question 5 is an open design task. Expect wide variation and mark against the four sub-parts rather than against a model answer. If your class actually built something this term, mark their own project here and display the best designs.
- Question 6 carries the digital-literacy competency DL 5.5. The water-into-fuel claim is invented for this paper, but claims of that kind circulate constantly; a candidate who spots that it breaks the conservation of energy has understood two sub-strands at once.
- Section A items 34, 35 and 40 also test judgement rather than recall. Discuss them with the class after marking.
- The two figures are hand-drawn sketches, in the style pupils are expected to produce in their own answers; redraw them on the chalkboard if you wish.
