  - *Marking note: A practice written against the wrong tool earns no mark.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Scrape and wash off the soil, then dry the blade** (1 mark).
- **Rub the blade with oil and check or wipe the handle** (1 mark).
- **Hang it in a dry store, off the floor** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The three steps must be in a sensible order.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Rust eats away the metal, so the tool becomes blunt, weak and finally breaks** (1 mark).
- **Condition 1: moisture, from water, rain, dew or damp soil** (1 mark).
- **Condition 2: air (oxygen) in contact with the bare metal, especially under soil left on it** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'Dirty tools' alone is not a condition; the moisture must be named.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **the tools used and the work each does**; **how the farmer holds, carries and uses them, and the protective clothing worn**; **what is done to the tools after work (cleaning, sharpening, oiling, repair)**; **where and how the tools are stored**; **the condition of the tools** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'Everything about the farm' earns no mark.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **greet the farmer and ask permission**; **stay together and follow instructions**; **do not touch tools or animals without being told**; **ask questions politely and do not criticise the farmer**; **do not trample the crops or frighten the birds**; **thank the farmer before leaving** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: Accept any clear rule of courtesy or safety.*

**(c)** [4 marks]

- (i) Any TWO of: **oil the wheelbarrow wheel and tighten the bolts**; **refit or replace the loose hoe handles**; **wash out the sprayer after every use** (1 mark each).
- (ii) **A correct reason beside each of the two recommendations given** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: The reason must match the recommendation it is written against.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Four clear rules, each stating an action** (1 mark each), for example: **sign for and return each tool to its rack**; **clean and dry tools before returning them**; **oil the metal parts**; **report damaged tools at once**; **use each tool only for its proper work**; **keep the store dry and locked**.
  - *Marking note: A rule with no action, such as 'tools are important', earns no mark.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **Two tools named with a correct use for each** (1 mark each), for example **hoe - to dig and loosen the soil**; **rake - to level the bed**.
  - *Marking note: A tool named without its use earns no mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Well-kept tools last longer, so money is not spent replacing them each season** (1 mark).
- **Work is done faster and with fewer accidents, so less is spent on hired labour and on treatment** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A general statement about saving money, with no link to the tools, earns no mark.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- This sub-strand is best taught with the tools in the pupils' hands. A lesson in the tool store - naming the tools, checking the handles, filing an edge and oiling a blade - will teach more than any number of notes, and it is exactly what the exemplars ask for.
- Candidates lose marks by writing vague rules. 'Be careful' and 'handle with care' earn nothing; 'carry the cutlass with the blade pointing downwards and away from the body' earns the mark. Train pupils to write an action.
- Insist that a maintenance practice be matched to the right tool. Question 4(a) is marked tool by tool, and 'sharpen it' written against the wheelbarrow earns no mark.
- Rust is the thread running through the whole sub-strand: soil plus moisture plus bare metal. Once pupils can explain that, Questions 2(a), 2(b) and 4(c) follow naturally.
- A community farm visit is the heart of indicator B7/JHS1.4.5.1.2. Prepare the pupils with a simple observation sheet before they go - tools used, how handled, how maintained, where stored - and the report in Question 5 will be within every candidate's reach.
- Safety first: pupils must never be allowed to sharpen or swing a cutlass without close supervision, and boots and gloves should be worn for every practical lesson on the school farm.
