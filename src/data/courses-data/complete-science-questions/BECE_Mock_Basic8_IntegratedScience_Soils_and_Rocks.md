### Question 1 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- **Aim: to find out how much water each of the three soils holds back, or lets through** (1 mark).
- **The property compared is the water-retaining ability (water retention) of the soils**, which is the other side of how freely they drain (1 mark).
- Accept also: *to compare the drainage of the three soils*.
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 each. 'To test the soils' is too vague. Accept either retention or drainage for the second mark, since each implies the other.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Soil A: 100 − 42 = 58 cm³** (1 mark).
- **Soil B: 100 − 25 = 75 cm³** (1 mark).
- **Soil C: 100 − 8 = 92 cm³** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks, 1 each. The working 100 minus the volume collected must be seen or implied. Insist on cm³ at least once; do not penalise the missing unit three times over.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **A is the sandy soil**: it let the most water through (42 cm³), because its particles are large and the spaces between them are wide, so water drains freely (1 mark).
- **C is the clay soil**: it let only 8 cm³ through and held back 92 cm³, because its particles are very fine and tightly packed, so the water is held in the tiny spaces (1 mark).
- It follows that **B is the loamy soil**, which lies between the two.
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 for each soil correctly identified WITH a reason drawn from the particle size or the spaces. A bare letter with no reason earns nothing.*

**(d)** [3 marks]

- **The same mass (or volume) of soil in each funnel.**
- **The same volume of water poured into each funnel** - here 100 cm³.
- **The same funnels, and the same filter paper or cotton wool** at the neck of each.
- Also accept: **all three soils dried in the same way before the test**; **the water poured at the same rate**; **the same time allowed for the water to drain before reading**; **the soils packed to the same tightness**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE controlled variables (3 marks). The answer must be something that is KEPT THE SAME; naming the soil type, which is the thing being changed, earns nothing.*

### Question 2 (12 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **Texture** - how coarse or fine the soil feels, which depends on the size of its particles.
- **Colour** - dark brown or black soils are usually rich in humus, while pale or red soils are usually poorer in it.
- **Water-retaining ability and drainage** - how much water the soil holds and how fast the rest passes through.
- Also accept: **air content (porosity)**; **structure, that is the way the particles cling together into crumbs**; **the amount of humus**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE physical properties (3 marks). The fertility or the pH of a soil is not a physical property and earns nothing.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- **Size of the particles** - sandy: **large (coarse)**; clay: **very small (fine)** (1 mark for the row).
- **Drainage of water** - sandy: **very fast, free drainage**; clay: **very slow, often waterlogged** (1 mark).
- **Amount of water retained** - sandy: **little**; clay: **a great deal** (1 mark).
- **Ease of working with a hoe** - sandy: **light and easy to work**; clay: **heavy and hard to work - sticky when wet, hard and cracked when dry** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 4 marks, 1 for each complete row. Both cells of a row must be correct for the mark; a row with one cell right and one wrong scores nothing.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **It holds enough water for the crop but allows the surplus to drain**, so the plant is neither thirsty nor waterlogged.
- **It keeps air in its spaces**, so the roots and the soil organisms can respire.
- **It is usually rich in humus and plant nutrients**, and it holds those nutrients instead of letting them wash away.
- Also accept: **it is easy to work and the roots spread through it freely**; **it does not cake or crack like clay, nor dry out like sand**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE reasons (3 marks). 'It is the best soil' repeats the question and earns nothing.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **The light, gritty (sandy) field**: dig in **organic manure, compost, poultry droppings or green manure**, and mulch the surface, so that the soil holds more water and more nutrients (1 mark).
- **The heavy, sticky (clay) field**: work in **sand and well-rotted manure or compost** to open the soil, and **make ridges, mounds or drains** so that the surplus water can run off (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 each. The treatment must match the field; a candidate who adds sand to the sandy field earns nothing for that mark.*

- **W - the topsoil** (1 mark).
- **X - the subsoil** (1 mark).
- **Y - the weathered or broken rock**, the layer of loose, angular fragments (accept 'partly weathered rock') (1 mark).
- **Z - the parent rock, or bedrock**, the solid unbroken rock (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 4 marks, 1 each. Accept 'horizon A', 'horizon B', 'horizon C' and 'horizon D' only where the matching ordinary name is also given. The whole set of layers is called the soil profile.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **W is darker, because it is rich in humus; X is paler and contains little humus.**
- **W is loose and crumbly and holds most of the plant roots, earthworms and other soil organisms**, while **X is more compact and stony, and few roots and organisms reach it**.
- Also accept: **X receives the minerals washed down from W by rain water**, and **W is the thinner of the two layers in this cut**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO differences (2 marks). A difference must compare the two layers; a statement about W alone earns nothing.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **It is the layer in which the crop is planted and in which almost all the feeding roots grow** (1 mark).
- **It holds the humus, the plant nutrients, most of the soil water and air, and the earthworms, bacteria and fungi that keep the soil alive**, so the yield of the farm depends on it (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks. An answer that says only 'it is on top' earns nothing.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Clearing and burning the vegetation and leaving the land bare**, so that the rain washes the topsoil away and the sun burns out the humus - prevent it by **keeping a cover crop, mulching, or planting trees as a windbreak** (1 mark).
- **Ploughing or making ridges straight up and down a slope**, which lets the run-off carry the soil downhill - prevent it by **ploughing and ridging along the contour, or by terracing the slope** (1 mark).
- Also accept: **sand winning, mining and road building that strip the topsoil** - require that the topsoil be set aside and replaced afterwards; **overgrazing by too many animals** - control the number of animals and move them from place to place; **continuous cropping without manure or fallow** - rotate crops and return organic matter.
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 for each activity that is correctly paired with a remedy that actually answers it. An activity with no remedy, or a remedy that does not fit, earns nothing for that item.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **Igneous rock** - formed when **molten rock material (magma or lava) cools and becomes solid** (1 mark).
- **Sedimentary rock** - formed when **layers of sand, mud, shell and other sediment are deposited, pressed and cemented together over a very long time** (1 mark).
- **Metamorphic rock** - formed when **an existing rock is changed by great heat and pressure inside the earth**, without melting completely (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks, 1 for each type correctly named AND matched with its formation. A name alone, with no formation, earns nothing.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Igneous: granite** (accept basalt, pumice, obsidian) (1 mark).
- **Sedimentary: limestone** (accept sandstone, shale, mudstone, conglomerate) (1 mark).
- **Metamorphic: marble** (accept slate, quartzite, schist, gneiss) (1 mark).
- Note for the teacher: granite is widely quarried in Ghana for building stone and chippings, and limestone is quarried at Nauli and Buipe for cement.
  - *Marking note: 3 marks, 1 each. The example must be matched to the right type; a correct rock named under the wrong heading earns nothing.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Colour**, and whether the colour is even or mixed.
- **Hardness** - whether it can be scratched with a nail or scratches glass; and whether it crumbles in the hand.
- **Texture** - the size of the grains and whether the surface feels rough or smooth, glassy or dull.
- **Whether it shows layers, bands or separate crystals**, which helps to place it as sedimentary, metamorphic or igneous.
- Also accept: **how heavy it feels for its size**; **where and when it was collected**; **a sketch or photograph**.
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: award 1 mark for any TWO correct features and 2 marks for FOUR. The name of the rock is not a feature to be observed; it is the conclusion.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **The mineral part of every soil comes from rock that has been broken down**: weathering reduces the rock beneath to smaller and smaller pieces, which mix with the humus from decayed plants and animals to make soil (1 mark).
- **The kind of rock therefore decides the kind of soil formed** - its particles, its colour and the minerals it contains. This is why the rock under a soil is called its parent material (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks. The first mark is for the breaking down of rock into the soil particles; the second for the link between the type of rock and the type of soil.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- **Weathering is the breaking down of rock into smaller and smaller pieces where the rock lies**, by heat and cold, water, chemicals or living things (1 mark).
- **In weathering the pieces are not carried away; in erosion the broken material or the soil IS carried away** by running water, wind, ice or gravity (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks. The distinction is the one examiners look for: weathering breaks down in place, erosion transports.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- **Heating and cooling**: the sun heats the rock by day and it cools at night, so the rock expands and contracts again and again until it cracks and pieces flake off the surface.
- **The action of water**: rain water seeps into the cracks and, being slightly acidic, slowly dissolves some of the minerals, weakening the rock; running water also wears the surface away.
- **The roots of the tree and other plants**: as a root grows in a crack it widens it and forces the pieces of rock apart; lichens and mosses also attack the surface chemically.
- **Wind-blown sand and grit** wear and scratch the exposed surface; **burrowing animals, and people quarrying or farming**, break the rock further.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR ways (4 marks), provided each is described and not merely named. The candidate need not use the words physical, chemical and biological, but give credit where they are used correctly.*

**(c)** [4 marks]

- **Stage 1** - the **solid rock is attacked by heat, cold, water and plant roots and begins to crack** (1 mark).
- **Stage 2** - the **cracks widen and the rock breaks into boulders, stones and gravel** (1 mark).
- **Stage 3** - **continued weathering grinds these pieces into fine particles - sand, silt and clay** (1 mark).
- **Stage 4** - **lichens, mosses and small plants grow in the particles, die and decay; their remains and those of animals add humus**, and the mixture of mineral particles, humus, water and air forms a soil deep enough for crops (1 mark).
- Teachers should note that this takes **hundreds of years**, which is why topsoil once lost cannot quickly be replaced.
  - *Marking note: 4 marks, 1 for each stage, and the stages must be in the correct order. A candidate who gives all four points out of order earns at most 3 marks. The humus stage is essential for the fourth mark.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- **The soil water round the root hair is a very weak solution, while the cell sap inside the root hair is a stronger, more concentrated solution** (1 mark).
- **Water therefore passes through the thin wall and membrane of the root hair into the cell by osmosis**, from the weaker to the stronger solution; the many long, thin root hairs give a very large surface for this to happen (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks. The word osmosis alone earns 1 mark; the second mark needs the difference in concentration. Dissolved mineral salts are carried in with the water, but they also enter by other means - do not penalise a candidate who says only that they come in with the water.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **(i) Too dry**: there is too little water in the soil for the roots to replace what the leaves are losing, so the cells lose their firmness and the plant droops (1 mark).
- **(ii) Waterlogged**: the water fills the air spaces in the soil, so **the roots cannot get the oxygen they need to respire**; they begin to rot and can no longer take in water, so the plant wilts even though it is standing in water (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 each. Part (ii) is the discriminating one; the mark requires the lack of oxygen at the roots.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Set-up** (1 mark): take three identical tins or pots with drainage holes, label them sandy, loamy and clay, fill each with the same volume of its soil, and plant the same number of seeds of the same crop - maize or beans - at the same depth in each.
- **Fair test and measurement** (1 mark): give each pot the same volume of water at the same times, keep all three in the same place with the same sunlight, and then **measure the height of the seedlings and count the leaves every three days for two or three weeks**, recording the results in a table before comparing them.
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 for a workable set-up, 1 for controlling the other conditions AND taking measurements. A candidate who merely says 'plant in three soils and see' earns the first mark only.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Mulch the surface** with grass, straw, leaves or crop residues, so that less water evaporates from the soil.
- **Dig in compost or organic manure**, which helps the soil to hold water like a sponge.
- Also accept: **plant a cover crop**; **plant trees as shade or as a windbreak**; **weed the field, since weeds take up the water**; **make ridges or tied ridges to trap the rain where it falls**; **irrigate from a dugout or small dam**; **stop burning the crop residues**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO measures (2 marks).*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- This paper completes the Basic 8 syllabus. It is also the only paper in the series that examines two content standards, because the syllabus places the rocks standard, B8/JHS2.5.6.1, in the same sub-strand as the soils standard.
- Question 1 is the curriculum's own experiment. If your class actually set up the three funnels this term, mark their own results and figures against this question; their volumes will differ from those in Fig. 1, and that is as it should be.
- In Question 1(d), insist on variables that are KEPT THE SAME. A large number of candidates name the soil type, which is the one thing that must differ.
- Question 2(b) is marked row by row and both cells must be right. Show the class the completed table afterwards; a clear comparison table is worth more to them than a page of prose.
- In Question 5(c) the stages must be in order, and the humus stage is the one most often forgotten. Stress that soil is mineral particles AND humus; rock dust alone is not soil.
- Question 6(b)(ii) separates the class: many candidates can say why a dry plant wilts, far fewer that a waterlogged plant wilts because its roots cannot get oxygen.
- Question 4 asks for a rock identification guide, which is exemplar 3 of the rocks indicator. If your class made one, display it in the laboratory and mark their own entries against part (c).
