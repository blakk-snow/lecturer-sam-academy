- **Waste** is **any material that is no longer wanted or useful to the person or place that produced it** (1 mark).
- **Waste management** is the **collection, sorting, treatment and safe disposal of waste**, including reducing, reusing and recycling it (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- **Solid waste** - plastic bottles, sachet-water bags, broken bottles, sweepings.
- **Liquid waste** - bath water, water from the kitchen sink, waste water from a factory.
- **Gaseous waste** - smoke from a chimney, exhaust fumes from a vehicle.
- **Medical waste** - used syringes, bandages and expired drugs (accept domestic, agricultural, industrial or electronic waste with a correct example).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR types, each with a correct example (4 marks). A type named with no example, or with a wrong example, scores nothing for that item. Candidates may use either classification - by state (solid, liquid, gaseous) or by source (domestic, industrial, agricultural, medical, electronic) - or a mixture of the two.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- To **keep the community clean and free from offensive smells**, and to **prevent diseases** such as cholera, typhoid and malaria.
- To **stop gutters from choking and causing floods**, and to **protect the soil, the streams and the air from pollution**.
- To **recover useful materials and manure** from the waste, and to keep the community attractive to visitors and to business.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE sound reasons (3 marks).*

### Question 2 (12 marks)

**(a)** [5 marks]

- **A - reduce** (prevention: avoid producing the waste in the first place) (1 mark).
- **B - reuse** (1 mark).
- **C - recycle** (1 mark).
- **D - recover** - composting, or recovering energy from the waste (1 mark).
- **E - disposal** - landfill or incineration (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each (5 marks). The order must be as shown. Accept 'refuse' or 'prevent' for A, 'composting' for D and 'dumping in a sanitary landfill' for E.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **Waste that is never produced does not have to be collected, treated or disposed of at all**, so no land, fuel or money is spent on it and it cannot pollute the environment (1 mark).
- It also **saves the raw materials and the energy** that would have been used to make the goods in the first place (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. A candidate who merely writes 'because it is at the top' scores 0.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **A (reduce):** **carry a basket to the market instead of collecting plastic bags**, or write and photocopy on both sides of the paper (1 mark).
- **B (reuse):** **wash a glass bottle and use it again to store drinking water**, or use empty tins as pots for seedlings in the school garden (1 mark).
- **C (recycle):** **collect plastic bottles and sachet bags and send them to a recycling factory** to be made into new plastic items (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each (3 marks). The example must match the level: a candidate who gives recycling as an example of reuse scores 0 for that mark.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Nothing useful is recovered** - the materials, the plant nutrients and the energy in the waste are all lost (1 mark).
- It **takes up land**, and dumps and open burning **pollute the soil, the water and the air and breed flies, rats and mosquitoes** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO sound reasons (2 marks).*

### Question 3 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Diseases such as cholera, typhoid and dysentery spread**, because flies settle on the refuse and then on food, and dirty water enters the wells and streams.
- **Mosquitoes breed in the stagnant water** held in discarded tins and containers and spread **malaria**; rats and cockroaches also multiply.
- **Gutters choke with plastics and the town floods** when it rains.
- **The air, the soil and the streams are polluted** by smoke, bad smells and the liquid that drains from the dump; **animals swallow plastics and die**, and the community looks dirty.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR correct effects (4 marks).*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Plastics do not rot**, so they **collect in the gutters and block them** (1 mark).
- When it rains the **water cannot flow away, so it overflows and floods the streets and houses** (1 mark).
- The **flood water carries rubbish and human waste into homes and wells, spreading cholera and typhoid**, and the **pools left behind breed mosquitoes that spread malaria** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks as shown - blocking, flooding and the spread of disease. A candidate who stops at the flooding scores 2 marks.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- Open burning gives off **thick smoke, soot, carbon dioxide and poisonous gases**, especially when **plastics and rubber** are burnt (1 mark).
- The smoke **pollutes the air**, so the air is dirty to breathe and visibility is poor (1 mark).
- People living nearby suffer **coughing, catarrh, sore eyes and chest diseases such as asthma and bronchitis**, and the fire may spread and destroy property (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks as shown; 1 mark each for the gases, the pollution and the effect on health.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [1 marks]

- **Taking the waste to a public container**, used by **80 households**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **36 + 24** (1 mark)
- **= 60 households** (1 mark)
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 mark for adding the two correct values and 1 mark for the answer.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **20 ÷ 200 × 100** (1 mark)
- **= 10%** (1 mark)
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 mark for the correct method and 1 mark for the answer with the per cent sign.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Burning** - it gives off **smoke and poisonous gases that pollute the air** and cause chest diseases (1 mark).
- **Dumping in a gutter or the bush** - it **chokes the gutters and causes floods**, breeds flies and mosquitoes and litters the land (1 mark). (Accept **burying** in an unprepared pit, since plastics do not rot and the drainage can pollute the groundwater.)
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 mark for each harmful method correctly named WITH its reason. A method with no reason scores nothing.*

**(e)** [3 marks]

- **Provide more public containers and have them emptied regularly**, and extend the collection service to more houses.
- **Teach households to separate their waste** and to **compost the biodegradable part** for the farms and gardens, while plastics and metals are collected for recycling.
- **Stop open burning and dumping in gutters** through education and the enforcement of the sanitation bye-laws, supported by a regular community clean-up day.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE workable recommendations (3 marks). The recommendation must follow from the survey results; a general statement such as 'the people should be serious' scores 0. This is the curriculum exemplar on presenting a report on a community survey.*

### Question 5 (9 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Find out what waste the school produces and where it comes from**, then **agree on the aim and plan the activity with the head teacher** (1 mark).
- **Provide and clearly label separate bins** for food waste, paper, plastics and metals, and **place them at the classrooms, the kitchen and the canteen** (1 mark).
- **Educate the pupils at assembly** on which waste goes into which bin, and **appoint a sanitation prefect or duty group** for each week (1 mark).
- **Empty the bins regularly - compost the food waste in the school garden and hand the plastics and metals to the recyclers** - and keep records of what is collected (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR sensible steps in a workable order (4 marks). This is the curriculum exemplar that asks pupils to carry out a waste management activity using the knowledge they have gained.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Compare the compound before and after** - count the heaps of litter or photograph the same spots each week.
- **Measure the waste**: see whether the **number of bags sent to the dump each week has fallen**, and how much **compost and how many sacks of plastics have been recovered**.
- **Check whether the bins are being used correctly** and **ask the pupils, teachers and cleaners for their views**; look also at whether the cost of waste collection has fallen.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE genuine ways of evaluating (3 marks). The way must allow a comparison or a measurement; 'the school will be clean' alone scores 0. This part carries the evaluation exemplar of indicator B8/JHS2.5.1.1.2.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Talk about the project at assembly and at the P.T.A. meeting, and put up posters and slogans** on the notice board; form a **sanitation club** and reward the cleanest class each month.
- **Invite the chief, the assembly member and the environmental health officer**, hold a **community clean-up day**, and spread the message through the information centre, the local radio station or social media.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO practical ways (2 marks).*

### Question 6 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Choose a shaded corner of the garden and clear a space or dig a shallow pit** (1 mark).
- **Collect biodegradable waste** - kitchen peelings, vegetable remains, grass, leaves and animal droppings - and **build the heap in layers**: plant waste, then droppings or old manure, then a thin layer of topsoil, until it is about a metre high (1 mark).
- **Water the heap lightly and cover it** with leaves, grass or an old sack to keep in the moisture and the heat (1 mark).
- **Turn the heap every two weeks** to let in air; after about **two to three months** the compost is **dark, crumbly and sweet-smelling** and is ready to be spread on the beds (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR correct steps in a workable order (4 marks). The layering and the turning are the two steps examiners should look for.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- Composting **turns the waste into free manure that adds nutrients and humus to the soil** and saves the money spent on fertiliser, while burning destroys it all.
- Composting **produces no smoke**, so it **does not pollute the air or harm health**, and it keeps the waste out of the gutters and off the dump.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO advantages (2 marks).*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Plastics, glass, tins and other metals** - they are **non-biodegradable, so they do not rot** and would remain in the compost and in the soil (1 mark).
- **Medical waste, batteries and other chemical waste** (accept diseased plants or meat scraps) - they are **dangerous, poison the soil, and attract rats and flies** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 mark for each kind of waste correctly named WITH a reason.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Wash and reuse the bottles and containers** to store water, kerosene or seeds, or cut them open to serve as **pots for seedlings in the school garden**.
- **Collect and sell the sachet bags and bottles to a recycling company**, where they are made into new bottles, buckets, chairs, roofing sheets or paving blocks (accept: make bags, mats and craft items from clean sachet bags).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO practical uses (2 marks).*
