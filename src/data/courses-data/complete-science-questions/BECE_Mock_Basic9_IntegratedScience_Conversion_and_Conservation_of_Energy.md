- The energy given up by the falling water **equals the electrical energy sent out plus the energy wasted as heat, sound and the motion of the water leaving** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The second mark requires the idea that the wasted energy must be included in the account.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **The water is free and renewable, so the running cost is low**, while diesel must be bought and its price keeps rising (1 mark).
- **It gives off no smoke or carbon dioxide and little noise**, so it does not pollute the air (accept: the reservoir also serves for fishing, water supply and transport) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Two distinct reasons are required.*

### Question 3 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **The law of conservation of energy** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Q = 1 000 - (600 + 50)** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Q = 350 J** (unit required) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Award the method mark for the correct subtraction even if the arithmetic fails.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) **The 600 J of kinetic energy** (1 mark).
- (ii) **The sound energy (50 J) and the heat energy (350 J)** (1 mark).
- (iii) **600/1 000 × 100 = 60%** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (ii) both wasted outputs are needed for the mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Oil or grease the bearings and moving parts to reduce friction** (1 mark).
- **Tighten and service the machine regularly, keep it clean and cool, or fit a modern motor of the right size** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Two distinct practical suggestions are required.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Energy cannot be destroyed** - the law of conservation of energy (1 mark).
- The 400 J has been **changed into heat and sound that spread into the surroundings; it is wasted, not destroyed** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who merely repeats the law without applying it to the 400 J earns 1 mark.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Light energy to electrical energy** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Electrical energy to chemical energy** (1 mark).
- (iii) **Chemical (then electrical) energy to light energy** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (iii) accept 'electrical energy to light energy'.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Chemical → heat → kinetic → electrical energy** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Much of the heat escapes in the exhaust gases and in cooling the engine** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Energy is lost to friction, sound and vibration, and as heat in the wires** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (i) accept the shorter chain 'chemical to electrical' only if heat or motion is mentioned somewhere in the answer.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **The sun is free and will not run out**, while fuels must be bought and are non-renewable (1 mark).
- **No smoke, carbon dioxide or noise is produced**, so the air stays clean (accept: it can serve places far from the grid) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Two distinct reasons are required.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **The solar home system**, because the energy from the sun is free and plentiful, there is no fuel to buy, and LED lamps need very little energy (1 mark).
- Difficulty: **the panel gives no energy at night or on cloudy days, so a battery must be bought, cared for and replaced** (accept: the first cost is high) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who chooses the generating set may earn both marks if the reason and the difficulty are sound and based on energy.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **List the appliances room by room with their power ratings and the hours they are used** (1 mark).
- **Read the meter regularly (for example at the same time each day for a week) to find the units used** (1 mark).
- **Note where energy is wasted** - lamps and fans left on in empty rooms, security lamps burning by day, old filament bulbs, faulty switches (1 mark).
- **Work out the energy and cost and write a report with a table of findings and recommendations** for the headteacher (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Marks are for the steps of the survey; the order need not be exactly as in the scheme.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Any one opportunity with its action, for example **lamps and fans left on in empty rooms - appoint a monitor to switch them off** (1 mark).
- A second, for example **security lamps burning by day - fit a timer or a light-sensitive switch** (1 mark).
- A third, for example **old filament bulbs - replace them with LED lamps** (accept: use daylight, service the air conditioners, repair faults) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Each mark needs both the opportunity and the action; a bare list of places earns no mark.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- (i) Filament lamps use **2 kW × 5 h = 10 kWh** and the LED lamps **0.2 kW × 5 h = 1 kWh** (1 mark).
- (i) Saving = **10 - 1 = 9 kWh each day** (1 mark).
- (ii) **9 × 30 × GH¢1.50 = GH¢405.00** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Allow the candidate's own daily saving to be carried forward into (ii).*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Apparatus** clearly named, for example a bicycle with a dynamo, a small lamp and connecting wires (1 mark).
- **What is done** - the wheel is turned (the pedals are worked) so that the dynamo is driven (1 mark).
- **Observation** - the lamp lights, and more brightly as the wheel turns faster (1 mark).
- **Energy conversion shown** - **kinetic (mechanical) energy → electrical energy → light energy** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Any correct demonstration of the candidate's own is accepted provided all four points are given.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **Use only low-voltage cells or a dynamo, never the mains, and keep water away from the electrical parts** (1 mark).
- **Clamp the apparatus firmly and keep fingers, hair and clothing away from turning parts** (accept: do not touch hot parts; keep visitors behind a line) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Two distinct rules are required.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Wasted energy is changed into heat and sound that spread out and cannot easily be used again** (1 mark).
- **The energy supplied must be paid for and comes largely from limited sources**, so waste costs money, uses up fuel and adds to pollution (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A simple restatement of the law of conservation of energy, without the idea of usefulness or cost, earns no mark.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- The whole sub-strand turns on one distinction, and it is the distinction candidates most often miss: conversion is a change of form, conservation is the avoidance of waste. Questions 1(d), 3(d) and 6(c) each test it from a different side, and a candidate who confuses the two words will lose marks in all three.
- Energy chains must be written in the right order. 'Chemical, light, electrical' for a torch earns nothing, however well the forms are named.
- In Question 3 the arithmetic is easy; the marks are really for applying the law of conservation of energy. Insist that candidates account for every joule that enters the motor.
- Question 5(c) is the only extended calculation. Watch for candidates who find the energy used by one lamp and forget that there are twenty, and for those who forget to subtract the energy still used by the LED lamps.
- Several Section A items use situations from everyday Ghanaian life - the clay stove, drying clothes in the sun, the biogas plant. Candidates should be encouraged to answer from what they see at home rather than from memorised lists.
- In Question 6(a) credit a demonstration of the candidate's own design. The four marks are for the apparatus, the action, the observation and the named conversion, and the last of these is the one most often left out.
- Follow-up work: let each class read the school meter at the same hour for two weeks, introduce one change in the second week, and then plot the daily units for the two weeks on the same graph. The report required by the indicator should carry that graph and the money saved.
