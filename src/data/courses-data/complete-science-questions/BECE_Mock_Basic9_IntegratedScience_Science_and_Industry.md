- (ii) **A wide, shallow pan gives a large surface area for evaporation** (1 mark); **the sun supplies heat and the wind carries away the vapour, so evaporation is faster** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'Boiling' is wrong; no boiling takes place in the pans.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Dissolve the dirty salt in water and filter to remove the sand and rubbish** (1 mark).
- **Evaporate the filtrate to obtain clean salt crystals** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Both steps are needed; one step alone earns one mark.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- (i) **An industry that uses local materials, simple tools and skills handed down in the community** (1 mark).
- (ii) Any ONE of **kente weaving, pottery, blacksmithing, basketry, shea butter extraction, gari processing, salt winning, fish smoking, adinkra printing** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Do not accept a modern factory in (ii).*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- (i) **To dissolve the alkali (potash) out of the ash** (1 mark); **the insoluble matter is left behind and strained off** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Saponification** (1 mark); **soap (and glycerol) is formed** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'lye' for the alkaline solution.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Test: **dip red litmus paper (or add a local plant indicator) into the solution** (1 mark).
- Result: **the red litmus turns blue** (accept the colour change of the local indicator) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A test without the expected result earns one mark only.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO precautions each with a reason: **wear gloves and avoid splashes** - the alkali burns the skin (1 mark); **use a long ladle and keep the face away** - the hot mixture spits (1 mark); **work in the open air** - the smoke and fumes irritate the chest and eyes (1 mark); **keep children away from the fire** - to prevent burns (1 mark). Maximum 2 marks.
  - *Marking note: No mark for a precaution without its reason.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- (i) **Removes suspended solid particles (mud, grit)** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Absorbs substances that give taste, smell and colour** (1 mark).
- (iii) **Kills bacteria and other germs** (1 mark).
- (iv) **Keeps out dust, germs and insects (and shows the bottle has not been opened)** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Do not accept 'to make the water pure' alone in (i) or (ii).*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- (i) **Fermentation** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Carbon dioxide** (1 mark); **it forms bubbles that make the dough rise and give the bread a light, spongy texture** (1 mark).
- (iii) **The yeast is most active in warmth; in the cold it works too slowly** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'the yeast respires' for the process in (i) only if fermentation is named.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **the yeast was dead, too old or forgotten**; **the water was too hot and killed the yeast**; **the place was too cold**; **no sugar was added, so the yeast had no food**; **too much salt was added**; **the dough was not left long enough** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: Two distinct reasons are required.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- Tools - weaver: **a simple wooden loom worked by hand and foot** (1 mark); factory: **power-driven machines** (1 mark).
- Quantity - weaver: **small output, a few cloths at a time** (1 mark); factory: **large output, many rolls a day** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The table must be copied; four correct entries earn the marks.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **little money or power needed to start and run**; **uses local materials and keeps local skills and culture alive**; **provides work in the village and trains apprentices**; **each cloth is unique and made to order**; **little waste and no industrial pollution** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: Two distinct advantages are required.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **an improved or partly mechanised loom to weave faster while keeping his own designs**; **factory-spun, fast-dyed yarn of even quality**; **simple testing of colour fastness and strength**; **a mobile phone and the internet for orders and sales**; **electric lighting in the shed** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: The suggestion must keep the weaving itself in the hands of the weaver; 'buy power looms and close the shed' earns no mark.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- The curriculum defines an industry as individual firms producing the same commodity. Candidates who write that an industry is 'a factory' lose the mark in Question 1(a), and this definition should be learnt in the curriculum's own words.
- Question 2 is the backbone of the paper. The three days of pressing are not merely a local habit: they both remove liquid and allow fermentation, which is what makes the gari safe and sour. Classes that have watched the process answer this well.
- In Question 3(b) the answer is evaporation followed by crystallisation. Every year candidates write 'boiling'; there is no boiling in a salt pan, and the mark is lost.
- Saponification is the one technical term the soap-making question requires. Candidates should also know that the ash supplies the alkali; answers that say the ash is used 'for colour' earn nothing.
- Question 5 shows that the same scientific ideas - filtration, absorption, disinfection and fermentation - run through modern factories and village workshops alike. This is the main message of the sub-strand.
- Question 6(c) must be marked with care. A suggestion that replaces the weaver with machines answers a different question; the mark is for technology that helps the craftsman while the craft remains his.
- Follow-up work: let groups visit or describe one industry in their own town, draw a flow chart of its stages in the style of Fig. 1, and write beside each stage the scientific process at work. Displaying the charts side by side shows the class how much science their community already uses.
