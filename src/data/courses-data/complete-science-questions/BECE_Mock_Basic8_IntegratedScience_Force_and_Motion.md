### Question 1 (9 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- **Magnetic materials (any two):** **iron, steel, nickel, cobalt** (1 mark for two).
- **Non-magnetic materials (any two):** **copper, aluminium, wood, rubber, plastic, glass** (1 mark for two).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 mark for two correct magnetic materials and 1 mark for two correct non-magnetic materials. One correct material in a pair scores nothing for that pair.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **North near north: the magnets repel** - they push each other apart, because **like poles repel** (1 mark).
- **North near south: the magnets attract** - they pull together, because **unlike poles attract** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. Accept the general rule as the explanation.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- A **permanent magnet keeps its magnetism for a long time**, while a **temporary magnet loses its magnetism as soon as the current or the magnetising force is removed**.
- A permanent magnet is usually made of **steel and is harder to magnetise**, while a temporary magnet is usually made of **soft iron and is magnetised easily** (accept: an electromagnet is a temporary magnet).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO valid differences (2 marks).*

**(d)** [3 marks]

- **Place the knitting needle flat on a table and hold it firmly**, then take a bar magnet and place **one pole at one end of the needle** (1 mark).
- **Stroke the needle from one end to the other in one direction only**, lifting the magnet well clear of the needle at the end of each stroke and returning to the same starting end; repeat **many times (about 30 to 50) with the same pole** (1 mark).
- **Test the needle** by seeing whether it can pick up pins or iron filings, or by suspending it freely to see whether it comes to rest pointing north and south (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks as shown. The second mark depends on 'in one direction only' with the same pole; a candidate who strokes to and fro scores 0 for that mark. This is the curriculum exemplar on producing magnets using a bar magnet.*

### Question 3 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- The **acceleration of a body is directly proportional to the force applied to it** and takes place **in the direction of the force** (1 mark).
- and is **inversely proportional to the mass of the body**; in symbols **F = ma** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. Award the second mark for the symbol form F = ma with the symbols explained.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **The larger the force applied, the larger the acceleration** - the two rise in the same ratio, so the **acceleration is directly proportional to the force** (1 mark).
- This is because the **mass of the trolley (2 kg) stays the same throughout**; in every row, force ÷ acceleration = 2 kg (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks. The first mark needs 'directly proportional' or a clear statement that doubling the force doubles the acceleration; the second needs the constant mass.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **F = ma, so a = F ÷ m** (1 mark)
- **a = 20 ÷ 4** (1 mark)
- **a = 5 m/s²** (1 mark)
  - *Marking note: 3 marks: 1 mark for the correct formula, 1 mark for the correct substitution and 1 mark for the answer with the unit m/s². A correct answer with no working scores 2 marks.*

**(d)** [3 marks]

- **The loaded truck has a much larger mass than the empty one** (1 mark).
- From **F = ma, a = F ÷ m**, so with the **same force the larger mass gives a smaller acceleration** (1 mark).
- So the loaded truck **picks up speed slowly**, and a **larger force - a stronger push or a more powerful engine - is needed** to start it as quickly as the empty truck (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks as shown. Accept any sound daily-life example, such as pushing a loaded truck at Kaneshie market, a loaded wheelbarrow of sand, or a trotro full of passengers.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Showing direction** - the **magnetic compass** used by travellers and surveyors.
- **Producing sound** - the **loudspeaker** of a radio, a television or a mobile phone.
- **Giving a warning** - the **electric bell or the security alarm** on a door.
- **Lifting and separating scrap iron** - the **electromagnetic crane in a scrap yard**, or the separation of iron from other waste at a recycling plant (accept: magnetic door catch of a cupboard or refrigerator; electric motor in a fan, blender or corn mill; the bicycle dynamo).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR applications (4 marks). The mark needs both the use and the device or place; a bare list of devices with no use scores a maximum of 2 marks. This is the curriculum exemplar on demonstrating applications of magnetic force.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- The crane carries a **large electromagnet**; when the **current is switched on it becomes strongly magnetised and attracts the pieces of iron and steel**, which stick to it (1 mark).
- The crane **lifts and swings the load** across to the lorry or the heap where it is wanted (1 mark).
- The **current is then switched off**, the electromagnet **loses its magnetism and the scrap drops** at the right place, without anyone touching it (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks as shown. Candidates who describe a permanent magnet lifting the scrap score a maximum of 1 mark, since the load could not be released.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Set-up:** place a **trolley carrying a steel bar on a smooth, level bench**, mark a start line and a finish line, and arrange to pull the trolley with a **strong magnet** (1 mark).
- **Method:** pull the trolley with **one magnet and time how long it takes to reach the finish line**; then repeat with **two or three magnets held together, keeping the same trolley and the same distance**, so that only the force is changed (1 mark).
- **Result:** the trolley reaches the finish line in a **shorter time when the larger magnetic force is used**, which shows that the **larger the force, the larger the acceleration** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks as shown. Insist that only the force is changed: the same trolley, the same mass and the same distance. This is the curriculum exemplar on performing an experiment to show the relationship between force and motion using magnetic force.*

### Question 5 (9 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- A **simple machine** is a device with **few or no moving parts that makes work easier by changing the size or the direction of the force applied** (1 mark).
- A **complex (compound) machine** is a machine **made up of two or more simple machines working together** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Lever** - a crowbar, a pair of scissors or the handles of a wheelbarrow.
- **Pulley** - the pulley used to draw water from a well or to lift blocks on a building site.
- **Inclined plane** - a ramp used to roll drums onto a lorry (accept: **wedge** - an axe or a knife; **screw** - a bolt or a bottle top; **wheel and axle** - a door knob or a steering wheel).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE simple machines, each with a correct example (3 marks). A name with no example scores half of nothing - award the mark only when both are given. This is the curriculum exemplar that recaps simple machines from B7/JHS1.4.4.2.1.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- A **simple machine is one device with few or no moving parts**, while a **complex machine is built from two or more simple machines working together**.
- A simple machine is usually **worked by the effort of a person and is cheap and easy to repair**, while a complex machine often **needs fuel or electricity, does much more work in a shorter time, and costs more to buy and maintain**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO valid differences (2 marks).*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Corn mill** - grinds maize into dough for banku and kenkey (accept **blender** - grinds pepper, tomatoes and onions).
- **Tractor** - ploughs and harrows the land on a farm (accept **sewing machine** - sews cloth into garments; **bicycle or motorcycle** - carries people and loads; **water pump** - lifts water for irrigation).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO complex machines, each with its correct use (2 marks). Do not award a mark for a simple machine such as a cutlass, a ramp or a single pulley.*

### Question 6 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **J - the brake lever** on the handlebar (1 mark).
- **K - the chain**, running over the toothed wheels (sprockets) (1 mark).
- **L - the pedal and crank** (1 mark).
- **M - the front wheel** (accept: wheel and tyre) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each (4 marks).*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **J - a lever**: the brake lever turns about a pivot so that a small effort of the fingers gives a large pull on the brake cable (1 mark).
- **M - a wheel and axle**: the large wheel turns on the small axle at the hub (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. The example need not be explained; the correct name of the simple machine earns the mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- It is **made up of several simple machines working together** - levers (brake levers), wheels and axles (the wheels, the pedals and cranks), the chain and toothed wheels, and screws (the nuts and bolts) (1 mark).
- These parts **all act together to change the push of the rider's legs into the turning of the wheels**, which no single simple machine could do (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks. A candidate who simply says 'it has many parts' scores 0; the simple machines must be identified for the first mark.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- The pupil **travels to school, to the farm and to the market far more quickly and with less effort**, so he or she **arrives on time and less tired**, and **saves the money that would be spent on transport fares**.
- **Loads such as water, firewood and farm produce can be carried**, and the family can **reach the clinic quickly in an emergency**; the bicycle also **uses no fuel, so it does not pollute the air**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO sound benefits (2 marks). This answers the curriculum exemplar on how the functions of a complex machine can improve the quality of life.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- Question 1(d) and Question 2 together cover the first exemplar of B8/JHS2.4.4.1.1: making a magnet with a bar magnet and making one with electricity. If your class actually carried out the two methods, mark their own records against these answers.
- In Question 1(d) the stroking must be in one direction only and with the same pole. Candidates who stroke to and fro lose that mark, because the needle would not become a magnet.
- Question 2(e) is the discriminating item of the paper: the candidate must connect the property of soft iron to the use made of it. Award 1 mark for the property alone.
- Newton's Second Law is examined at the level the indicator sets: the acceleration rises with the force and falls with the mass, with F = ma used on whole numbers. Do not penalise a candidate who writes a = F/m instead of F = ma.
- Award the method marks in Question 3(c) even where the arithmetic is wrong, but insist on the unit m/s² in the final answer.
- In Question 4(c) the control of the experiment matters: the same trolley, the same mass and the same distance, with only the magnetic force changed. Refuse the third mark to a candidate who changes the load as well as the force.
