- Any THREE of: **longer droughts or dry spells**; **heavy rain in a short time causing floods**; **rivers, streams and wells drying up**; **certain plants or animals disappearing from the area**; **crops failing or maturing at unusual times**; **stronger winds and more storms**; **hotter nights** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: The sign must be a lasting change, not a single day's weather.*

**(c)** [4 marks]

- (i) Any TWO of: **volcanic eruptions**; **changes in the sun's energy**; **changes in ocean currents**; **changes in the earth's orbit or tilt**; **methane from natural wetlands**; **wildfires started by lightning** (1 mark each).
- (ii) A correct explanation, for example **volcanic ash blocks sunlight so less heat reaches the earth** (1 mark) **and the earth cools for a period** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A human activity named in (i) earns no mark.*

### Question 2 (12 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- (i) **Radiation (heat and light) from the sun reaching the earth** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Heat from the earth escaping through the layer into space** (1 mark).
- (iii) **Heat from the earth sent back to the surface by the layer** (1 mark).
- (iv) **The layer of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (iii) the idea of the heat returning to the earth is essential.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **carbon dioxide**, **methane**, **water vapour**, **nitrous oxide** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: Oxygen and nitrogen are not greenhouse gases and earn no mark.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- (i) **All the heat radiated by the surface would escape into space** (1 mark); **none would be returned, so the earth would lose heat and be very much colder** (1 mark).
- (ii) **The temperature rises (the earth becomes warmer)** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'global warming' in (ii).*

**(d)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE activities each with the correct gas: **burning petrol or diesel - carbon dioxide** (1 mark); **deforestation, charcoal burning or bush burning - carbon dioxide** (1 mark); **cattle rearing, rotting waste in dumps or flooded rice fields - methane** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: No mark for an activity without the gas it adds.*

### Question 3 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- (i) **26.0 °C** (1 mark) and **28.0 °C** (1 mark).
- (ii) **28.0 - 26.0 = 2.0 °C** (1 mark).
- (iii) **2.0 ÷ 4 = 0.5 °C per ten years** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The unit °C is required for the final two marks.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- Trend: **the mean temperature rises steadily from 1985 to 2025** (1 mark).
- Meaning: **the district is becoming warmer - its climate is changing** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'The line goes up' alone earns the first mark only.*

**(c)** [4 marks]

- Any FOUR of: **plant and protect trees**; **stop bush burning**; **use improved stoves, gas or solar energy instead of much firewood and charcoal**; **share transport, walk, cycle or use buses**; **switch off lights and use LED lamps**; **compost waste or use a biogas digester instead of burning it** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: The action must reduce greenhouse gases, not merely tidy the town.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- (i) **An economy that improves people's well-being while reducing harm to the environment** (1 mark).
- (ii) Any ONE of **solar panel installer**, **tree nursery worker**, **recycling collector**, **biogas technician**, **organic farmer**, **ecotourism guide** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept any job whose work protects the environment.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- Solar panels: **clean electricity from sunlight, so less fuel is burnt and less carbon dioxide released** (1 mark).
- Biogas digester: **the methane from the waste is captured and burnt as fuel instead of escaping, and less firewood is needed** (1 mark).
- Trees: **they absorb carbon dioxide as they grow and store the carbon** (1 mark).
- Recycling plastics: **saves the energy and oil needed to make new plastic and keeps waste from being burnt** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Each mark needs the link to greenhouse gases or energy saved.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **(60 ÷ 200) × 100** (1 mark) **= 30%** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The percentage sign is required for the second mark.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **units of electricity bought before and after**; **firewood, charcoal or gas used by the kitchen before and after**; **number and height of surviving trees after one or two years**; **mass of plastic collected and sold each term**; **quantity of waste still burnt or dumped** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: The evidence must be something that can be measured and compared.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **M** (1 mark).
- (ii) **L** (1 mark).
- (iii) **40 000 ÷ 2 000 = 20 times** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Only the letter of the country is needed in (i) and (ii).*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **M produces more than half its electricity without burning fuel, so little carbon dioxide is released** (1 mark).
- **Its law against felling has kept forest loss lowest, so its trees go on absorbing carbon dioxide** (1 mark).
- **It acts on both the source of the gas and the removal of the gas, while L and N act on one side only** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The answer must use figures from the table.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **it still loses 40 000 hectares of forest a year, far more than one day's planting can replace**; **only 15% of its electricity is from renewable sources, so most of its power still releases greenhouse gases**; **no figures are given for how many seedlings survive, so the planting may achieve little** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: A reason must be supported by the table or by sound reasoning.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO actions each with a benefit: **Green Ghana Day tree planting** - restores forest cover and absorbs carbon dioxide (1 mark); **action against illegal mining in forests and rivers** - protects trees and water (1 mark); **solar and LED street lighting** - less electricity used (1 mark); **improved cook stoves and gas for cooking** - less firewood and charcoal burnt (1 mark); **solar power plants feeding the grid** - clean electricity (1 mark). Maximum 2 marks.
  - *Marking note: No mark for an action without its benefit.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- Example - natural: **volcanic eruption** (accept changes in the sun, ocean currents, earth's orbit) (1 mark); human: **burning of fossil fuels** (accept deforestation, bush burning, cattle rearing) (1 mark).
- Control: **natural factors cannot be controlled by people, while human factors can be reduced or stopped** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The table must be copied; both columns of the second row are needed for the third mark.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE actions each with a benefit: **plant and protect trees** - absorbs carbon dioxide and gives shade and work (1 mark); **a by-law against bush burning and illegal mining** - saves forest, farms and rivers (1 mark); **buses or cycle paths** - less fuel burnt (1 mark); **solar street lights and panels on public buildings** - less electricity bought (1 mark); **a composting or biogas site for market waste** - less methane and new jobs (1 mark). Maximum 3 marks.
  - *Marking note: No mark for an action given without its benefit.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **many actions cost little or nothing and even save money**; **green actions create jobs and income in the district**; **doing nothing costs more later through floods, failed harvests and disease**; **support is available from national programmes and funding organisations** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: A bare 'it is important' earns no mark; an argument is required.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- The sub-strand joins two ideas that candidates often separate: the science of climate change and the economics of a green economy. Every green action in this paper is tied back to a greenhouse gas, and answers that stop at 'it is good for the environment' do not earn the mark.
- Natural and human factors must be kept apart. Volcanic eruptions, changes in the sun's energy, ocean currents and the earth's orbit are natural; everything that people burn, fell or rear is human. Question 1(c) and Question 6(a) both test the distinction, and a human activity written as a natural factor earns nothing.
- In Question 2 the three arrows must be distinguished carefully: P comes in from the sun, Q escapes to space, and R is returned to the earth by the layer. The greenhouse effect itself is natural and necessary; it is the increase in the gases that is the problem, and this point is worth stressing in class.
- Graph reading in Question 3 is straightforward, but the unit is part of the answer. A rise written as '2' without °C loses the mark.
- Question 5 is the data-assessment question the second indicator calls for. Candidates must quote figures from the table; an answer about country M that mentions no number cannot earn full marks.
- Question 5(c) and Question 6(c) ask for judgement. Candidates should learn that a launch ceremony is not a result: the test of an initiative is what can be measured a year or two later - trees alive, units of electricity saved, hectares of forest still standing.
- Follow-up work: let the class keep a record of the electricity units, firewood and water used in the school for one term, then introduce two simple changes and measure again. Let them also plant a small woodlot and count the survivors after the dry season. These two records turn the whole sub-strand into evidence the pupils have gathered themselves.
