### Question 1 (12 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- P - **chemical energy** (1 mark).
- Q - **electrical energy** (1 mark).
- R - **light energy** (1 mark).
- S - **heat (thermal) energy** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'Battery energy' or 'current' earns no mark.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Energy supplied = 60 J** (1 mark).
- **Energy given out = 15 J + 45 J = 60 J** (1 mark).
- **The two totals are equal, so the energy has only been changed in form and none has been destroyed** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The sum must be shown; a bare statement of the law earns the third mark only.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

  - *Marking note: Reason mark only if R is chosen.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **In a closed system no energy enters or leaves without being counted** (1 mark).
- **So all the energy given out can be added up and shown to equal the energy supplied** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'It is covered' or 'it is in a box' alone earns no mark.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- The commonest error in this sub-strand is to say that energy is 'lost' or 'used up'. Insist on the wording of the law: energy is changed in form, never destroyed. Question 3(d) and Question 6(b) both turn on that distinction.
- Keep the two meanings of 'conservation' apart. The LAW of conservation of energy is a statement about nature; energy conservation in the home is a habit of avoiding waste. Question 3(a) asks for the first and Question 5(a) for the second, and candidates who confuse them lose marks in both.
- Teach the energy chain with arrows from the first lesson. Pupils who write 'chemical to electrical to light and heat' confidently will handle Fig. 1, the table in Question 4(a) and the diagram in Question 3(b) without difficulty.
- A torch, a solar garden lamp and a small motor from a spoilt toy are enough apparatus for the whole sub-strand. Let pupils feel the warm torch after five minutes; that single observation explains the 45 J in Fig. 1 better than any amount of talk.
- In calculations, insist on the unit. Question 1(b) and Question 3(c) each carry a mark that is lost by an answer written as '150' instead of '150 J'.
- Follow-up work: let the class carry out an energy audit of the school for one week - lights left on, fans in empty rooms, filament bulbs still in use - and present the findings with the posters and leaflets named in the curriculum.
