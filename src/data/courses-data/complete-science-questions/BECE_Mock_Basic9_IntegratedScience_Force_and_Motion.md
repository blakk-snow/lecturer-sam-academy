**(b)** [3 marks]

- (ii) Wide tyres **spread the weight over a large area**, so the pressure on the soil is small and the tractor does not sink (1 mark).
- (iii) The wide foundation **spreads the load over a greater area of soil**, reducing the pressure on it (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Each mark requires the area of contact to be mentioned.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Sucking **lowers the air pressure inside the straw** (1 mark).
- The **greater atmospheric pressure on the surface of the drink pushes the drink up the straw** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: An answer that says the straw 'pulls' or 'sucks' the drink up, with no mention of pressure, earns no mark.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Activity clearly described**, for example pressing a drawing pin between the thumb and the finger, or pressing a nail and a coin into clay with the same force (1 mark).
- **Observation given**, for example the point hurts (or sinks in) far more than the head, showing that the smaller area gives the greater pressure (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The observation mark is lost if the candidate only names an activity.*

### Question 2 (12 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- (i) **Area = 0.6 × 0.4 = 0.24 m²** (1 mark).
- (i) **Pressure = 480 ÷ 0.24 = 2 000 Pa** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Area = 0.4 × 0.2 = 0.08 m²** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Pressure = 480 ÷ 0.08 = 6 000 Pa** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The units m² and Pa are required; accept N/m².*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Position Q** (1 mark).
- (ii) The **force (weight) is the same in both positions** (1 mark).
- (ii) But in Q the **area of contact is smaller (0.08 m² against 0.24 m²)**, and pressure = force ÷ area, so the pressure is three times as great (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who says the block 'is heavier' in Q loses both explanation marks.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- (i) **The block will sink into the ground** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Lay it on its largest face (position P)** - the larger area lowers the pressure (1 mark).
- (ii) **Stand it on a wide flat board or slab** - this spreads the weight over a greater area and lowers the pressure (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Each suggestion must be accompanied by the reason in terms of area.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **4 000 Pa** (1 mark).
- Because **the area is unchanged and the force is doubled, so the pressure is doubled** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A correct long calculation (960 ÷ 0.24) also earns both marks.*

### Question 3 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Pressure = 600 ÷ 0.3** (1 mark).
- (i) **= 2 000 Pa** (unit required) (1 mark).
- (ii) **Force = 5 000 × 0.2 = 1 000 N** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Award the first mark for the correct substitution even if the division fails.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Pressure = 500 ÷ 0.05** (1 mark).
- (i) **= 10 000 Pa** (1 mark).
- (ii) **It doubles (to 20 000 Pa)**, because the area is halved while the weight is unchanged (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (ii) 'it increases' alone earns the mark only if the reason or the value is given.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- The water leaves through a **much smaller area** and so moves faster (1 mark).
- The force of the jet acting on that **small area gives a much greater pressure**, which drives the mud off (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The idea of the smaller area is essential.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Yes - letting out a little air makes the tyre flatten and touch the sand over a larger area**, so the pressure on the sand is lower (1 mark).
- **But only a little, and the tyres must be pumped up again before he returns to the road**, since very soft tyres wear quickly and are unsafe (accept: he could reduce the load) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who answers 'no' may earn the second mark for a sound safety argument, but not the first.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction** (1 mark).
- (ii) P - **the action: the balloon pushes the air backwards** (1 mark).
- (ii) Q - **the reaction: the air pushes the balloon forwards with an equal and opposite force** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept the naming the other way round if the candidate makes clear which force acts on which body.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) The **balloon pushes the air backwards and the air pushes the balloon forwards with an equal and opposite force** (1 mark).
- (i) The forward force **acts on the balloon**, and friction on the string is small, so the balloon moves forward (1 mark).
- (ii) **It would move faster** (and empty sooner), since the forward force is greater (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (i) the second mark depends on the candidate noting that the two forces act on different bodies.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- One correct example with both forces named, for example **a swimmer pushes the water back; the water pushes the swimmer forward** (1 mark).
- A second correct example with both forces named, for example **a gun pushes the bullet forward; the bullet pushes the gun back (recoil)** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: An example named without its pair of forces earns no mark.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- Forces **cancel only when they act on the same body** (1 mark).
- Here **the action acts on the air and the reaction on the balloon**, so the balloon has an unbalanced forward force and moves (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The idea of two different bodies is essential for the first mark.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **A body stays at rest or moves with uniform velocity in a straight line unless an external force acts on it** (1 mark).
- (ii) **The rate of change of momentum is proportional to the applied force and takes place in the direction of the force** (accept: the acceleration produced is proportional to the force and inversely proportional to the mass) (1 mark).
- (iii) **f = ma** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept the second law stated in the acceleration form at this level.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) **a = f/m = 120/40** (1 mark).
- (i) **a = 3 m/s²** (unit required) (1 mark).
- (ii) **f = 40 × 5 = 200 N** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Award the method mark wherever f = ma is correctly rearranged.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- (i) The passengers **were moving forward and, by their inertia, continued to move forward when the vehicle stopped** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Wearing a seat belt** (accept head rests, air bags, securing loads, driving at a moderate speed) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The word inertia is not required if the idea is clear.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- (i) **Momentum = mass × velocity** (1 mark).
- (ii) The loaded vehicle has **greater mass and so greater momentum, needing a larger force (or a longer time and distance) to stop** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'It is heavier' alone, without momentum or force, earns no mark in (ii).*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Apparatus** named, for example two spring balances hooked together (or two trolleys and two pupils) (1 mark).
- **Method** - the two pupils pull in opposite directions and both balances are read at the same moment (1 mark).
- **Observation** - the two readings are always equal, however hard they pull (the trolleys move apart in opposite directions) (1 mark).
- **Conclusion** - **the two forces are equal in size and opposite in direction**, which justifies Newton's third law (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Any sound activity of the candidate's own design is accepted if all four points are given.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **Check the zero of each balance and read both at the same moment at eye level** (1 mark).
- **Keep the balances in line with the pull, do not exceed their range, and repeat with different pulls** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Two distinct precautions are required.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **The prediction was wrong - both balances read the same** (1 mark).
- Because **the force of the first pupil on the second is equal and opposite to the force of the second on the first**; being stronger only decides how large that pair of forces becomes (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who says the prediction was right earns no mark, however the answer is argued.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- The published curriculum numbers the indicator on Newton's third law (B9/JHS3.4.4.1.2) under the pressure content standard, and states the third law again under B9/JHS3.4.4.2.1. The paper therefore covers pressure and all three laws of motion; teachers should not be surprised to meet both topics in one sub-strand.
- In every explanation about pressure the area of contact must be mentioned. Answers such as 'the knife has more force' are the commonest way of losing marks in Question 1(b) and Question 2(b).
- Question 2 is the heart of the paper: the same block, the same weight, two different areas. Candidates who divide by the wrong area usually have not read the dimensions from the figure, so remind classes to write down the face they are using before they calculate.
- Units are part of the answer. A pressure written as '2 000' without Pa or N/m² loses the final mark, as does an acceleration without m/s².
- In Question 4(d) and Question 6(c) the same misconception is tested from two sides: that action and reaction cancel. The answer always turns on the two forces acting on different bodies, and nothing less earns the mark.
- The second law may be stated in the momentum form or in the acceleration form. Basic 9 candidates usually give the acceleration form, and this is accepted; the relation f = ma must, however, be written correctly.
- Follow-up work: let each group press a drawing pin and then a coin into a block of clay with the same force and measure the depth of each mark; then hook two spring balances together and tabulate the pairs of readings for five different pulls. The two sets of results cover both halves of this sub-strand.
