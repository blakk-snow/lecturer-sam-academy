  - *Marking note: Two answers naming devices that work in the same way, such as a radio and an earphone, count once.*

### Question 5 (12 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **A device that makes work easier to do** (1 mark).
- (ii) Any TWO of: **inclined plane, pulley, wheel and axle, screw, wedge** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: A lever named in (ii) earns no mark, as it is excluded by the question.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- A - **first-class lever** (1 mark).
- B - **second-class lever** (1 mark).
- C - **third-class lever** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'first order', 'second order' and 'third order'.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Fulcrum: at the wheel (the axle at the front)** (1 mark).
- **Load: in the tray, between the wheel and the handles** (1 mark).
- **Effort: at the handles, where the hands lift** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Marks are for the positions, not merely for the words.*

**(d)** [3 marks]

- **The load moves a greater distance (and faster) than the effort** (1 mark).
- **The tool gives a delicate, well-controlled grip or movement** (1 mark).
- **One other example: a broom, a fishing rod, a spade or the human forearm** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A wheelbarrow or a see-saw named as the example earns no mark.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- **Work input: the work done by the effort on the machine (the energy supplied to it)** (1 mark).
- **Work output: the useful work done by the machine on the load** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The word 'useful' is expected in the second answer.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Efficiency = (work output / work input) x 100%** (1 mark).
- **= (1,800 / 2,400) x 100%** (1 mark).
- **= 75%** (1 mark, percentage sign required).
  - *Marking note: A correct answer with no working earns 2 of the 3 marks.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- (i) **600 J was used in overcoming friction and in lifting the moving parts of the machine** (1 mark); **it appears as heat (and sound), so it is wasted but not destroyed** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Oil the moving parts to reduce friction (accept: use lighter or smoother parts)** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'The machine is old' earns no mark.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- Inertia is the heart of this sub-strand. Pupils who can say 'the body tends to continue doing what it was already doing' will answer Questions 2 and 3 easily; those who have learnt the law only as a sentence to recite will not.
- Insist on BOTH halves of Newton's first law. Half the candidates stop at 'a body at rest remains at rest' and lose a mark in Question 2(a).
- Repulsion, not attraction, is the sure test for a magnet. This is the single most frequently missed point in the magnetism indicator, and Question 4(c) is set on it. A pair of bar magnets and a length of thread are all the apparatus the lesson needs.
- When classifying levers, teach pupils to find the fulcrum first, then ask which of the load and the effort lies between the fulcrum and the other. That single rule settles all three classes without memorising lists of tools.
- Third-class levers often puzzle pupils because the effort is larger than the load. Point out the gain in speed, distance and control, as Question 5(d) requires; the human forearm is the example every pupil carries about.
- In efficiency calculations, insist on the formula line, the substitution line and the percentage sign. Remind pupils that an efficiency above 100% is impossible and is a sign that the fraction has been inverted.
- Follow-up work: let groups measure the effort needed to raise a load with a single pulley and with an inclined plane, calculate the efficiency of each, then oil the pulley and repeat the measurement.
