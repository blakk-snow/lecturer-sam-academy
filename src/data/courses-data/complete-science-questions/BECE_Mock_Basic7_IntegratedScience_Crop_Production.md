  - *Marking note: 'Foliar spraying' is not accepted for R; the liquid is poured at the base, not sprayed on the leaves.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **P (broadcasting)** (1 mark).
- **The plants are many and close together, so the whole bed must be covered evenly; ringing each plant would be slow and would disturb them** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: No mark for the reason if Q is chosen.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Fertilizer against the stem would scorch (burn) the stem and roots** (1 mark).
- **At a short distance it lies over the feeding roots, which can still take up the nutrients** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'It will spoil the plant' alone earns 1 mark.*

**(d)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **use only the measured quantity**; **apply when the soil is moist, not in a dry, hot afternoon**; **do not broadcast on a windy day**; **keep the fertilizer off the leaves and stems**; **use gloves or a scoop and wash the hands afterwards**; **keep fertilizer away from food and drinking water** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'Be careful' earns no mark.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Clear a shaded space or dig a shallow pit** (1 mark).
- **Heap the materials in layers, mixing dry and fresh material, with a little soil between the layers** (1 mark).
- **Water the heap to keep it damp and cover it** (1 mark).
- **Turn the heap regularly (every one to two weeks) until it is well rotted** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The steps must be in a workable order; a bare list of materials earns no mark.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **dark brown or black in colour**; **crumbly, with the original materials no longer recognisable**; **earthy smell, not a foul smell**; **the heap has cooled down and shrunk in size** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'It looks like soil' is accepted once, for the colour or the crumbly texture, but not for both.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **it costs nothing and uses waste from the compound**; **it adds humus and improves the soil**; **it releases nutrients slowly and is not easily washed away**; **there is no risk of scorching the crop**; **it keeps the compound clean** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: Candidates who argue that NPK should also be used in small amounts should not be penalised, provided three reasons for compost are given.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Bed 3** (1 mark).
- (ii) **7 kg** (1 mark).
- (iii) **Bed 1** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 7 without the unit in (ii).*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Too much fertilizer was applied** (1 mark).
- **The excess made the soil solution too strong and scorched (burnt) the roots and leaves** (1 mark).
- **The damaged plants grew poorly, so the yield fell** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'the plants were burnt by the fertilizer' for 2 marks if the idea of excess is also stated.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **the same size and type of bed and soil**; **the same crop**; **the same number of plants**; **the same watering and weeding**; **the same length of time** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: The treatment itself is the variable being tested and must not be given as an answer.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Use poultry manure worked into the soil together with the correct measured amount of NPK** (accept: use the measured amount of NPK only) (1 mark).
- **Reason drawn from the table: Bed 3 (correct amount) gave the best yield and Bed 4 shows that extra fertilizer lowers the yield; the manure also improves the soil** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The reason must refer to the results in the table.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE, each with its reason: **store in a dry place** - *it absorbs moisture and cakes*; **keep the bag sealed and off the floor** - *to keep out moisture*; **store away from food, water and children** - *the chemicals are poisonous*; **keep it out of the sun and away from fire** - *heat spoils it and some fertilizers are a fire risk* (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: A rule given without its reason earns no mark.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **In dry soil the fertilizer cannot dissolve, so the plant cannot take it up** (1 mark).
- **The plants may be scorched, and some of the nutrient is lost in the heat** (1 mark).
- **Apply when the soil is moist - after a light rain or watering, in the cool of the morning or evening** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The better time must be stated for the third mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **read the label and dilute it with the stated volume of water**; **apply in the cool of the morning or evening**; **spread it evenly and avoid spraying into the wind**; **wash the sprayer and his hands after use**; **do not apply just before harvesting** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'Pour it on the plants' earns no mark; the idea of diluting or of measuring must appear in at least one answer.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- The division organic/inorganic is the backbone of this sub-strand. A quick class test is to ask, 'Did it once live, or was it made in a factory?' Wood ash and green manure are the two that candidates most often place wrongly.
- Question 2(b) is the curriculum's own 'table of differences' exemplar. Insist that both columns of a row compare the same property; many candidates write 'organic is from plants' against 'inorganic is quick', which compares nothing.
- The volume comparison in Questions 1(c) and 2(c) is poorly done in most classes. Weigh a wheelbarrow of manure against a 1 kg scoop of NPK in front of the pupils; the idea of concentration is then never forgotten.
- In Question 3, 'foliar spray' is refused for R because the liquid is poured at the base. Teach the three common methods by what the farmer actually does: scatter over the whole bed, place in a ring round each plant, or wet the soil or the leaves with a solution.
- Question 5 carries the most important practical lesson of the sub-strand: more fertilizer is not better. Bed 4 in the table is the one to discuss at length after the paper has been marked.
- Safety and storage (Question 6) are frequently skipped in teaching. A bag of fertilizer beside a basket of tomatoes in a kitchen is a real and common danger; make the rule concrete - fertilizer never shares a room with food.
- Follow-up work: let each group prepare one bed of the school garden, measure out the correct quantity of manure or fertilizer, apply it by the right method and keep a simple record of the growth of the crop for the rest of the term.
