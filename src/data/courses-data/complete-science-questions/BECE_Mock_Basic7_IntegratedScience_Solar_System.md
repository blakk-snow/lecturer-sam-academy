  - *Marking note: The trapping of heat must be stated; 'Venus is bigger' earns no mark.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- (i) **365 / 88** (1 mark) **= 4.1, which is about 4 times** (1 mark).
- (ii) **A year on Mercury is much shorter than an Earth year (about a quarter as long)** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Award the second mark for 4 even if the division is not shown, but the working mark is then lost.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **It is seen shining brightly just before sunrise or just after sunset** (1 mark).
- **It is not a star but a planet, which shines by reflecting the light of the Sun** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The second mark requires the idea of reflected light or of Venus being a planet.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE materials, each with a correct use, for example: **a large ball of clay for the Sun**; **smaller balls, beads or seeds for the planets**; **loops of wire or lines on cardboard for the paths**; **thread or sticks to hold the planets** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: A material named with no use, or a use with no material, earns no mark.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- Any FOUR steps in a workable order: **fix the largest ball at the centre as the Sun**; **make four planets of different sizes**; **make four oval paths of increasing size round the Sun**; **place each planet on its own path in the correct order**; **label the bodies and state that the model is not to scale** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: Steps given out of a workable order lose 1 mark overall.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- Any TWO of: **the order of the planets from the Sun**; **that each planet has its own path**; **the oval shape of the paths**; **that the Sun is at the centre** (1 mark each).
- Any ONE of: **the true sizes of the bodies**; **the true distances between them**; **the real speeds of the planets**; **that the paths are not solid rings** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'It is not real' is too vague for the third mark.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **The pupil is not correct** (1 mark).
- **The Earth rotates on its own axis once in about 24 hours** (1 mark).
- **The half facing the Sun has day and the half turned away has night, so the Sun only appears to move** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who agrees with the pupil can earn no mark in this part.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **it supplies light for seeing and for photosynthesis**; **it supplies heat and keeps the Earth warm**; **it drives the water cycle and the winds**; **its gravity holds the planets in their paths**; **it supplies energy for solar panels** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'It makes the day bright' and 'it gives light' count as one answer only.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Shine a torch (the Sun) on a globe or ball marked to show Ghana, in a darkened room** (1 mark).
- **Keep the torch still and turn the globe slowly; the lit half has day, the dark half night, and the mark passes from one to the other** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: No mark for an activity in which the torch is moved round the globe.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- The order of the inner planets - Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars - must be known cold. A short mnemonic made by the class in Twi, Ga or Dagbani is usually remembered better than one copied from a textbook.
- Rotation and revolution are interchanged by many candidates every year. Teach them together with their effects: rotation gives day and night, revolution gives the year, and insist on that pairing in every answer.
- Candidates often assume that the planet nearest the Sun must be the hottest. Question 4(b) is built on that misconception; the atmosphere of Venus, and not its distance, is the key, which matches core competency CP 5.8 in the curriculum.
- Note that the planets' paths are elliptical, not circular. Pupils should draw them with a loop of thread round two pins, which shows at once why the distance from the Sun changes.
- In Question 5 the marks are for design thinking, not for art. A candidate who states clearly what each material stands for and places the planets in the right order scores well even if the model is described in plain language.
- Insist on the words 'not drawn to scale' whenever pupils draw the solar system. The distances are so great that no true-scale drawing can be made on a sheet of paper, and saying so is part of honest scientific work.
- Follow-up work: on a clear night, ask pupils to find the bright object near the western sky just after sunset and to note how it does not twinkle. Comparing it with a twinkling star makes the star-planet difference in Question 2(d) real.
