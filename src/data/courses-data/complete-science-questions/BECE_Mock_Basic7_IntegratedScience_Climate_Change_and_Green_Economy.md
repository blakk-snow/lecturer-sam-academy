## MARKING SCHEME - SECTION B (60 marks)

*General rules: (1) Accept any other correct and relevant answer. (2) A named source of energy must be the source itself, for example 'the Sun' or 'moving water'; 'light' or 'heat' alone is not a source. (3) Where an explanation is asked for, a bare statement of fact without the reason earns half the marks available for that point, rounded down. (4) Where a candidate gives more answers than are asked for, mark only the first ones up to the number required. (5) Do not deduct marks for spelling unless the meaning is changed. (6) British spellings are used throughout, but American spellings are not penalised.)*

  - *Marking note: 'It saves money' is excluded by the question.*

- **A clear slogan of not more than eight words calling for energy to be saved** (1 mark).
- **The message explained: everyone must switch off what is not in use, and saving energy protects the environment** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A slogan longer than eight words loses the first mark.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **Sunlight passes through the clear cover and is absorbed by the dark inside surface, heating the air** (1 mark).
- **The hot air takes up moisture from the fruit on the trays** (1 mark).
- **The moist air escapes through vents while fresh air is drawn in, and the fruit is kept free of rain, dust and flies** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A mere statement that 'the Sun dries it' earns 1 mark.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE materials, each with its correct use, for example: **wood or bamboo - the frame**; **clear polythene or glass - the cover**; **black paint or a black metal sheet - the absorbing surface**; **wire mesh or woven cane - the trays** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: A material named without its use earns no mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Dry equal masses of the same fruit, one in the dryer and one in the open, at the same time** (1 mark).
- **Weigh both daily, or record the drying time, and compare the loss of water and the cleanliness** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A test with no comparison earns 1 mark at most.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **less fruit wasted, so more food and income all year**; **no fuel or electricity burnt, so trees are saved and less carbon dioxide released**; **cleaner, safer dried food**; **new jobs in making dryers and selling dried fruit** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'It is a good project' earns no mark.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- **Work that earns a living while protecting or restoring the environment** (1 mark).
- **One sound example, such as raising tree seedlings, installing solar panels or recycling waste** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'Any job done outside' earns no mark.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- Any FOUR of: **they absorb carbon dioxide and store carbon**; **they release oxygen**; **their roots hold the soil and reduce erosion and flooding**; **they cool the air and help to bring rain**; **they act as windbreaks and hold back the desert**; **they provide habitats, fruit and timber** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'Trees are beautiful' earns no mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **plant at the start of the rains and choose suitable species**; **water the seedlings during dry spells**; **fence or guard them against animals and bush fires**; **weed round them and replace those that die**; **make a named person or class responsible for each tree** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'Plant more trees' does not answer the question.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- Teach the greenhouse effect as a mechanism, not as a slogan. Pupils must be able to say that the gases let the Sun's radiation in and hold the Earth's heat back. Candidates who merely write 'the Earth is becoming hot' cannot earn the explanation marks in Question 2(c).
- Do not let pupils confuse the greenhouse effect with the hole in the ozone layer. They are different problems with different gases, and the confusion costs marks every year.
- Note that some greenhouse effect is natural and necessary; without it the Earth would be far too cold. It is the increase caused by human activity that is the problem, and able pupils should be taught to say so.
- Firewood and charcoal need careful handling. They are renewable in principle, because trees grow again, but not in practice while the cutting outruns the planting. Question 1(b) accepts either answer when the reason is sound.
- Question 5 is a design task and should be taught with the materials in hand. A class that has actually built a solar dryer from a wooden box, a polythene sheet and black paint will answer it in a few minutes.
- Insist on the comparison in Question 5(c). An investigation without a control - the fruit dried in the open - proves nothing, and this is the point of the mark scheme.
- Follow-up work: let the class audit the school's own electricity use for one week, count the bulbs and appliances left on, and present the findings to the head teacher as in Question 4.
