### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- **Mixed farming** (1 mark).
- **Reason: she keeps animals and grows crops on the same holding at the same time** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'Mixed cropping' earns no mark.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- Fowls to crops - any TWO of: **droppings used as manure**; **they eat insect pests**; **they scratch out weed seeds** (1 mark each).
- Crops to fowls - any TWO of: **maize grain and bran as feed**; **green leaves and crop residues as feed**; **stalks used as litter or for the house** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: The same point written twice in different words counts once.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **compost or allow the droppings to rot before use**; **dig them into the soil and keep them off the leaves**; **do not apply shortly before harvesting leafy vegetables**; **wash hands and wash the vegetables well** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'Use gloves' is accepted as one answer.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **stop using artificial fertilizer**; **stop using chemical pesticides or weedicides and use natural methods**; **check that the poultry feed and any water running onto the plot are free of chemicals**; **keep records and obtain proper certification** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'Grow more vegetables' earns no mark.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- **Farming without artificial fertilizers and chemical pesticides or weedicides** (1 mark).
- **Using compost, manure and natural methods of pest and weed control instead** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'Farming without chemicals' alone earns 1 mark.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- Any FOUR of: **apply compost or manure**; **practise crop rotation with legumes**; **mulch the beds**; **use neem extract, ash or other natural pest control**; **hand weed and hand pick pests**; **encourage natural enemies**; **use resistant varieties** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'Do not use chemicals' is a definition, not a practice, and earns no mark here.*

**(c)** [4 marks]

- Any TWO merits: **food free from chemical residues**; **soil life and structure protected**; **no pollution of water bodies**; **less money spent on chemicals**; **higher price for the produce** (1 mark each).
- Any TWO demerits: **lower yields**; **much more labour**; **pests and diseases harder to control quickly**; **certification costs money and takes time** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: A merit written in the demerit column earns no mark.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **A named system, for example mixed cropping (accept mixed farming or crop rotation, if well defended)** (1 mark).
- Any THREE reasons that fit the farmer's situation: **the land is small, so no land can be left fallow for long**; **several foods from one plot and food spread through the year**; **insurance if one crop fails**; **a legume keeps the soil fertile without costly fertilizer**; **the work can be done by hand** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: The reasons must refer to this farmer's land, money or family; general praise of the system earns no mark.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **Land rotation needs a large area, with several plots lying fallow at a time** (1 mark).
- **On two acres the fallow would be too short to restore fertility, and the resting land would feed nobody** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'It is an old system' earns no mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **a demonstration plot in the school garden shown to the farmers**; **an open day, durbar or exhibition with posters**; **simple leaflets or posters in the local language**; **a talk on local radio** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'Tell them' is too vague for a mark.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- Mixed cropping and mixed farming are confused by candidates every year. Fix the difference in one line: mixed CROPping is crops only, grown together; mixed FARMing is crops plus animals on the same farm.
- Land rotation and crop rotation are the second common confusion. In land rotation the LAND moves and the crop may stay; in crop rotation the CROP moves and the land stays. Question 3(b) is built on exactly that distinction.
- Candidates should be able to say why a legume is in every good rotation. The nodules on the roots of groundnut or cowpea can be dug up and shown in a single lesson; pupils who have seen them never forget the point.
- The marks in Question 6 are for advice fitted to the farmer's own situation - two acres, little money, no machinery. A well-learnt list of advantages that ignores his circumstances cannot score full marks.
- Organic farming must be presented honestly. It protects the soil and gives food free of chemical residues, but it is laborious and its yields are often lower at first; pupils who can state both sides are the ones who will argue well in the curriculum's debate exemplar.
- Use the community itself as the teaching aid. Ask pupils to name the systems used on their own family farms and to group them under the five headings; this covers indicator B7/JHS1.3.4.1.2 and competency CG 5.4 at the same time.
- Follow-up work: lay out a small demonstration plot in the school garden, half under mixed cropping and half under a single crop, and let the class record and compare the two harvests over the term.
