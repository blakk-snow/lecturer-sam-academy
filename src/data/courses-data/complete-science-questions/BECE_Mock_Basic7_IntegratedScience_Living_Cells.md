- **Water the plant (and return it to the light)** (1 mark).
- **The vacuoles fill with water again and the cells become firm** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The second mark needs the vacuole or the cell, not just 'the plant drinks water'.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **The animal cell has no cell wall to resist the water entering** (1 mark).
- **So it swells and may burst instead of becoming firm, while the plant cell is held by its wall** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'the membrane is thin and flexible' for the first mark.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- The commonest error in this sub-strand is writing 'cell wall' for the boundary of an animal cell. Teach the rule plainly: every cell has a cell membrane; only the plant cell adds a cell wall outside it.
- Marks in Question 1(a) and Question 3(a) are for naming the lettered parts exactly. The nucleus is a part; 'nucleolus' is not accepted for it, and 'chlorophyll' is not accepted for 'chloroplast' - chlorophyll is the green substance inside the chloroplast.
- In drawings (Question 2(c)) candidates lose marks for label lines that stop in the middle of nowhere. Insist on a straight ruled line from the written label to the part itself, and on labels written outside the drawing.
- Question 4(a) is a comparison table. Candidates who write only 'yes' and 'no' should be accepted, but those who leave the vacuole row without the difference in size lose that mark.
- Question 5 examines the modelling exemplar. The reasons matter more than the materials: a bag chosen because it is flexible shows real understanding, while a bag chosen 'because it is there' does not.
- Question 6 links the cell to the whole plant. Candidates who explain wilting without once mentioning the vacuole have not met the indicator; this is worth practising with a wilted and a watered plant side by side.
- Follow-up work: let each pupil look at onion epidermis, a leaf peel and a cheek smear under the school microscope or a hand lens, make one large labelled drawing of each, and then build one model cell from local materials and present it to the class.
