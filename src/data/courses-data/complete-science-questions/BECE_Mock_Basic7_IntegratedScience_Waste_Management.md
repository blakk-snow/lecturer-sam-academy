### EXAMINER NOTES

- This sub-strand has only one indicator, but it is a wide one. Do not let the teaching stop at lists of types of waste: the curriculum asks pupils to 'explain the science underlying' waste management, which means decomposition, micro-organisms, respiration and pollution.
- Biodegradable does not mean 'soft'. Pupils often classify paper as non-biodegradable because it is manufactured, and classify a tin as biodegradable because it rusts. Rusting is not decomposition by micro-organisms; make the distinction explicitly.
- Reduce, reuse and recycle are frequently confused in scripts. Fix each with one memorable school example - a refillable bottle, a tin used for seedlings, sachets sent to a recycling buyer - and insist that candidates match the example to the right term.
- A compost heap is the cheapest laboratory in the school. Build one, take its temperature with a thermometer after a week, and the warmth in Fig. 2 stops being a fact to memorise and becomes evidence of respiration.
- Open burning must be treated as a wrong answer throughout. Pupils meet it every day, so they need the reason - the poisonous gases from plastics and rubber - not merely the prohibition.
- Follow-up work: carry out the clean-up project the indicator describes, weigh or count the sacks of each kind of waste collected, and let the club present the figures at assembly. The report in Question 6 then writes itself.
