  - *Marking note: One item in the wrong group loses the mark for that group only.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **remove dead material and uneaten food**; **top up or partly change the water**; **feed the fish lightly**; **keep the tank in the light but out of the hot midday Sun**; **check the plants** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'Clean it' alone is too vague.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- A clear position, for example **I agree, the tank is a model rather than a true ecosystem** (1 mark).
- **Reason: a true ecosystem is self-sustaining, while the tank is too small and needs food to be added and waste removed** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Either position may be taken; the mark is for the reason, which must mention self-sustaining or outside help.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **animals lose shelter and food and move away or die**; **the soil is eroded and loses fertility**; **the area becomes hotter and drier**; **streams dry up**; **shade-loving plants die** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'The forest is finished' is a restatement and earns no mark.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **timber, firewood, fruits, bush meat or herbs**; **clean air and oxygen**; **protection of the soil from erosion**; **water and rainfall**; **shade and a cooler climate**; **a place for study and tourism** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: Two answers about wood and firewood count as one.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **A workable activity, for example raising and planting seedlings of local trees at the start of the rains** (1 mark).
- **A way of judging success, for example counting the surviving seedlings or noting the return of grasses, insects and birds** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: An activity with no way of judging success earns 1 mark only.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- Biotic and abiotic are the two words on which this whole sub-strand turns. Drill them with a five-minute walk round the school compound in which pupils list what they see in two columns; the exercise is worth more than any definition copied from the board.
- Arboreal is the category pupils forget, because most textbooks name only land and water. Fix it with a real tree on the school compound: the ants, lizards, birds, mosses and climbing plants on it make an arboreal ecosystem that can be seen in five minutes.
- Insist on the direction of the arrows in a food chain. The arrow means 'is eaten by', so it points from the plant to the animal, never the other way round.
- Question 4 is built on interdependence, the heart of the content standard. Candidates who merely write 'pollution killed everything' cannot score full marks; the chain from plants to fish to birds must be shown.
- The tank in Question 5 should actually be set up if the school has a suitable container. Pupils who have watched a model ecosystem for a term answer questions of this kind from experience rather than from memory.
- Question 5(d) rewards honest reasoning rather than a fixed answer. Accept either position, provided the candidate argues from the idea of a self-sustaining unit.
- Follow-up work: ask pupils to choose any one local ecosystem - a pond, a lagoon, a wood lot, a school garden - and to keep a term-long notebook of the living and non-living things in it and the changes they observe. This covers core competencies CP 5.1 and DL 5.6 at the same time.
