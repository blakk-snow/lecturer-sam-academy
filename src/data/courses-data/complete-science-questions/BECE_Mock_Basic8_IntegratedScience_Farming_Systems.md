### Question 1 (9 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- A farming system is the **particular way a farmer combines crops, animals and land** on his holding (1 mark),
- using his **labour, capital and other resources to produce food and other farm products** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. The combination of the components must appear for the first mark. A list of crops alone earns no mark.*

**(b)** [6 marks]

- (i) **Mixed farming** - the growing of crops and the keeping of farm animals **together on the same holding**, for example maize with cattle (2 marks)
- (ii) **Mono-cropping** - the growing of **only one kind of crop on the same piece of land season after season**, for example maize every year (2 marks)
- (iii) **Crop rotation** - the growing of **different crops one after the other on the same piece of land in a planned order**, so that the same crop does not return to the land every season (2 marks)
  - *Marking note: 2 marks for each term (6 marks): 1 mark for the idea and 1 mark for a correct example or for the point that distinguishes it. Candidates commonly confuse mixed farming with mixed cropping - mixed cropping has no animals in it.*

**(c)** [1 marks]

- **The climate and rainfall of the area** (accept the type of soil, the size of land available, the capital and labour the farmer has, the nearby market, or the custom and experience of the community).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for any one sound reason.*

### Question 2 (12 marks)

**(a)** [1 marks]

- **Mixed farming** (crops and animals kept together on one holding)
  - *Marking note: 1 mark. Do NOT accept ‘mixed cropping’, which does not include animals.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- The **cattle manure is spread on the maize field**, adding nutrients and improving the soil.
- The **poultry droppings are used as manure on the vegetable plot** (accept: the oxen plough the fields).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO (2 marks). Both answers must be taken from the arrows in Fig. 1.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- The **maize stalks and husks are fed to the cattle**, and the **maize grain is used as poultry feed**.
- The **spoilt vegetable leaves are fed to the fowls**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO feed links shown in Fig. 1 (2 marks).*

**(d)** [1 marks]

- The **oxen plough both fields**, providing draught power that would otherwise cost money to hire.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark. The ploughing is stated beneath the diagram; manure is NOT accepted here, as the question excludes it.*

**(e)** [3 marks]

- The **farm wastes are used on the farm itself**, so he **spends less money on fertiliser and on feed** (1 mark).
- He has **several products - maize, vegetables, milk, eggs - and so a steadier income throughout the year** (1 mark).
- The **risk is spread**: if one enterprise fails, the others still bring in money; and the **land and labour are fully used** while the soil is kept fertile (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE sound advantages (3 marks). Credit answers that are clearly drawn from Fig. 1.*

**(f)** [2 marks]

- It needs **more skill, labour and capital**, and the farmer's **attention is divided** between the crops and the animals.
- **Animals may stray and damage the crops**, and **both crops and animals bring pests and diseases** that cost money to control (accept: housing, water and veterinary care are extra costs).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO (2 marks). ‘It is difficult’ on its own earns no mark; the difficulty must be explained.*

**(g)** [1 marks]

- **The waste from one part of the farm becomes a resource for another**, so the soil stays fertile, fewer bought inputs are needed and the land can go on producing year after year.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for the idea of recycling the wastes or of keeping the soil fertile for the future.*

**(a)** [1 marks]

- **Crop rotation** (accept crop rotation with a bush-fallow year)
  - *Marking note: 1 mark. Do NOT accept ‘shifting cultivation’ - the farmer stays on the same piece of land, as the centre of Fig. 2 states.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- Groundnut is a **legume**, and the **bacteria in the nodules on its roots fix nitrogen** from the air into the soil (1 mark).
- The soil is therefore **left richer in nitrogen for the crop that follows**, which grows better without bought fertiliser (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. The word legume or the mention of root nodules should appear; award the nitrogen mark for a correct description even where the terms are missing.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- The land is **left to rest under bush (fallow)** and **no crop is planted** on it (1 mark).
- This allows the **soil to recover its fertility and structure naturally**, as the plants that grow and die add organic matter to it (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- It **keeps the soil fertile**, since different crops take different nutrients and the legume restores nitrogen, so **less fertiliser is needed**.
- It **breaks the life cycle of pests, diseases and weeds** that attack one crop, and it **gives the farmer different products** from the same piece of land.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO advantages (2 marks).*

**(e)** [2 marks]

- The soil would **lose the particular nutrients that maize needs** and her **yields would fall** year after year (1 mark).
- The **pests and diseases of maize would build up in the land**, so she would have to **spend more on fertiliser and chemicals** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO (2 marks). A bare statement that ‘the soil will spoil’ earns no mark unless it is explained.*

**(f)** [1 marks]

- **Land has become scarce**: the population has grown and much land is taken for housing and other uses, so farmers must **crop their small holdings every season** to feed their families.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for the scarcity of land, population pressure, or the need to produce food or income every year.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- They provide **food for the farmer's household** and **income when the surplus is sold** (1 mark).
- They provide **raw materials for industry** (cocoa, cotton, oil palm) and **feed and bedding for the farm animals** (1 mark).
- Their **cover and their roots protect the soil** from erosion, and their residues add organic matter to it (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct uses (3 marks).*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- They provide **meat, milk and eggs** - protein food for the family and produce for sale (1 mark).
- They provide **manure that improves the fertility of the soil**, and **hides, skins, horns and feathers** for other uses (1 mark).
- They provide **draught power for ploughing and for carrying loads**, and they are a **form of savings** that can be sold when money is needed (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct uses (3 marks). Do not award two marks for two kinds of food (for example meat and milk) given as separate points.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Animal manure and crop residues return nutrients and organic matter to the soil**, so its fertility is maintained **without heavy use of bought fertiliser**.
- **Crop residues feed the animals** (and legumes restore nitrogen), so **less is bought from outside** and the farm can go on producing for many years.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO (2 marks). The answer must link the practice to the land's ability to go on producing - that is sustainability.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- He **pays cash for all the feed**, which is usually the largest cost in keeping animals, and he has **no crop income to fall back on** if prices rise or the animals fall sick (1 mark).
- The mixed farmer **feeds his animals on his own crop residues and spreads their manure on his fields**, so his **costs are lower and he earns from more than one product** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. The comparison with the mixed farmer must be made for the second mark.*

### Question 5 (9 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- In **subsistence farming** the farmer produces **mainly to feed his own household**, usually on a small piece of land with simple tools, with little left over to sell (1 mark).
- In **commercial farming** the farmer produces **on a large scale mainly for sale and for profit**, often using machinery and hired labour (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. A candidate who describes only one of the two systems scores a maximum of 1 mark.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- **Advantages:** the **fallow period restores the fertility of the soil naturally**, and **little capital, fertiliser or machinery is needed**; the pests and weeds of the old plot are left behind (2 marks).
- **Disadvantages:** it **needs a large area of land**, which is now scarce; it **destroys forest and leads to soil erosion**, especially where the bush is burnt; and **yields are low**, as only a small area can be cleared and worked by hand (2 marks).
  - *Marking note: 4 marks: 1 mark each for TWO advantages and 1 mark each for TWO disadvantages. A candidate who gives four advantages and no disadvantage scores a maximum of 2 marks.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Practise crop rotation**, including a legume in the cycle.
- **Apply compost, farmyard manure or green manure**, and **mulch or plant cover crops** to protect the soil surface.
- **Avoid burning the bush**, ridge across the slope or plant grass strips and trees to check erosion, and rest part of the land under fallow.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE sound practices (3 marks).*

### Question 6 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Agree on the purpose of the study and prepare the questions** and the recording table beforehand.
- **Obtain permission** from the teacher and from the farmers (or the chief) before visiting the farms.
- **Visit the farms in small groups and observe** the crops grown, the animals kept and how the land is used; **interview the farmers** and record their answers.
- **Sort the farms into the different farming systems, summarise the records in a table and present the findings** to the class.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR sensible steps in a workable order (4 marks). Observation or interview, and recording, must both appear for full marks.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- A **ruled table with clear column headings**, for example **Name of farmer | Crops grown | Animals kept | How the land is used (size, fallow, rotation) | Farming system** (2 marks).
- **Rows left blank for the entries**, with the table neatly drawn and the headings written in full (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks: 2 marks for headings that would really allow the systems to be compared (at least three useful headings) and 1 mark for a neat, ruled table with blank rows. A list written without a table scores a maximum of 1 mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- ‘**Which crops do you grow, and which animals do you keep on this land?**’
- ‘**Do you rest the land or change the crop each season, and what do you do with the crop residues and the droppings?**’ (accept: do you farm mainly to eat or to sell?)
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO questions that would really help to identify the farming system (2 marks). A question that can be answered without visiting the farm earns no mark.*

**(d)** [1 marks]

- **Greet the farmer politely and ask permission before entering the farm** (accept: do not pluck or damage any crop; keep away from the animals and the cutlasses; stay with the teacher; wear shoes and wash the hands afterwards).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for any one sound courtesy or safety rule. ‘Be careful’ on its own earns no mark.*
