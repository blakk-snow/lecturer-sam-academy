- Any THREE of: **stilt or prop roots that support it in the soft mud**; **breathing roots that grow up out of the mud for air**; **thick leathery leaves that cope with the salt**; **the ability to get rid of or store excess salt**; **seedlings that germinate on the parent plant** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: Credit any clear description even if the technical name is not used.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Tall growth lets the tree reach the sunlight above the crowded forest** (1 mark).
- **Broad leaves catch as much light as possible, and the plentiful water makes the water loss affordable** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'To look beautiful' earns no mark.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE items, each with its correct use, for example: **notebook and pencil - to record observations**; **camera or phone - to photograph what cannot be collected**; **hand lens - to examine small specimens**; **tape or rule - to measure**; **bags or envelopes - to carry leaf specimens**; **field guide or chart - to help name the organisms** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: An item named without its use earns no mark.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **the name of the organism (English and local name)**; **the land form and place where it was found**; **the date it was seen**; **the features noticed and the adaptation each gives**; **the name of the recorder** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'A nice drawing' is not a record.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **the valley is damper and the water collects there, while the plateau drains quickly**; **the valley soil is deeper and richer, the plateau soil shallow and stony**; **the plateau is more exposed to sun and wind**; **temperatures differ between the high and the low ground**; **farming and grazing differ in the two places** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'One is up and one is down' alone earns no mark.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **observe, draw or photograph instead of uprooting and killing**; **collect only small specimens where necessary**; **do not disturb nests, eggs or young animals**; **keep to the paths and avoid trampling**; **light no fires**; **carry all rubbish away** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'Behave well' is too vague for a mark.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- **The natural place in which a plant or animal lives** (1 mark).
- **It supplies the food, water, shelter and breeding place that the organism needs** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'Where animals sleep' is accepted only with the idea of food or shelter.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- Any FOUR of: **animals lose shelter and nesting places and move away or die**; **food plants are destroyed, so food becomes scarce and competition increases**; **the shade is lost, so the ground is hot and dry and shade-loving plants die**; **the bare soil is washed away by rain and the streams are silted**; **associations such as pollination are broken**; **some kinds of plants and animals disappear from the area** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: Two answers that both say 'animals die' count once.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **replant trees and protect the seedlings from fire and animals**; **set part of the slope aside as a protected area**; **stop charcoal burning and use improved stoves or gas**; **farm the flat land instead of the slope**; **agree a closed season for hunting** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'Report them to the police' alone earns no mark.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- Teach the land forms from the local landscape first. A class that has stood on a hill and looked down into a valley answers Question 1 from memory of the place, not from memory of a definition.
- An adaptation is a feature PLUS an advantage. Pupils who write only 'the goat has hooves' lose half the marks in Question 2(b); insist on 'which grip the rock so it does not slip'.
- The three associations are best taught from Ghanaian examples the pupils can see: bees on the mango and cashew flowers, cattle egrets following the herd, mistletoe on a shea tree, ticks on a dog and root nodules on groundnut.
- Commensalism gives the most trouble. Stress the test question: is the second partner helped, harmed, or neither? If neither, the association is commensalism.
- Distinguish parasitism from predation. The parasite lives on or in its host and feeds on it over a long period; the predator kills its prey and eats it at once.
- Question 5 should follow a real field visit. The album named in the curriculum is a practical piece of coursework, and pupils who have made one will have no difficulty with the question.
- Follow-up work: let each group adopt one land form near the school, record its plants and animals over a term, and display the albums side by side so that the class can compare the valley with the plateau or the plain.
