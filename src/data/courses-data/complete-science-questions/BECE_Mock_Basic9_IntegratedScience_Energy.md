### Question 1 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Using energy carefully so that it is not wasted**, that is, doing the same work with less energy (1 mark).
- (ii) **Switch off the lamps, fan and television when they are not in use** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Iron all the clothes at one time (ironing in bulk)** or **fit energy-efficient lamps** or **use daylight during the day** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (ii) the two ways must be different from each other.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) The iron is **heated up once instead of four times**, so less energy is used in heating it (1 mark).
- (ii) An LED **gives the same light for much less electrical energy**, while a filament bulb wastes most of its energy as heat (1 mark).
- (iii) The lid **keeps the heat and steam in the pot**, so the food cooks faster and less fuel is used (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A bare statement such as 'it saves energy' earns no mark; a reason is required.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **It reduces the electricity bill and the money spent on fuel** (accept: it makes the supply serve more people) (1 mark).
- **It makes non-renewable fuels last longer and reduces pollution and the release of carbon dioxide** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Two distinct reasons are required; two ways of saying 'saves money' earn 1 mark only.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **No / I do not agree** (1 mark).
- **Reason:** the food would warm up and spoil, and the motor must then work hard to cool the whole cabinet again, so little energy is saved; **it is better to keep the door shut, defrost it and keep it away from the cooker** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who agrees cannot gain the reason mark, because the reason must support a sound judgement.*

### Question 2 (12 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- A - **the object (the lighted candle)** (1 mark).
- B - **the pinhole / small opening** (1 mark).
- C - **the light-proof box (body of the camera)** (1 mark).
- E - **the image of the candle** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'aperture' for B and 'inverted image' for E.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) It is **real** (it is caught on a screen) (1 mark).
- (i) It is **inverted (upside down)**; accept 'smaller than the object' or 'laterally inverted' (1 mark).
- (ii) **Light travels in straight lines**, so rays from the top of the flame cross at the hole and reach the lower part of the screen, and rays from the foot reach the upper part (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'Clear' or 'bright' is not a property for the purpose of this question.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- Correct relation: **height of image / height of object = length of box / distance of the object** (1 mark).
- Substitution: **height of image = 12 × 15 / 60** (1 mark).
- **Height of image = 3 cm** (unit required) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Award the first two marks for correct method even if the arithmetic is wrong; the final mark needs the unit cm.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- (i) The picture becomes **larger** (1 mark).
- (ii) The picture becomes **brighter but blurred (not sharp)**, because overlapping patches of light reach the screen (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (i) accept 'bigger and less sharp'; the key idea is the increase in size.*

### Question 3 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- (i) **P = IV** (power = current × voltage) (1 mark).
- (ii) **I = P/V = 1 500/240 = 6.25 A** (unit required) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 6.3 A.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- (i) **0.24 kW × 5 h = 1.2 kWh** (1 mark).
- (ii) **0.1 kW × 4 h = 0.4 kWh** (1 mark).
- (iii) **1.5 kW × 1 h = 1.5 kWh** (1 mark).
- (iv) **0.15 kW × 24 h = 3.6 kWh** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who works in watt-hours and then divides by 1 000 gains full marks; the unit kWh must appear.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Energy in one day = **1.2 + 0.4 + 1.5 + 3.6 = 6.7 kWh**, so in 30 days = **201 kWh** (1 mark).
- Cost = **201 × GH¢1.50 = GH¢301.50** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Allow the candidate's own total from (b) to be carried forward; the second mark is for multiplying correctly by the tariff.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- Any one sensible change with a reason drawn from the table, for example **fit LED lamps in place of the filament lamps, because the lamps run for five hours a day** (1 mark).
- A second change with a reason, for example **keep the refrigerator door closed, defrost it and move it away from the cooker, because it runs for 24 hours and uses the most energy (3.6 kWh a day)** (accept: iron in bulk, since the iron has the highest power rating) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The reason must refer to the power rating or the hours of use shown in the table.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **The umbra (full or total shadow)** (1 mark).
- (ii) **The penumbra (partial shadow)** (1 mark).
- (iii) **A fully lit, bright part of the screen**, since light from the whole lamp reaches it (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'Dark part' alone is not accepted for (i); the name is required.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Light travels in straight lines and the ball is opaque**, so it cuts off the light from every part of the lamp (1 mark).
- (ii) The **lamp is broad, so light comes from many points on it** (1 mark).
- (ii) At Q and R the ball **blocks the light from some parts of the lamp but not from others**, so these regions receive some light (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The idea of a broad source with many points is essential in (ii).*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- The shadow becomes **larger (both the umbra and the penumbra widen)** (1 mark).
- Because the rays grazing the ball **travel a greater distance to the screen and so spread out more** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'the edges become less sharp' as part of the explanation.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **A sharp, completely dark shadow (umbra only), with no penumbra around it** (1 mark).
- Because a very small lamp is **a point source**: every point of the screen either sees the whole source or none of it (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who says the shadow disappears earns no mark.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- (i) **The cutting off of the sun's light from one heavenly body by another**, so that it passes into a shadow (1 mark).
- (ii) **The moon is between the sun and the earth** (the three in a straight line), and the moon's shadow falls on the earth (1 mark).
- (iii) **The earth is between the sun and the moon**, and the earth's shadow falls on the moon (1 mark).
- (iv) Any one correct difference, for example **a solar eclipse is seen from only a small part of the earth and lasts a few minutes, while a lunar eclipse is seen from the whole night side and lasts much longer** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (ii) and (iii) the order of the three bodies must be clearly stated.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) The **umbra of the moon's shadow covers only a small patch of the earth's surface** (1 mark).
- (i) **Only observers standing in that umbra see a total eclipse** (those in the penumbra see a partial one), and the patch moves quickly across the earth (1 mark).
- (ii) **Do not look at the sun directly; use a proper filter or view the image projected through a pinhole on to a card** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'the moon is much smaller than the earth' as part of the first mark in (i).*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- The three bodies drawn **in a straight line in the order sun, moon, earth** (1 mark).
- **Straight rays drawn from the sun grazing the edges of the moon** and continuing to the earth (1 mark).
- The **shadow on the earth shown and labelled (umbra, and penumbra around it)**, with all three bodies named (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Marks are for the physics, not for the quality of the drawing; the sizes and distances need not be to scale.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Apparatus and setting up** - a coin placed at the bottom of an opaque cup and the eye moved until the coin is just out of sight (or a straight stick stood slanting in a beaker) (1 mark).
- **What is done** - water is poured gently into the cup without moving the head or the coin (1 mark).
- **Observation** - the coin comes into view (or the stick appears bent at the water surface) (1 mark).
- **Conclusion** - **light changes its path (is refracted) when it passes from water into air**, so it does not travel in one straight line through the two media (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Any correct activity of the candidate's own is accepted, provided the method, the observation and the conclusion are all given.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- (i) **Refraction** (1 mark).
- (ii) **The speed of light changes in the new medium**, so a ray meeting the surface at an angle changes direction (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Do not accept 'reflection' in (i).*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Light from the fish **is refracted as it leaves the water, so the fish appears nearer the surface (shallower) than it really is** (1 mark).
- He should **aim below the image he sees** (accept: stand almost directly above the fish) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The idea that the apparent position is higher than the real position is essential for the first mark.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- This sub-strand is unusually wide: it joins the conservation of energy and energy calculations to three applications of light energy. The paper therefore samples all seven indicators rather than examining one of them deeply, and the weighting follows the number of indicators under each content standard.
- In Question 3 the marks are method marks. A candidate who writes energy = power × time and substitutes correctly keeps the first mark even if the arithmetic fails; the final mark is lost when the unit kWh is missing.
- Candidates commonly write that the pinhole camera gives a 'virtual' image. Insist on 'real', since the image is caught on a screen, and require the straight-line argument in Question 2(b)(ii).
- The words umbra and penumbra must be used in Question 4(a). 'Dark part' and 'light part' describe but do not name, and so earn no mark.
- In Question 5(c) credit the physics of the sketch, not the drawing: the straight line of the three bodies, the rays grazing the moon and the labelled shadow on the earth. Scale is not required and should not be rewarded or penalised.
- Question 6 rests on a practical activity. Candidates who have actually poured water over a hidden coin, or looked at a stick in a beaker, write the observation confidently; those who have only read about it usually omit the observation, which carries a mark of its own.
- Follow-up work: let each group build a pinhole camera from a shoe box and greaseproof paper, then read the meter at home for a week and work out the family's daily energy use in kWh and its cost, comparing the figure before and after the lamps are changed.
