### Question 1 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- P - **head** (1 mark).
- Q - **thorax** (1 mark).
- R - **abdomen** (1 mark).
- S - **wing** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'fore wing' for S.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- T (the **hind leg**) is used for **jumping** (and for pushing the insect into the air before flight) (1 mark).
- The **antennae** are used for **feeling and smelling** - for finding food and sensing danger (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'for walking as well as jumping' for the hind leg.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- The body is divided into **three parts - head, thorax and abdomen** (1 mark).
- It has **three pairs of legs (six legs) joined to the thorax** (1 mark).
- It has **one pair of antennae on the head** and **wings on the thorax** (1 mark, maximum 2).
  - *Marking note: Maximum 2 marks.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Biting and chewing** mouth parts (1 mark).
- With them the insect **bites off and chews the leaves and young shoots**, leaving ragged holes, so that the plants cannot make enough food and the yield falls (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Do not accept 'piercing and sucking'.*

### Question 2 (12 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- J - **eggs** (an egg pod in the soil) (1 mark).
- K - **young nymph** (a nymph without wings) (1 mark).
- L - **older nymph**, with wing buds (1 mark).
- M - **adult** (the fully winged insect) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'nymph' for both K and L only where the candidate shows the difference, for example 'young nymph' and 'late nymph with wing buds'. Do not accept 'larva' or 'pupa'.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- (i) **In a pod in moist soil**, below the surface (1 mark).
- (ii) The soil and the pod **protect the eggs from drying up and from enemies** (and keep them moist until they hatch) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'so that the young have food and cover when they hatch'.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Moulting** (casting off the outer covering) (1 mark).
- (ii) The **hard outer covering (cuticle) cannot stretch as the insect grows**, so it must be cast off and a larger one formed (1 mark).
- (iii) **L is larger than K and has wing buds**, while K has no wings at all (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'ecdysis' in (i).*

**(d)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Incomplete metamorphosis** (1 mark).
- (ii) Because there is **no pupal stage**: the young stage (nymph) **looks like the adult** and only grows larger and develops wings at each moult (1 mark).
- (iii) The **housefly**, the **mosquito** or the **butterfly** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (ii) either the absence of the pupa or the resemblance of the nymph to the adult earns the mark; both together are not required.*

- (i) Jar 1 - **complete metamorphosis** (1 mark).
- (i) Jar 2 - **incomplete metamorphosis** (1 mark).
- (ii) In Jar 1 the young was **worm-like and quite unlike the adult**, and it passed through a **still, resting stage inside a case (a pupa)** before the adult came out (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (ii) either the pupal stage or the difference between the young and the adult earns the mark.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- It **feeds on grasses, weeds and the leaves of crops** (1 mark).
- It **jumps and flies from plant to plant** in the field (1 mark).
- The female **lays her eggs in a pod in the soil** (1 mark).
- The nymph **moults several times as it grows**, and in large numbers the insects **move together in swarms** (1 mark, maximum 3).
  - *Marking note: Maximum 3 marks.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- They **eat the leaves, shoots and even the grain of crops**, so the yield falls (1 mark).
- A **swarm can clear a whole field in a short time**, causing food shortage and loss of income for the family (1 mark).
- They **destroy the grass on which cattle, sheep and goats feed**, and farmers must **spend money on insecticides and labour** to control them (1 mark, maximum 3).
  - *Marking note: Maximum 3 marks.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- They are **eaten as food rich in protein** in some communities, and are sold for income (1 mark).
- They serve as **food for birds, lizards, frogs and other animals** in the food chain (1 mark).
- Their droppings and dead bodies **return nutrients to the soil**, and they help to keep weeds down (1 mark, maximum 2).
  - *Marking note: Maximum 2 marks.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **No** - a clear position is taken (1 mark).
- **Reason:** grasshoppers are **part of the food chain** - birds, lizards and even people feed on them - so destroying them completely would **upset the balance of nature**; the aim should be to **keep their numbers low** at the time the crops are young (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who answers 'yes' can still earn the second mark only with a well-argued reason; the expected answer is 'no'.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **Cultural control** - for example **ploughing the land to expose and destroy the egg pods**, clean weeding or early planting (1 mark).
- **Biological control** - for example **protecting the birds, lizards and frogs** that feed on grasshoppers (1 mark).
- **Chemical control** - for example **spraying carefully with an approved insecticide**; or **manual control** - hand-picking the insects (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The method and its example must agree; a correct example with the wrong method name earns no mark.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Ploughing **turns up the egg pods buried in the soil** (1 mark).
- The exposed eggs are **dried by the sun or eaten by birds and ants**, so **fewer nymphs hatch** (1 mark).
- Clean weeding **removes the grasses and weeds on which the nymphs and adults feed, hide and lay their eggs**, so fewer insects survive near the crop (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who writes only 'it kills them' without linking to the eggs or the feeding plants earns at most 1 mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- They **kill useful insects as well**, such as bees and the birds' and insects' natural enemies of the pest (1 mark).
- They **pollute the soil, water and crop and may harm the farmer or the person who eats the food**; they are also **costly**, and the pests may become **resistant** to them in time (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Maximum 2 marks.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- One clear, correct piece of advice, for example **'Plough your land early; the sun will destroy the eggs hidden in the soil'** (1 mark).
- A second clear, correct piece of advice, for example **'Keep the farm free of tall weeds'** or **'Protect the birds and lizards; they eat the pests for you'** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The advice must be short, addressed to farmers and scientifically correct.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- Three stages drawn, in pencil and large enough to be seen: **egg (or egg pod), nymph and adult** (1 mark).
- **Egg** and **nymph** correctly labelled (1 mark).
- **Adult** correctly labelled (1 mark).
- **Arrows showing the correct order**, egg -> nymph -> adult and back to egg (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who inserts a pupa loses the mark for the stages. Accept a straight line of stages with an arrow returning to the egg.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- (i) **Plot B** (1 mark).
- (i) Rise = **31 - 14 = 17 grasshoppers** (1 mark; the working or the figure 17 is enough).
- (ii) Plot B **kept its weeds, which provided food, shelter and places to lay eggs**, while plot A was weeded clean and offered none of these (1 mark).
- (iii) **Keeping a farm free of weeds reduces the number of grasshoppers on it** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (i) award the second mark for 17 even where the candidate does not show the subtraction.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- This paper covers Sub-Strand 2: Life Cycle of Organisms for Basic 9, whose single content standard is the life cycle of the grasshopper and the effect of its activities on humans.
- The captions of both figures say 'an insect' and not 'a grasshopper', because Question 2(d) asks the candidate to work out the kind of metamorphosis shown. Invigilators should not name the insect for the candidates.
- Nymph and larva are the two words most often confused. Insist on the test: a nymph looks like its parent, a larva does not.
- In Question 2(a) candidates must distinguish K from L. 'Nymph' written twice without any difference earns only one of the two marks.
- Question 6(a) is the drawing exemplar of the syllabus. A candidate who draws four stages, inserting a pupa, has misunderstood the whole sub-strand and loses the first mark, even if the drawing is neat.
- Question 6(b) is a data-handling task. Candidates should be taught to quote figures from a table when explaining a trend; 'plot B had more' without the figures earns only the first mark.
- Follow-up work: let the pupils dig in a grassy corner of the school farm at the beginning of the rains to look for egg pods, and keep a few nymphs in a ventilated jar with fresh grass to watch them moult.
