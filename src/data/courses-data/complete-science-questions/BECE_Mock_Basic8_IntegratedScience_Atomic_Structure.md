- (i) Magnesium is in **Period 3** (its electrons occupy 3 shells) - 1 mark.
- (ii) Sodium is in **Group 1** (it has 1 electron in its outermost shell) - 1 mark.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each (2 marks). Accept the reason drawn from the electron configuration: 2, 8, 2 has three shells; 2, 8, 1 has one outer electron. Do NOT accept Group 3 for magnesium.*

**(c)** [5 marks]

- (i) Neutrons = 39 − 19 = **20** (1 mark)
- (ii) Electron configuration = **2, 8, 8, 1** (1 mark)
- (iii) Valence electrons = **1** (1 mark)
- (iv) The charge on the ion is **+1** (1 mark)
- (iv) Reason: the atom has only **one electron in its outermost shell**, and it loses this electron easily to form a stable ion, K⁺ (1 mark)
  - *Marking note: 5 marks: 1 mark each for (i), (ii) and (iii); in (iv) 1 mark for the charge and 1 mark for the reason. Do NOT accept −1 or +19.*

### Question 6 (13 marks)

**(a)** [10 marks]

- (i) Sodium atom: **1** valence electron; chlorine atom: **7** valence electrons (2 marks, 1 each).
- (ii) The sodium atom **loses (transfers) its single outermost electron** to the chlorine atom (1 mark); the chlorine atom **gains** this electron and now has a complete outer shell of 8 (1 mark); the **positive and negative ions formed are held together by their opposite charges** (1 mark).
- (iii) Sodium ion: **+1** (Na⁺); chloride ion: **−1** (Cl⁻) (2 marks, 1 each).
- (iv) Type of bond: **ionic (electrovalent) bond** (1 mark); formula: **NaCl** (1 mark).
- (v) Any one of: flavouring food; preserving meat and fish (salting); oral rehydration salt (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 10 marks in all, marked against the brackets shown in the question. In (ii) award the third mark only for the idea that the ions are held together by their opposite charges. In (v) do NOT accept ‘it is used for cooking’ alone without a use such as flavouring.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- The compound is **magnesium chloride** (1 mark).
- A magnesium atom (2, 8, 2) **loses 2 outermost electrons** to form **Mg²⁺**, while **each** chlorine atom gains 1 electron to form **Cl⁻** (1 mark).
- **Two** chlorine atoms are therefore needed for every magnesium atom, giving the formula **MgCl₂** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks, 1 mark each. The last mark requires the formula MgCl₂ WITH the 1 : 2 ratio explained. Do NOT accept MgCl or Mg₂Cl.*
