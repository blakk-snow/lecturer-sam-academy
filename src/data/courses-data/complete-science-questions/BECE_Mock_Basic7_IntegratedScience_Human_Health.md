- (i) **It spread to very many countries and continents at the same time** (1 mark); **and affected a very large number of people, beyond local control** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Ebola spreads by direct contact with body fluids, not chiefly by droplets in the air** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'Many people died' alone does not define a pandemic.*

### Question 5 (12 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- A - **washing the hands with soap under running water** (1 mark).
- B - **wearing a nose mask** (1 mark).
- C - **keeping a social (physical) distance** (1 mark).
- D - **vaccination or immunisation** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'Injection' is accepted for D only if vaccination is clearly meant.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- A - **removes and destroys virus particles on the hands** (1 mark).
- B - **traps the droplets breathed out and keeps out those from others** (1 mark).
- C - **droplets fall to the ground before reaching another person** (1 mark).
- D - **the body makes antibodies in advance, so the person is protected** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A repeat of the name of the measure, with no explanation, earns no mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **cover the nose and mouth when coughing or sneezing**; **avoid crowded places**; **use hand sanitiser**; **isolate or quarantine infected persons**; **avoid handshaking and touching the face**; **keep rooms well ventilated** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: A measure already shown in Fig. 2 earns no mark.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **schools close and learning is lost**; **businesses close, people lose their incomes and the economy suffers**; **hospitals are overwhelmed so other patients suffer**; **travel, trade and social gatherings are restricted** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: Two answers that both say 'people suffer' count once.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Observe the safety measures and report symptoms early** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Educate and support one another, avoid stigma and help the health workers** (1 mark).
- (iii) **Provide testing, treatment, vaccines and accurate information, and enforce regulations** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A role placed at the wrong level earns no mark.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **People hide their illness instead of reporting it, so cases are found late and spread unchecked** (1 mark).
- **The family is left without food and support and must move about to find help, spreading the disease** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'It is unkind' alone earns no mark; the effect on the spread of disease is required.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **a clear heading naming the disease**; **the main symptoms**; **how the disease is spread**; **the preventive measures, with simple pictures**; **what to do and where to report, with a contact number**; **large, simple lettering in a language the people read** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'Make it beautiful' earns no mark.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- Pupils must be able to go in both directions: from the nutrient to the disease and from the symptom to the nutrient. Question 1 goes one way and Question 2 the other, and candidates who have learnt only a list of diseases are caught by the second.
- Insist on local foods in the answers. A pupil who writes 'eat protein' has not answered a question that asks what the mother should buy; 'fish, beans and eggs' has.
- A symptom is what is seen or felt in the patient. Many candidates write 'lack of vitamin C' in a symptom column; that is the cause. Train pupils to separate cause, symptom, effect and prevention - the four words the indicator itself uses.
- Antibiotics do not cure viral diseases. This single fact, taught early, prevents a great deal of harm in the community as well as earning marks.
- For the viral diseases, teach the three levels of spread named in the exemplar - person to person, community to community, country to country - and the difference between droplet spread (COVID-19, H1N1) and body-fluid contact (Ebola). Question 4 is built on exactly that distinction.
- Stigma is a scientific problem, not merely a social one: it drives cases underground. Question 6(b) rewards pupils who can explain that.
- Follow-up work: let groups actually design and display the poster named in the curriculum, and judge them on content - symptoms, spread, prevention, where to report - rather than on decoration.
