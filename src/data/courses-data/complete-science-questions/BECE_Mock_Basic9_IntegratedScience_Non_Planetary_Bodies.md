- (ii) The **streak of light seen when such a body burns up in the Earth's atmosphere** (1 mark).

  - *Marking note: The three terms must be distinguished; two identical definitions earn one mark only.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Friction between the fast-moving body and the air** heats it until it glows (1 mark).
- (ii) They are **small and burn up completely** in the atmosphere before reaching the ground (1 mark).
- (iii) **Many meteors seen in a short time**, when the Earth passes through the **trail of dust left by a comet** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'air resistance' for friction in (i).*

**(c)** [4 marks]

- (i) It lies in a **fresh crater or hollow with soil thrown out around it** (1 mark).
- (i) It is **unusually heavy, rich in iron (a magnet may stick to it) and has a dark melted crust**, unlike the rocks of the district (1 mark).
- (ii) A **large crater is formed and buildings, farms and living things nearby are destroyed** by the shock and heat (1 mark).
- (ii) **Dust is thrown high into the air, cutting off sunlight and affecting the weather and climate** (accept an earth tremor) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (i) 'people said so' is not an observation and earns no mark.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- Asteroid - made of **rock and metal** (1 mark).
- Comet - made of **ice, frozen gases and dust** (1 mark).
- Asteroid - orbit **nearly circular**, mostly within the belt (1 mark).
- Comet - orbit a **long, narrow ellipse** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Marks are for the paired comparison; one column alone earns the marks in that column only.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- They **all move round the Sun** in closed orbits, returning to the same part of the path (1 mark).
- They are **all held in their paths by the gravitational pull of the Sun** (1 mark).
- Each **moves faster when nearer to the Sun**, and most travel round the Sun in the same direction (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Maximum 3 marks.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **No, the claim is not correct** (1 mark).
- **Reason 1:** a comet is a **natural body moving on a fixed orbit round the Sun, and its return can be predicted** years ahead, so it is not caused by, and does not cause, events on the Earth (1 mark).
- **Reason 2:** **records of many past comets show that no disaster followed them**; the belief is a superstition that observation and record keeping disprove (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who agrees with the claim cannot earn the reason marks. This part examines the competence of identifying and disproving misconceptions.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **Find out the date, the time and the part of the sky** from a reliable source such as an astronomy website or the Ghana Astronomy Centre (1 mark).
- **Choose a dark, open place away from street lights**, with permission and adult supervision, on a clear night (1 mark).
- **Record the observation**: sketch the comet and write down the date, the time, the direction and anything used, such as binoculars (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'use a sky map or phone application' for the first mark.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **Who published it** - whether the author or organisation is a recognised scientific body, and **how recent the page is** (1 mark).
- **Whether the same information is given by other reliable sources**, and whether the page offers evidence rather than advertisements or rumours (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Two distinct checks are required.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- They **carry material left from the formation of the solar system**, so they tell us how the Sun, the Earth and the planets began (1 mark).
- **Watching the bodies that come near the Earth gives warning of a possible collision** in good time (1 mark).
- The study **replaces superstition with evidence** and **interests young people in science, mathematics and careers in technology** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'comets may have brought water to the early Earth' for any one mark.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- The sub-strand has only one indicator, so the paper spreads that indicator over five topics: the members of the solar system, asteroids, comets, meteoroids and a final comparison. Teachers should note that the emphasis of the indicator is on MOVEMENT, which is why several items ask about orbits, speed and direction rather than about names alone.
- The words meteoroid, meteor and meteorite are the commonest source of lost marks. Drill them as a sequence: in space, in the air, on the ground.
- Question 3(c) tests the common misconception that a comet's tail trails behind it like smoke from a lorry. The tail always points away from the Sun, so when the comet is moving away from the Sun the tail goes in front of it.
- Fig. 1 and Fig. 2 are not drawn to scale, and candidates must not be asked to measure distances on them. In reality the asteroid belt is far wider and the bodies in it are very far apart.
- Question 5(c) examines the core competence of identifying and disproving a misconception. Treat beliefs raised by candidates with respect, but mark only the scientific reasoning.
- Question 6 reflects the digital literacy competence named in the curriculum. Where the school has no internet, the same marks can be earned for checking a library book, a newspaper or a radio announcement against a second source.
- Follow-up work: keep a sky diary for one term. Pupils record the date, time and direction of anything unusual they see at night, and the class discusses the records at the end of the term.
