  - *Marking note: An action without a reason earns 1 mark only.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- Dish 1 - **48 cm3** (1 mark).
- Dish 2 - **22 cm3** (1 mark).
- Dish 3 - **6 cm3** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept answers without the unit, but a wrong unit (for example g) loses the mark for that dish.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **The dish in the sun received more heat energy from the sun** (1 mark).
- **More heat makes water evaporate faster** (1 mark).
- **The cupboard was cool and the still, closed air soon held as much vapour as it could, so evaporation was slow** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'there was no wind in the cupboard to carry the vapour away' for the third mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **the dishes were identical, so the exposed surface was the same**; **the same volume of water was used in each dish**; **all the dishes were left for the same time (six hours)** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'They used water' is not a fair-test measure and earns no mark.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **The pupil is not correct** (1 mark).
- **The water has changed into water vapour and entered the air; it is not destroyed and will return as rain** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: No mark for the explanation if the candidate agrees with the pupil.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **Evaporation leaves salts and dirt behind, so the rain that falls is fairly pure** (1 mark).
- **Falling rain picks up dust and smoke from the air** (1 mark).
- **The roof and gutters add dust, leaves, bird droppings and germs to the water** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who writes only 'the tank may be dirty' earns 1 mark.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **boil the water before drinking**; **filter it through a clean cloth or filter**; **treat it with an approved water-treatment agent (for example chlorine tablets)**; **keep the tank covered and clean**; **clean the roof and gutters and let the first dirty water run to waste** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: Accept any safe, practical method used in Ghanaian homes.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **the water in the tank is still part of the cycle; when it is used it evaporates or soaks into the ground**; **the total amount of water on the earth does not change, it is only used again and again**; **the tank holds only a very small part of the rain that falls**; **collecting roof water reduces run-off and flooding** (1 mark each).

### EXAMINER NOTES

- The commonest confusion in this sub-strand is between evaporation and transpiration. Fix the difference by the place it happens: evaporation from any free water surface, transpiration from the leaves of a plant. Both put water vapour into the air.
- Candidates often write that rain falls 'because the cloud is heavy'. The mark in Question 1 and in Section A item 22 is for the idea that the tiny drops join together until they are too heavy to be held up in the air.
- In Question 2(a) the last mark is for the arrow that returns collection to evaporation. Insist in class that a water-cycle chart is not finished until the loop is closed; that single arrow is what makes it a repeated pattern.
- Question 3 uses the boiling-water model. Teach pupils to say what each piece of apparatus stands for - burner for sun, cold plate for the cool upper air - and also where the model falls short, which is the thinking the 'challenging' marks reward.
- Question 5 is a fair-test question. Many candidates can calculate the water lost but cannot say what was kept the same. Practise naming the controlled variables in any simple experiment.
- The 'importance' indicator is examined in Questions 2(b), 4 and 6. The five headings in the curriculum - energy, carrier of nutrients, water table, weather pattern and clean water - are worth learning as a list, but candidates should be able to explain each one in a sentence of their own.
- Follow-up work: set up the Fig. 2 model in class, then send pupils out to map the natural sources of water in their own community and to draw one labelled diagram showing how the water cycle serves the town.
