## MARKING SCHEME - SECTION B (60 marks)

*General rules: (1) Accept any other correct and relevant answer. (2) The name of a career must be the name of the worker or of the field, for example 'pharmacist' or 'pharmacy'; 'he works in a hospital' is not the name of a career. (3) Where a scientist is named, the field of work and the contribution must agree; a correct name with the wrong work earns no mark. (4) Titles such as Professor and Doctor need not be written, and spelling of the names is marked generously provided the person is clearly recognisable. (5) Where a candidate gives more answers than are asked for, mark only the first ones up to the number required. (6) Do not deduct marks for spelling unless the meaning is changed.)*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- Keep the factual load fair. The curriculum names the scientists pupils should know, and nothing beyond the field of work and the main contribution should be demanded. Dates of birth and personal details are not science.
- Pair every name with a piece of work. 'Allotey - mathematics and physics' and 'Fleming - penicillin' are what earn marks; a list of names learnt by heart, with no work attached, earns nothing in Question 2 or Question 3.
- Careers should be taught through the workplace, as in Fig. 1. Ask pupils what they have seen a laboratory technician, a nurse, an extension officer or a mason actually doing, and the answers to Question 1 come from their own experience.
- Fleming's dish is the best lesson in this sub-strand: an accident that was noticed. Use it to teach observation, curiosity and follow-up rather than as a story to be recited.
- Insist on reasons in Question 5. 'Ghana should focus on engineering' is half an answer; 'because our roads, bridges and water systems must be designed and maintained by our own people' completes it.
- Girls must be addressed directly. The curriculum names two Ghanaian women scientists; put their pictures on the classroom wall beside Einstein's and the message of Question 5(c) is made without a word.
- Follow-up work: invite a local scientist, nurse, engineer or extension officer to the class, let the pupils interview the visitor and file the record in the portfolio of Question 6.
