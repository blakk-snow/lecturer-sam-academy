- Work: it **binds (lashes) the handle firmly to the head** so that the joint does not loosen or twist in use (1 mark).
- Other method: **nailing, screwing, bolting, or fitting the handle into a socket and wedging it** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Any one sensible alternative method earns the second mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Test: **pull the rake through loose soil or over dry leaves several times** (accept any reasonable trial on the work the tool is meant to do) (1 mark).
- Result: **the teeth remain straight and firm, the joint stays tight and the leaves or soil are gathered easily** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The test must be on the work the tool is meant to do.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **use thicker, harder nails or steel rods**; **drive the teeth deeper into the head**; **set the teeth closer together**; **make the teeth shorter**; **use a harder wood for the head** (1 mark each, maximum 2 marks).
  - *Marking note: The change must act on the strength or fixing of the teeth; 'be careful when using it' earns no mark.*

**(a)** [5 marks]

- Order: **R, Q, T, S, P** (4 marks; award 2 marks if only one pair of letters is out of place).

  - *Marking note: Award the 4 marks only for the full correct order; a single transposition scores 2.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) The hot metal is **soft and can be beaten into shape** (1 mark).
- (ii) Quenching **hardens** the blade and tempering **removes the brittleness** so that it does not snap (1 mark).
- (iii) Seasoned wood **will not shrink, crack or work loose** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (ii) either hardening or tempering correctly explained earns the mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **cheap and easily available locally**; **made of good steel that hardens well**; **reduces the cost of the finished tool**; **recycles scrap and keeps the environment clean** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: Two distinct advantages are required.*

**(a)** [3 marks]

**(b)** [4 marks]

  - *Marking note: Four distinct precautions; repeated ideas score once.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- Reason: aluminium is **too soft - it bends in hard soil and cannot be hardened to keep an edge** (1 mark).
- Any TWO ways: **look for scrap steel (vehicle springs, old hoe blades, drum sheet)**; **ask a local blacksmith or technical school for material or help**; **make fewer trowels with the steel available**; **fold and rivet the aluminium over a steel strip to stiffen it** (1 mark each).

### EXAMINER NOTES

- This sub-strand is practical. The written paper can test the knowledge behind the practical work - the tools, the materials, the joints, the processes and the precautions - but it cannot replace the workshop. Classes that have actually assembled and made tools answer Questions 3, 5 and 6 far better than classes that have only read about them.
- In Question 2 the lettering runs A to E on the cutlass and in Question 3 it runs J to M on the rake, so that no letter is used twice in the paper. Candidates should be reminded to answer with the letter given and not with a letter of their own.
- The commonest error in Question 2(a) is to call the tang the 'handle' because it lies inside the handle. The broken lines in the figure are the clue: they show a part that is hidden, and the question says so.
- Reasons must be tied to a property of the material. 'Steel is good' earns nothing; 'steel is hard and keeps a sharp edge' earns the mark. Teach the pairing of material and property throughout this sub-strand.
- Question 4(a) is an ordering item. Candidates who begin with heating rather than measuring have missed the first lesson of making anything: measure and mark out before you cut or heat.
- Safety answers must be specific. 'Be careful' and 'be serious' earn no marks, while 'hold hot metal with tongs' and 'clamp the work in a vice' do.
- Follow-up work: let each group assemble three tools from the school store, draw them, label the parts, name the material of each part and show on the drawing how the parts are joined. Then let the groups make one simple tool each - a dibber, a rake or a seed drill - and keep a written record of the materials, the steps, the difficulties met and the result of the test. This record is the evidence for the third indicator.
