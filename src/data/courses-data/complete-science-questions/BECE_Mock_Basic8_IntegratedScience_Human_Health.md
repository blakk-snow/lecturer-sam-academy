### Question 1 (9 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- A **communicable disease is caused by a germ and can be passed from one person to another**, directly or through food, water, air or a vector (1 mark).
- A **non-communicable disease cannot be passed from person to person**; it comes from the person's own body, diet or way of life, for example diabetes, hypertension or sickle-cell disease (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. Accept 'infectious disease' for communicable.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- **Through the air, in droplets from coughing and sneezing** - **measles** (accept tuberculosis, influenza) (1 mark).
- **Through contaminated food and water** - **cholera** (accept typhoid fever, dysentery) (1 mark).
- **Through infected blood and body fluids, including shared blades and needles** - **hepatitis B** (accept HIV) (1 mark).
- **Through the bite of a vector** - **malaria**, carried by the female Anopheles mosquito (accept mother-to-child transmission, or direct skin contact with ringworm or scabies) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR routes, each with a correct disease (4 marks). A route with no disease, or with a disease that does not travel that way, scores nothing for that item.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Wash the hands with soap under running water** before eating and after using the toilet, and **keep the toilets, the canteen and the compound clean with safe drinking water**.
- **Cover the mouth and the nose when coughing or sneezing**, keep the classrooms well aired, and **do not share cups, blades, needles or toothbrushes**.
- **Immunise the pupils, keep a sick pupil away from the others until he or she is well, and teach the pupils about the diseases** at assembly.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE sound measures (3 marks).*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Through **contact with the blood or body fluids of an infected person**, including **shared razor blades, needles, syringes, clippers and toothbrushes**.
- Through **transfusion with unscreened infected blood**, or the use of unsterilised instruments for injections, tattooing or tribal marks.
- Through **unprotected sexual intercourse with an infected person**, and **from an infected mother to her baby during pregnancy or at birth**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct routes (3 marks). Do not award a mark for routes that do not spread hepatitis B, such as sharing a desk, shaking hands or mosquito bites.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Yellowing of the eyes and the skin (jaundice)** and **dark, cola-coloured urine**.
- **Tiredness and general weakness**, with **loss of appetite, nausea and vomiting**.
- **Fever** and **pain in the abdomen**, especially on the right side below the ribs.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct symptoms (3 marks). Note that many people carry the virus with no symptoms at all; a candidate who says so in addition is not penalised.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **On the patient:** the **liver is damaged, which may lead to cirrhosis, liver cancer and even death**; the patient becomes weak, misses school or work, and may remain a carrier for life.
- **On the family:** **money is spent on treatment and the family loses the patient's work and income**; other members of the household may be infected, and the family suffers worry and sometimes stigma.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO effects (2 marks).*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [1 marks]

- **The liver**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- The condition is **jaundice** (1 mark).
- The **damaged liver can no longer remove the yellow bile pigment from the blood**, so it **collects in the blood and stains the eyes and the skin yellow** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 mark for the name and 1 mark for the explanation. Accept 'the liver is not working properly, so the yellow colouring stays in the blood'.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Immunisation with the hepatitis B vaccine** - the surest protection.
- **Never sharing razor blades, needles, syringes, clippers or toothbrushes**, and insisting on **sterile instruments** for injections, tattooing and tribal marks.
- **Using only screened blood for transfusion**, **avoiding unprotected sexual intercourse**, and **screening pregnant women so that their babies are protected at birth**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct preventive measures (3 marks).*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- The vaccine makes the **body produce antibodies, so the person is protected before ever meeting the virus**, and it is **far cheaper and safer than treating the disease**.
- It **prevents the liver damage, cirrhosis and liver cancer** that can follow, and when **many people are vaccinated there are fewer carriers**, so the disease spreads less in the community.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO sound reasons (2 marks).*

**(e)** [2 marks]

- **Treat the classmate with kindness and respect, keep the matter confidential and continue to study, play and eat together**, since hepatitis B is not spread by ordinary contact (1 mark).
- **Encourage him or her to attend the clinic and take the treatment prescribed**, make sure **nobody shares blades, needles or toothbrushes**, and get themselves **vaccinated and tested** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. Any answer that suggests sending the classmate away, announcing the result publicly or refusing to sit with the pupil scores 0 for that point. Stigma is a learning outcome of this sub-strand.*

### Question 5 (9 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- **HIV - human immunodeficiency virus** (1 mark).
- **AIDS - acquired immune deficiency syndrome** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. Accept 'immuno-deficiency' written as one or two words.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Through **unprotected sexual intercourse with an infected person**.
- Through **transfusion with unscreened infected blood**, or by **sharing needles, syringes, razor blades and other skin-piercing instruments**.
- From an **infected mother to her child during pregnancy, at birth or through breast milk**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct routes (3 marks).*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- By **shaking hands, hugging, playing together or sharing a desk, clothes, books or a toilet**.
- By **eating food prepared or shared with an infected person**, by **coughing, sneezing or breathing the same air**, or by **mosquito and other insect bites**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct answers (2 marks). This part is included to correct the fear and stigma that surround HIV.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- The child suffers **high fever, a rash over the body, red watery eyes, a runny nose and a cough**, and becomes **weak and off food**.
- Measles may lead to **blindness, ear infection, severe diarrhoea, pneumonia, malnutrition and even death**, and the child misses school for a long time.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO effects (2 marks).*

### Question 6 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Find out how common the disease is** from the records of the health centre, and set a clear target for the plan (1 mark).
- **Organise health education** at assembly, in the churches and mosques, at the market and on the information centre or local radio, using posters, drama and the class presentation (1 mark).
- **Arrange a screening and vaccination day with the health centre** for pupils and community members, and encourage pregnant women to attend antenatal clinic (1 mark).
- **Campaign against the sharing of blades, needles, clippers and toothbrushes**, and teach the barbers and hairdressers to sterilise their tools and use a new blade for each customer (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR workable activities (4 marks). This is the curriculum exemplar that asks pupils to search for information on hepatitis and develop a plan to minimise the disease.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **The nurse or community health officer at the health centre** - to **screen, vaccinate, treat and give correct information** (1 mark).
- **The chief, the assembly member, religious leaders or the P.T.A.** - to **call the community together and support the message** (accept the barbers and hairdressers, who must change their practice) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO people or groups, each with how they would help (2 marks). A name with no role scores nothing.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Two sources: **the health centre and its nurses**, and **the Ghana Health Service, the textbook, the school library or the internet** (1 mark for any two).
- Checking: **compare the information with a second source, show it to the nurse or the teacher, and use only material from known health bodies, checking how recent it is** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 mark for naming two sources and 1 mark for a sound way of checking. This part carries the digital-literacy competency.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Compare the number of hepatitis B cases recorded at the health centre before the plan and after six months or a year**, and **count how many people were screened and vaccinated** (1 mark).
- **Check whether the barbers and hairdressers now use a new blade for each customer**, and **test what people know with a short questionnaire before and after the education** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO ways that can actually be measured or compared (2 marks). A general hope that 'people will be healthy' scores 0.*
