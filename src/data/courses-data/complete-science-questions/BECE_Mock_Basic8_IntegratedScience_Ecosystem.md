### Question 1 (9 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- An ecosystem is **all the living organisms (plants, animals and micro-organisms) in a particular place** (1 mark)
- **together with the non-living parts of their surroundings** - soil, water, air, light and temperature - all **interacting with one another** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. Award 1 mark for the living things alone; the second mark needs the non-living surroundings or the interaction.*

**(b)** [6 marks]

- (i) **Producer** - an organism, usually a **green plant, that makes its own food** using energy from the Sun by photosynthesis (2 marks)
- (ii) **Primary consumer** - an animal that **feeds directly on producers (green plants)**; it is a herbivore, for example a grasshopper or a goat (2 marks)
- (iii) **Secondary consumer** - an animal that **feeds on primary consumers**, for example a lizard that eats grasshoppers (2 marks)
  - *Marking note: 2 marks for each term (6 marks): 1 mark for the basic idea and 1 mark for a correct example or for the link with the level below it. These three terms are named directly in the curriculum exemplar.*

**(c)** [1 marks]

- **Water** (also accept air, soil, sunlight, rocks or temperature)
  - *Marking note: 1 mark. The thing named must be non-living. Do NOT accept a dead animal or a fallen leaf, which were once living.*

### Question 3 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- P is the **producer** level (the green plants) (1 mark)
- Example: **maize plant** (accept grass, cassava, algae or any named green plant) (1 mark)
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. The example must be a green plant. Do NOT accept a mushroom, which is not a producer.*

**(b)** [1 marks]

- **R**
  - *Marking note: 1 mark. P is the producers, Q the primary consumers, R the secondary consumers and S the top consumers.*

**(c)** [1 marks]

- As **heat**
  - *Marking note: 1 mark. Do NOT accept ‘light’ or ‘sound’.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- At each level the organisms **use most of the energy for respiration, movement, growth and other life activities**, and much of it **escapes to the surroundings as heat** (1 mark).
- Only the **small part that remains stored in their bodies is passed on** when they are eaten, so less and less energy is available at each higher level (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. Accept the point that parts of the organism (bones, fur, roots) are not eaten or digested.*

**(e)** [2 marks]

- **So much energy is lost at every stage** that after four or five levels **very little is left** (1 mark).
- There would **not be enough energy to feed and support another level** of consumers, so the chain ends (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. The answer must connect the loss of energy with the inability to support a further level.*

**(f)** [2 marks]

- They **trap the energy of the Sun and make food**, which every other organism in the ecosystem depends on, directly or indirectly.
- They **release oxygen** into the air for other organisms to breathe, and **provide shelter and habitats** for animals (and their roots hold the soil together).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct points (2 marks). The food-making point should appear; award the second mark for oxygen, shelter or soil protection.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **Green plants absorb the sunlight** that falls on their leaves, using the green substance **chlorophyll** (1 mark).
- They use that energy, with **carbon dioxide and water**, to make food by **photosynthesis**, and the energy is **stored in the food** they make (1 mark).
- When **animals eat the plants**, the stored energy passes into their bodies, and so into the rest of the food chain (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks, 1 mark each. The word photosynthesis should appear; award the mark for a correct description even where the term is missing.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- The Earth would become **extremely cold and dark**, and water would freeze, stopping the water cycle (1 mark).
- **Photosynthesis would stop**, so green plants would die and no new food would be made (1 mark).
- **Animals would starve** as the food chains broke down, and in time **almost all life on Earth would come to an end** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks, 1 mark each for any three sound changes. This is the curriculum exemplar ‘discuss how life on earth will be like without the sun’; accept any well-reasoned consequence.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- They are the **only organisms that can trap the Sun's energy and make their own food** (1 mark).
- **Every other organism depends on them** for food, directly or indirectly, so the chain must start with them (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- Animals **have no chlorophyll**, the green substance needed to absorb and trap sunlight (1 mark).
- They **cannot carry out photosynthesis** at all, so they must take in **ready-made food** by eating plants or other animals (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. Chlorophyll (or the inability to photosynthesise) must appear for full marks.*

### Question 5 (9 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- Interdependence means that the organisms in an ecosystem **depend on one another for food, shelter, oxygen and other needs** (1 mark).
- None of them can live entirely on its own, so **a change that affects one organism affects the others too** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- Plants provide **shelter, shade and nesting places** for animals, while animals **pollinate flowers and scatter seeds** for the plants.
- Plants **release oxygen** that animals breathe, and animals **release carbon dioxide** that plants use; **animal droppings and dead bodies enrich the soil** for plants.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct points that are NOT about feeding (2 marks). An answer about eating earns no mark here.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Decomposers **break down dead plants and animals and their waste** (1 mark),
- **returning the nutrients to the soil** so that green plants can use them again, and **clearing away dead matter** that would otherwise pile up (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. Named examples such as bacteria, fungi and mushrooms may support the answer but do not earn a mark on their own.*

**(d)** [3 marks]

- The **number of rats would increase greatly**, because the snakes that fed on them are gone (1 mark).
- The rats would **destroy much more maize**, so the farmer's **harvest would fall** (1 mark).
- **Hawks and other animals that feed on snakes would lose a source of food** and reduce in number, so the **balance of the whole ecosystem is upset** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks, 1 mark each for any three sound effects. Credit any answer that traces a correct chain of consequences through the food web.*

### Question 6 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Mark out a small area** of the grass (for example one metre square) with pegs and string, so that the study area is clear.
- **Search the area carefully** - in the grass, under stones and leaves - and **identify and list the organisms** found there.
- **Observe quietly over several days** to see what each organism feeds on, looking for clues such as bitten leaves, droppings and remains of prey.
- **Record the findings in a table** (organism, what it feeds on, what feeds on it), then **use the records to draw food chains and a food web**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR sensible steps in a workable order (4 marks). Award method marks for any step that would really reveal feeding relationships. Observation and recording must both appear for full marks.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- A chain of **four organisms beginning with a green plant**, for example **grass → grasshopper → lizard → hawk** (1 mark).
- **Arrows drawn pointing from the organism eaten to the organism that eats it** (1 mark).
- **Every organism clearly named (labelled)**, and the organisms chosen are ones that could really be found in grass behind a school (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks as shown. A chain that begins with an animal loses the first mark; arrows drawn in the wrong direction lose the second. This is the ‘illustrate with diagrams’ exemplar.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Do not touch or handle snakes, scorpions or any unfamiliar animal**, and **wear shoes and long sleeves** against bites and stings.
- **Do not taste any plant, fruit or seed**, **wash the hands with soap** after the study, and **stay with the teacher** throughout.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO sensible precautions (2 marks). Do NOT accept ‘be careful’ on its own.*

**(d)** [1 marks]

- **Every organism has its own part to play** in the ecosystem; if they are removed or killed the **feeding relationships and the balance of the ecosystem are upset**, and the organisms would die away from their habitat.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for the idea of balance, or of respect for living things.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- This sub-strand has ONE content standard and ONE indicator, so the blueprint separates the items by exemplar, showing which of the three exemplars each item serves.
- The five terms named in the curriculum exemplar - producer, primary consumer, secondary consumer, food chain and food web - are each examined directly. Question 1(b) carries two marks per term: one for the idea and one for a correct example.
- Arrows matter. In Question 2(b) and Question 6(b), the arrow must point FROM the organism eaten TO the organism that eats it. A chain written in reverse order scores a maximum of half the marks.
- Question 2(d) asks for an organism that is both predator and prey. The lizard and the snake both qualify; the hawk does not, because nothing in the web feeds on it.
- In Question 2(f), the prediction and the reason are marked separately. A candidate who predicts a decrease earns nothing, even if the reasoning is neatly written.
- In Question 5(b) the two ways must NOT be about feeding - look for shelter, pollination, seed dispersal, oxygen and carbon dioxide, or the enriching of the soil.
- Question 4(b) is the curriculum exemplar on life without the Sun. Accept any well-reasoned consequence; the strongest answers link the cold and darkness to the stopping of photosynthesis and then to the collapse of the food chains.
- Question 6 is fieldwork. If your class actually carried out such a study, accept their own records, tables and drawn food webs as evidence, and let them present the work.
- The two figures are hand-drawn sketches, matching the style pupils are expected to produce in their own answers (the exemplar asks them to illustrate energy flow with diagrams); redraw them on the chalkboard if you wish.
