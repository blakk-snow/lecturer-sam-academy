### Question 1 (8 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- A seed bed is a **piece of land that has been prepared (cleared, dug, levelled)** for planting.
- The soil in it is made **loose and fine** so that seeds can germinate and the young roots can grow easily.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct points (2 marks). Award 1 mark for ‘land prepared for planting’ alone. Do NOT accept ‘a place where seeds are stored’.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Flat bed**
- **Raised bed**
- **Sunken bed**
- (Also accept: **nursery bed**, **ridges**, **mounds**)
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct types (3 marks). Accept ridges and mounds, which are used in many farming communities in Ghana.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- To **loosen the soil** so that seeds germinate easily and roots can spread.
- To **remove weeds, stumps and stones** that would compete with the crops or obstruct the work.
- To **improve drainage and the movement of air** in the soil, and to **mix in manure** so the soil is more fertile.
- To make **planting, weeding and harvesting easier** for the farmer.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct reasons (3 marks). Do NOT accept ‘to make the farm beautiful’.*

### Question 2 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- A - **flat bed** (the soil is level with the surrounding ground)
- B - **raised bed** (the soil is heaped above the surrounding ground)
- C - **sunken bed** (the soil is dug below the surrounding ground)
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each (3 marks). The name must be matched to the correct letter. Accept ‘flat seed bed’, ‘raised seed bed’ and ‘sunken seed bed’.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **B - the raised bed** (1 mark).
- Because the bed stands above the ground, **excess water drains away** and the roots **get air instead of rotting** in the water (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 mark for the correct bed and 1 mark for the reason. If the bed named is wrong, no marks are awarded even if the reason is sound.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **C - the sunken bed** (1 mark).
- Because it lies below the ground it **holds water (and moisture) around the roots for a longer time**, which the crops need in dry weather (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 mark for the correct bed and 1 mark for the reason. Accept ‘it conserves moisture’ for the reason mark.*

**(d)** [1 marks]

- It is **easy and quick to prepare** (little soil has to be moved), so a large area can be planted; it suits most crops on **well-drained land**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for any one correct advantage.*

**(e)** [2 marks]

- The soil of **B is heaped above the ground** while the soil of **C is dug below the ground**.
- **B drains water away** from the crops, whereas **C holds water** around the crops.
- **B is used in wet or waterlogged places**, while **C is used in dry places**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct differences (2 marks). The answer must compare the two beds; a statement about only one of them earns no mark.*

### Question 3 (12 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- (i) Cassava - **stem cuttings**
- (ii) Maize - **seeds** (grains)
- (iii) Plantain - **suckers**
- (iv) Yam - **setts** (pieces cut from the tuber)
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each (4 marks). Accept ‘stem’ for cassava, ‘grains’ for maize and ‘tuber pieces’ for yam. Do NOT accept ‘seeds’ for cassava, plantain or yam.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- Transplanting is the **lifting of young seedlings from a nursery bed** (or seed box) (1 mark)
- and **planting them in the main field** or in their permanent position (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. Award 1 mark only for ‘moving plants from one place to another’ without reference to the nursery or the main field.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Water the nursery bed before lifting** the seedlings, and lift them with **soil (a ball of earth) around the roots** so that the roots are not damaged.
- **Transplant in the evening or on a cool, cloudy day**, not in the hot sun.
- **Water the seedlings immediately after planting** and **provide shade** for a few days until they are established.
- **Firm the soil gently** around each seedling and keep the correct spacing.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct precautions (3 marks). Award method marks for any sensible practice that protects the seedling from damage or from drying up.*

**(d)** [3 marks]

- Tomato **seeds are very small** and need **close attention** (watering, shade and protection from pests), which is easier on a small nursery bed (1 mark).
- The **weak seedlings can be sorted out** and only the **strong, healthy ones transplanted** (1 mark).
- The seedlings can be planted in the field at the **correct spacing**, and **seed is not wasted**; the main field is also free for other use while the seedlings grow (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks, 1 mark each for any three sound reasons.*

### Question 4 (11 marks)

**(a)** [1 marks]

  - *Marking note: 1 mark. The unit must be given; award the mark for ‘18’ with cm.*

**(b)** [1 marks]

- The soil in Bed B was **deeper, looser and more fertile** (it may have had more manure), so the plants obtained more nutrients (1 mark).

**(f)** [2 marks]

- So that **other pupils, teachers and farmers in the community learn** which seed bed gives the best growth and can **use the findings** on their own farms.
- So that the work can be **checked, questioned and repeated** by others, which is how scientists share and confirm results.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO sound reasons (2 marks). Accept ‘to practise explaining ideas clearly’, which is the communication aim of the exemplar.*

### Question 5 (9 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- The **same crop (same variety of pepper)** and the **same number of plants** at the **same spacing**.
- The **same amount of water and manure**, and the **same sunlight and weather** for both beds.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct points (2 marks). Do NOT award a mark for ‘the type of seed bed’ - that is the thing being changed.*

**(b)** [1 marks]

- The **type of seed bed** - one bed is raised and the other is flat.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark. This is the only thing that may be different in a fair test.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Stand the **metre rule (or tape measure) upright beside the plant with the zero mark at the soil surface** (1 mark).
- **Read the mark at the tip of the plant** with the eye level with the reading, and **record the height in centimetres** in the table (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. Award method marks for any correct, careful procedure; look for measuring from the soil level and reading correctly.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Plants do not all grow at the same rate**; a single plant may happen to be unusually tall or unusually short (1 mark).
- The **average of several plants is fairer and more reliable**, because it represents the whole bed and reduces the effect of one odd plant (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. The key idea is the variation between plants and the reliability of the average.*

**(e)** [2 marks]

- A table with the **weeks (1, 2, 3, 4) in the first column** (1 mark)
- and a **column for each bed** - average height on the raised bed (cm) and average height on the flat bed (cm), with the **units given in the headings** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks. Award 1 mark for a ruled table with the time (weeks) as one heading and 1 mark for both beds shown with the unit (cm). A table drawn the other way round (beds as rows, weeks as columns) is equally correct. The table need not contain any readings.*

### Question 6 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Clear the land**: cut down the weeds and remove the rubbish and stones from the piece of land.
- **Mark out the bed** with pegs and a line to the size required (for example 1 m by 3 m).
- **Dig and loosen the soil** inside the marked area with a hoe, removing roots and stumps, and **mix in manure or compost**.
- **Heap the soil above the level of the ground** to form the raised bed, then **level and firm the top** of the bed and water it before planting.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR sensible steps in a workable order (4 marks). This is a practical design task, so award method marks for any step that would really produce a raised bed. The heaping of the soil above the ground must appear somewhere in the answer for full marks.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **Cutlass** - for **clearing the weeds and bushes** from the land.
- **Hoe** - for **digging, loosening and heaping up the soil** to form the bed.
- **Rake** - for **levelling the top of the bed** and removing stones and rubbish.
- **Watering can** - for **watering** the bed and the young plants.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for each tool correctly named WITH its use, maximum 2 marks. A tool named without a use earns no mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Handle the cutlass and hoe carefully** and **stand well apart** while working so that nobody is struck.
- **Wear shoes or boots** (and gloves where possible), **do not play with the tools**, and **wash the hands with soap** after the work.
- **Report any cut or injury to the teacher at once**, and work in the cool of the morning or evening.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO sensible precautions (2 marks). Accept any practical safety measure for garden work. Do NOT accept ‘be careful’ on its own.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Water the crops regularly**, especially in the dry season, and **mulch the bed** with dry grass to keep the soil moist.
- **Weed the bed regularly**, **apply manure or compost**, **control pests and diseases**, and **fence the garden** to keep goats and fowls out.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct practices (2 marks). Accept staking of tomato or pepper plants and thinning where it applies.*
