- **The warm, damp conditions suit the eggs and larvae** (1 mark).
- **Rotting rubbish and waste, which are food and breeding places, are plentiful** (1 mark).
- **The life cycle is completed in only one to three weeks and many eggs are laid each time** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'the flies breed faster because it is warm and wet' for 1 mark only.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **the eggs and larvae cannot move away, so they are easy to destroy**; **killing adults does not stop the ones already breeding in the refuse from hatching**; **removing the breeding places prevents new flies altogether, so the control lasts**; **adults can fly in from other compounds**; **cleaning needs no chemicals, so it is cheap and safe** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who merely repeats 'it is better' without a reason earns no mark.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **It settles on the food and pours out saliva onto it** (1 mark).
- **The saliva dissolves or softens the food** (1 mark).
- **The fly sucks up the liquid food through its sponge-like mouth parts** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'It bites the food' earns no mark; the housefly has no biting mouth parts.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Germs from the gutter stick to the hairy legs, body and mouth parts of the fly** (1 mark).
- **The fly lands on the kenkey and leaves the germs on it (it may also vomit or pass waste on the food)** (1 mark).
- **The customer eats the contaminated food and takes in the germs** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The three steps must be in a sensible order for full marks.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **dysentery**; **diarrhoea**; **cholera**; **typhoid fever**; **food poisoning** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: Malaria earns no mark.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **insecticide on or near food can poison the customers**; **it kills only the flies present at the time**; **the gutter keeps breeding new flies, so they return at once** (1 mark each, maximum 1 mark if no alternative is given).
- **She should clear the gutter and keep the food in a covered glass case or under a net cover** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Full marks need both a reason and the better practice.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **a clean-up of refuse heaps and choked gutters**; **refuse containers with tight lids, emptied regularly**; **keeping the school toilets clean and covered**; **asking vendors to keep food covered**; **keeping the compound swept and dry** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: A bare list is enough; a short description is not required for the mark but should be encouraged.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) Any TWO materials, for example **a plastic bottle** and **bait such as rotting fruit, fish or sugar water** (1 mark for both).
- (ii) **The smell of the bait attracts the flies into the bottle through a narrow opening** (1 mark); **the flies cannot find their way out and are trapped** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept any workable trap, for example a bowl of soapy water with bait covered by a perforated lid.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Count the flies (in a trap or at a fixed place) before the intervention and again some weeks after it** (1 mark).
- **Compare the two counts; fewer flies afterwards shows that the intervention worked** (accept: check whether fewer pupils report sick with diarrhoea) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who writes only 'look at the place' earns no mark; some form of comparison is needed.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO short, correct messages about covering food, clearing refuse, keeping gutters clean, using and covering latrines or washing hands (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: The message must be about reducing flies or protecting food; a general greeting earns no mark.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- (i) **Home W** (1 mark).
- (ii) **39** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 39 flies written with or without the word 'flies'.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Homes with open refuse (W and Y) caught many more flies than homes with covered refuse (X and Z)** (1 mark).
- **Open refuse gives the flies food and a breeding place** (1 mark).
- **Emptying often also matters: X, emptied daily, caught fewer flies than Z, emptied weekly, because refuse left standing allows the eggs to hatch** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Candidates must quote at least one pair of figures from the table for the first mark.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Keep refuse in a covered bin and empty it daily** (1 mark).
- (ii) Any TWO of: **only four homes were tested**; **the count was made on one day only**; **other differences between the homes, such as cleanliness or a nearby dump, were not controlled**; **the traps may not catch the same share of the flies in every home** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'They did not count well' earns no mark without a reason.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- The letters in Fig. 1 are deliberately not in the order of the cycle. Candidates who answer 1(b) with 'A, B, C, D' have read the letters instead of the arrows; train pupils to follow the arrows in any cycle diagram.
- 'Maggot' is the common name for the larva and is fully accepted. 'Caterpillar' is the larva of a butterfly or moth and must not be credited, nor 'nymph', which belongs to insects with incomplete metamorphosis.
- Question 3(c) rewards the key public-health idea of this sub-strand: control the breeding places, not the adult flies. Many candidates will write 'spray insecticide' for everything; the marks go to those who reason about where the eggs and larvae are.
- In Question 4(b) the three steps must form a chain - germs onto the fly, fly onto the food, food into the person. Answers that merely say 'the fly is dirty' earn one mark at most.
- Malaria appears as a distractor in Section A and is explicitly refused in the marking scheme. The housefly does not spread malaria; the mosquito does. This single correction is worth making in every lesson on this sub-strand.
- Question 6 brings numbers into the sub-strand. Candidates should be taught to quote figures from a table when they are asked to use results, and to see that a small sample taken on one day proves very little.
- Follow-up work: let pupils build the simple bottle trap of Question 5(b), set it in two different places in the school for the same length of time, count what is caught and present the comparison to the class.
