**(d)** [3 marks]

- **The predators that controlled the mice and the weaver birds would be gone, so their numbers would increase rapidly** (1 mark).
- The larger numbers of mice and birds would **eat more maize in the field and in store**, so the balance of the ecosystem is upset and the farmers lose more than they gain (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A prediction without justification earns 1 mark only.*

### Question 3 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Green plants make their own food by photosynthesis** and so bring the Sun's energy into the ecosystem (1 mark).
- (ii) Consumers **feed on plants or on other animals**, passing energy and materials along the food chain (1 mark).
- (iii) Decomposers **break down dead organisms and waste and release nutrients back into the soil and water** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'recycle nutrients' for (iii).*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) The **Sun** (1 mark).
- (ii) It means **'is eaten by'** and shows the direction in which food and energy pass (1 mark).
- (iii) **Bacteria** (accept fungi, mushrooms or moulds) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Do not accept 'earthworm' alone in (iii) unless described as feeding on dead matter.*

**(c)** [4 marks]

- Much of the energy is **used for the life processes of the organism (movement, growth, keeping warm)** (1 mark).
- This energy is finally **lost to the surroundings as heat**, and some is lost in **waste and in parts that are not eaten** (1 mark).
- Only the energy **stored in the body can pass to the next level**, so there is **less energy at every step** (1 mark).
- Therefore **food chains are short, and there must be far more producers than primary consumers, and more primary consumers than predators** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The fourth mark requires the link to numbers of organisms.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **About 520 kg** (accept 510 to 530 kg) (1 mark).
- (ii) **About 95 kg** (accept 90 to 100 kg) (1 mark).
- (iii) **520 kg - 95 kg = 425 kg** (accept 410 to 440 kg) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The unit kg is required in all three parts.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) Before 2022 the catch **fell only slowly** (from about 520 kg to about 465 kg) (1 mark).
- (i) After 2022 it **fell steeply and steadily** to about 95 kg (1 mark).
- (ii) The **mining appears to have damaged the river, so that the fish population has collapsed** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (i) a candidate must distinguish the two periods.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- The water is **filled with mud, which cuts off light from water plants and clogs the gills of fish** (1 mark).
- **Poisonous chemicals such as mercury enter the water**, killing organisms and passing along the food chain; breeding grounds on the banks are destroyed (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Two distinct ways are required.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **No (not completely)** - the graph only shows that the two things **happened at the same time**, which does not by itself prove cause (1 mark).
- Other evidence: **tests of the mud and chemicals in the water before and after 2022**, records of the number of fishermen and the nets used, or the catch from a **similar river with no mining** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who simply agrees with the pupil earns no mark.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- Bush burning - **destroys humus, soil organisms and the homes of animals, and leaves the soil bare to erosion** (1 mark).
- Cutting down a forest - **removes habitats, causes soil erosion and reduces rainfall in the area** (1 mark).
- Overhunting - **the species may disappear from the area, affecting all the organisms that depend on it** (1 mark).
- Heavy use of pesticide - **kills useful organisms as well as pests and builds up in the bodies of animals along the food chain** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: One correct effect for each row earns the mark.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- The pesticide **washes into the lagoon and is taken in by the small plants and animals**, and is **not broken down or passed out** of their bodies (1 mark).
- Each fish **eats many of these small organisms and keeps all their pesticide**, and each bird eats many fish (1 mark).
- So the quantity **becomes greater at every step of the food chain**, until the birds at the top are harmed (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The idea of building up along the chain must be clear.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- Prediction 1 with justification: the **grass dies because there is too little water for photosynthesis and growth**, so the producers and the food supply of the ecosystem are reduced (1 mark).
- Prediction 2 with justification: the **plant-eating animals starve, die or move away**, and their **predators decline too**, because each level depends on the one below (1 mark).
- A further correct prediction with justification - for example **water holes dry up so animals crowd together and disease spreads**, or **the bare soil is eroded when the rains return** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A prediction without a justification earns no mark.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **Stop all mining in and along the river** and report illegal mining to the authorities (1 mark).
- **Replant trees and grass along the banks** to hold the soil and shade the water (1 mark).
- **Ban poisons, chemicals and small-mesh nets in fishing, agree on a closed season, and keep refuse out of the river** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Maximum 3 marks; any three sensible measures are accepted.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **Every organism depends on others, so damage to one part upsets the whole system**, including the human beings in it (1 mark).
- People **depend on the ecosystem for food, water, fuel, medicines and income**, and a lost species cannot be brought back (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Two distinct reasons are required.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Reason 1:** the work is **short lived** - when the river and the land are destroyed the mining ends and nothing is left for the community (1 mark).
- **Reason 2:** the damage **costs far more than the wages earned**: farming, fishing and clean drinking water are lost and mercury harms health (1 mark).
- A better way: **licensed and regulated mining with reclamation of the land**, or **farming, fish farming, tree planting, agro-processing or ecotourism** supported by the district assembly (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Candidates may argue for regulated mining; award the marks where the reasoning deals with the damage to the ecosystem.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- The single indicator of this sub-strand is wide, so the paper is organised round the four exemplars: interdependence, ecosystem against habitat, food chains and webs, and the effect of interference on the balance of the system.
- The commonest error in Section B is to draw a food chain with the arrows pointing the wrong way. Teach the arrow as the words 'is eaten by', and insist on it in every exercise.
- In Question 2 the web is deliberately drawn with named organisms, since the task is to read relationships, not to recall names. Candidates must take their chains from the web as drawn and may not add links of their own.
- Question 4(d) is a reasoning item about evidence. Candidates should learn that two things happening at the same time do not by themselves prove that one caused the other, and that a comparison river acts as a control.
- Galamsey appears by name in the curriculum exemplar, and the subject is sensitive in many districts. Mark the science, not the opinion: the marks in 6(c) are for reasoning about the ecosystem and about livelihoods, not for the side the candidate takes.
- Where a candidate names an ecosystem, organism or practice from his or her own district that the scheme does not list, award the mark if it is correct.
- Follow-up work: let the class choose a small area - a school garden plot, a drain, a patch of bush or a pond - and record for one term the organisms found there, the non-living conditions, and any human interference; then build a food web from the records.
