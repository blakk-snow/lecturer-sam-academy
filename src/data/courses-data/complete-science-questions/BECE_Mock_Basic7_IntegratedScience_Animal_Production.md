- (i) Ama - **GH₵1,300** (1 mark); Kojo - **GH₵2,700** (1 mark).
- (ii) Ama - **GH₵800** (1 mark); Kojo - **GH₵700** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Follow through: a correct subtraction from a wrong total in (i) earns the mark in (ii).*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Any TWO of: **goats are ruminants and feed on grass, browse and crop residues, which cost nothing**; **goats can be grazed or tethered on free pasture**; **broilers are monogastric and need bought, prepared feed daily** (1 mark each).
- **Broilers grow quickly, so Kojo sold several batches of many birds within the year** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'Goats eat less' alone, without the reason, earns no mark.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- A clear recommendation (1 mark).
- Any TWO supporting reasons drawn from the table, for example: **the goats gave the higher profit (GH₵800 against GH₵700)**; **the goats needed far less cash for feed (GH₵400 against GH₵2,100)**; **the broilers returned money several times within the year**; **the broilers had larger total sales** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: Either enterprise is acceptable; the marks are for the reasons, which must use the figures.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Divide into groups and share out the area to be visited** (1 mark).
- **Prepare a record sheet and obtain permission before visiting** (1 mark).
- **Observe and count the animals and ask the keepers about their uses, recording at once** (1 mark).
- **Bring the records together, add up the numbers and present them in a table or chart** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Steps given in a workable order; a single step split into two earns one mark only.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **name of animal**; **group (ruminant, monogastric, poultry)**; **number counted**; **home or farm visited**; **uses of the animal**; **by-products obtained** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'Information' or 'remarks' is too vague and earns no mark.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **ask permission and greet the household politely**; **do not enter pens or chase, touch or frighten the animals**; **keep away from dangerous animals such as a bull or a nursing mother**; **wash hands with soap after the visit**; **wear shoes and avoid dung**; **stay in groups and report to the teacher** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'Be careful' or 'behave well' earns no mark without the action.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **the space and housing available**; **the feed she can get cheaply**; **the money she has**; **the market for the product**; **the time and labour needed**; **the neighbours and the rules of the town** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: A reason that is not a real consideration, such as 'the colour of the animal', earns no mark.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **it protects the animals from rain, sun, thieves and stray animals**; **it prevents diseases and parasites, so the animals grow well**; **it keeps flies, smells and germs away from the household and the neighbours**; **the droppings can be collected and used as manure**; **the animals do not stray and destroy the neighbours' property** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: Each reason must be different; 'it keeps them safe' twice earns 1 mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Any TWO of: **they supply protein food (meat, eggs, milk) and improve nutrition**; **they create employment and income**; **their hides, skins and horns supply raw materials for industry and export**; **their dung provides manure for crops**; **they reduce the money spent on imported meat** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: A domestic benefit to one family only, such as 'Afia will eat the eggs', does not answer this part.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- This sub-strand carries TWO content standards, B7/JHS1.2.4.1 and B7/JHS1.2.4.2, and the paper is balanced between them. Teachers who stop at classification leave half of the sub-strand untaught.
- Poultry are monogastric birds. Candidates should be taught the three working groups - ruminants, other monogastric animals and poultry - and should not be penalised for writing 'monogastric' for a fowl, provided they do not call it a ruminant.
- The surest classroom test of a ruminant is behaviour, not anatomy: watch a resting goat or cow bring food back to the mouth and chew it again. Section A item 25 and Question 1(c) both reward that observation.
- Question 3(b) on grit and the gizzard is often answered with 'the fowl likes stones'. Open a gizzard in class if the school kills a fowl; the small stones inside make the point in a way no diagram can.
- Question 4 brings money into the sub-strand. Candidates must subtract total cost from total sales; many subtract only the feed cost. Either enterprise may be recommended in 4(c), but the reasons must come from the figures.
- In Question 2, keep the distinction between a main product and a by-product clear. Milk and eggs are main products; hide, skin, horn, bone and dung are by-products obtained in addition to them.
- Follow-up work: let the class carry out the survey of Question 5 for real, draw a bar chart of the animals counted in the community, and invite a local keeper of animals to answer the pupils' questions about feeding and uses.
