## MARKING SCHEME - SECTION B (60 marks)

*General rules: (1) Accept any other correct and relevant answer. (2) In calculations, award a method mark for correct working even where the final answer is wrong, and insist on the per cent sign where a percentage is asked for. (3) Award marks for correct points, not for repeating the question. (4) Where a candidate gives more answers than are asked for, mark only the first ones up to the number required. ### Question 1 (9 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **P - rod-shaped**, like short sticks (accept *bacillus*, rod-like, cylindrical) (1 mark).
- **Q - round or spherical, joined together in pairs** (accept *coccus*, *cocci*, ball-shaped, in pairs) (1 mark).
- **R - spiral, coiled or wavy** (accept *spirillum*, corkscrew-shaped, curved) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks, 1 mark each. Accept the everyday description or the scientific name; the candidate does not need both. Do not award a mark for naming a disease here - the question asks for shape.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Food poisoning** (from *Salmonella*, *Staphylococcus* or *Escherichia coli*).
- **Gonorrhoea.**
- **Meningitis.**
- Also accept: **cholera, typhoid fever, tuberculosis, tetanus, whooping cough, syphilis, leprosy**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct bacterial diseases (3 marks). Refuse the mark for diseases that are not bacterial - malaria, measles, hepatitis B, HIV and AIDS, ringworm and athlete's foot are caused by protozoa, viruses or fungi.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Warmth** - the food is kept at room temperature, which is warm enough for the bacteria to grow.
- **Moisture** - cooked food such as rice, stew or kenkey holds water, which the bacteria need.
- **Food (nutrients)** - the bacteria feed on the starch and the protein in the meal.
- Also accept: **enough time** for the numbers to double again and again, and the **food left uncovered** so that bacteria from the air, from dust and from flies settle on it.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE conditions (3 marks). 'Dirtiness' alone is too vague; it must be linked to germs reaching the food.*

### Question 2 (12 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **Food poisoning is an illness that follows the eating of food or the drinking of water that contains harmful bacteria or the poisons (toxins) that the bacteria have produced** (1 mark).
- **Salmonella** (1 mark).
- **Staphylococcus** (accept *Escherichia coli*, *Clostridium*, *Shigella*, *Campylobacter*) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks: 1 for the explanation, 1 for each of TWO named bacteria. The explanation must mention the germ or its poison in the food; 'food that is bad' alone earns nothing.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- **Vomiting.**
- **Diarrhoea - frequent, watery stools.**
- **Stomach (abdominal) pain or cramps.**
- **Fever**, with chills.
- Also accept: **nausea, headache, weakness and tiredness, dry mouth and great thirst**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR symptoms (4 marks). Mark only the first four given. 'Sickness' on its own is too vague.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **The food was cooked in the morning and served in the afternoon**, so it stood for several hours in a warm, moist state in which any surviving bacteria multiplied many times over.
- **The pan was left open**, so dust, flies and unwashed hands could carry bacteria onto the food.
- Also accept: **the food was not reheated thoroughly before it was served**, and **there was no cold store or refrigerator** to keep it safe.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO sound reasons (2 marks). The reason must explain how the bacteria got in or how they multiplied.*

**(d)** [3 marks]

- **Wash the hands with soap under running water** before handling food, after using the toilet and after taking money, and keep the fingernails short.
- **Cook the food thoroughly and serve it hot**, and reheat any left-over food until it is steaming before it is sold again.
- **Keep the food covered** with a clean net or lid so that dust and flies cannot reach it.
- Also accept: **use clean water and clean, well-washed utensils**; **do not sell food that was prepared the day before**; **go for the medical examination and hold a valid health certificate from the Environmental Health Unit**; **wear a clean apron and cover the hair**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE practical measures (3 marks). The measure must be something the vendor herself can do; 'the government should build a market' earns nothing here.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- **Meningitis is the inflammation, or swelling, of the meninges - the thin coverings of the brain and the spinal cord** (1 mark).
- It is commonly caused by a **bacterium** (accept *Neisseria meningitidis*, the meningococcus; accept the note that some forms are viral) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 each. The first mark needs the brain or spinal cord covering; 'a disease of the head' is not enough.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **A sudden high fever.**
- **A severe headache.**
- **Vomiting.**
- **A dislike of bright light** - the patient turns away from the window.
- Also accept: **confusion or drowsiness, convulsions (fits), a rash, and in a baby a bulging soft spot on the head with refusal to feed**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE symptoms (3 marks). Do not award the stiff neck, which the question excludes.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Deafness, or damage to the hearing** - the commonest lasting effect.
- **Damage to the brain**, leading to difficulty in learning, loss of memory, epilepsy or paralysis.
- Also accept: **blindness**, **long absence from school**, and **death**, for meningitis can kill within a day or two.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO effects (2 marks). Accept effects on the family, such as the cost of treatment, only as the second answer.*

**(d)** [3 marks]

- **Vaccination of the people at risk**, especially in the meningitis belt before the dry season.
- **Avoid overcrowded sleeping rooms and keep rooms and halls well aired**, with windows open.
- **Cover the mouth and the nose when coughing or sneezing**, and avoid sharing cups, bottles and spoons.
- Also accept: **report every suspected case to a health facility at once so that the patient is treated and close contacts are given preventive drugs**, and **health education through schools, radio and the information centre**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE measures (3 marks). Measures that belong to other diseases - sleeping under a treated net, boiling drinking water - earn nothing here, because meningitis is spread by droplets.*

### Question 5 (9 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- It is caused by a **bacterium** (accept *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*, the gonococcus) (1 mark).
- **Through unprotected sexual contact with a person who has the infection** (1 mark).
- **From an infected mother to her baby during birth**, when the baby passes through the birth canal (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks: 1 for the bacterium and 1 for each of TWO routes. Refuse the mark for shaking hands, sharing a toilet seat, mosquito bites or eating together - these do NOT spread gonorrhoea, and they are the beliefs this lesson exists to correct.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **Pain or a burning feeling when passing urine**, often with the need to pass urine more often.
- **An unusual discharge** from the sexual organs.
- Also accept: **pain in the lower abdomen**, and the important point that **many infected women, and some men, show no symptoms at all** while still able to pass the infection on.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO symptoms (2 marks). Mark this part privately and do not read answers aloud.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **It can damage the reproductive organs and leave the person unable to have children** (infertility).
- **A baby born to an untreated mother may develop a severe infection of the eyes and become blind.**
- Also accept: **lasting pain in the lower abdomen**, **the spread of the infection to the joints and the blood**, and **a greater risk of catching other infections, including HIV**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO effects (2 marks).*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **He stopped the drug as soon as he felt better**, so the bacteria that survive are not killed; they multiply, become **resistant to the antibiotic**, and the illness returns in a form that is much harder to treat.
- **He was never examined or tested**, so the illness may not be gonorrhoea at all, and the drug the hawker sold may be the wrong one, the wrong dose, expired or fake.
- **His partner has not been treated**, so the two will keep passing the infection back to each other and on to others, and untreated gonorrhoea can leave him unable to have children.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO sound reasons (2 marks). The best answers mention resistance or the untreated partner. 'It is bad' earns nothing. Treat this item factually in class and name no one.*

### Question 6 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **The individual** - washes the hands with soap, keeps food and the body clean, avoids crowded, airless rooms, goes early to a health facility when ill and **finishes the full course of the medicine** (1 mark).
- **The community** - organises communal labour to clear gutters and clean the market and the toilets, supports vaccination and education campaigns through the chief, the Assembly member and the information centre, and cares for patients without shaming them (1 mark).
- **The government** - supplies vaccines, drugs, clinics and trained health workers, provides safe water and sanitation, makes and enforces food-safety laws through the Food and Drugs Authority and the Environmental Health Unit, and runs public education (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks, 1 for each level. The role must belong to the level named; a candidate who gives 'build hospitals' for the individual earns nothing for that mark.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- **(i) A short title of a few words** that can be read from a distance, for example *Cook it well, cover it, eat it hot*; *Wash your hands - save your stomach*; *Stop food poisoning today* (1 mark).
- **(ii) Two short, practical messages** (1 mark each), for example: *Wash your hands with soap before you eat*; *Cover your food against flies and dust*; *Do not eat food kept overnight without reheating*; *Buy from vendors with a health certificate*; *Drink only safe water*.
- **(iii) One non-word feature** (1 mark): **a large, clear picture or drawing** - a covered pan, a hand being washed, a fly on open food; also accept **bright, contrasting colours**, **very large bold letters**, a **tick-and-cross comparison**, or the **message repeated in the local language**.
  - *Marking note: 4 marks in all: 1 + 2 + 1. In (i) and (ii) reward brevity - a long paragraph copied from the notes is not a poster message and earns nothing. Any correct message is acceptable; do not insist on the examples given here. No marks are lost for drawing or handwriting quality.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Ask a health worker** - the nurse at the health centre, the community health officer or the Environmental Health Officer - and write down what is said.
- **Use reliable written sources** - the science textbook, a pamphlet from the health centre, or the website of the Ghana Health Service, the Food and Drugs Authority or the World Health Organization.
- **Compare at least two different sources** and keep only the facts on which they agree; reject what somebody merely heard in the street or saw forwarded on social media.
- Also accept: **show the draft poster to the science teacher or the nurse for checking before it is printed**, and **write the source of the information at the bottom of the poster**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE steps (3 marks). At least one step should involve checking or comparing, not merely collecting; but do not withhold marks if three sound collecting steps are given.*
