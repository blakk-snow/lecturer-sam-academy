### Question 1 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- (i) About **78%** (accept 78 to 79 per cent, or 'about four-fifths') (1 mark).
- (ii) Nitrogen gas is **unreactive (inert)**, so the plant cannot take it in through its roots or leaves in that form; it must first be changed into nitrates (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Do not award (ii) for 'there is too little nitrogen in the air'.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) As **nitrates dissolved in the soil water** (accept ammonium compounds) (1 mark).
- (ii) For **making proteins**, which are needed for **growth and the repair of worn-out parts** (1 mark).
- (ii) For making other nitrogen compounds such as **chlorophyll, enzymes and the material of the nucleus** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Do not award a mark for 'to give the plant energy'.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- Nitrogen passes **from the air into the soil** as compounds (fixation) and **from the soil into plants and then animals** (1 mark).
- It is **returned to the soil** when plants and animals die or pass out waste, and **returned to the air** by denitrifying bacteria (1 mark).
- The **same series of changes is then repeated**, so the nitrogen is used over and over again and the amount in nature stays roughly the same (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The idea of returning to the starting point and beginning again must be present for the third mark.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- (i) **Nitrogen** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Apply well-rotted manure or compost**, **apply a nitrogen fertiliser (sulphate of ammonia, urea, NPK)**, or **grow a leguminous crop such as cowpea or groundnut on the plot** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept any one correct remedy in (ii).*

### Question 2 (12 marks)

- 6 - **denitrification** (1 mark).

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Nitrogen gas is fixed** into compounds and reaches the soil as **nitrates** (by nitrogen-fixing bacteria, by lightning, or after nitrification) (1 mark).
- The **grass absorbs the nitrates** through its roots and uses them to make **plant protein** (1 mark).
- The **cow feeds on the grass**, digests the plant protein and uses it to build **animal protein** in its own body (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The three stages must be in the right order. A candidate who leaves out the plant earns at most 1 mark.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Ammonium compounds would not be changed into nitrates**, so the nitrate in the soil would fall (1 mark).
- The plants would be **short of nitrogen**, so their leaves would **turn yellow, growth would be stunted and yields would fall** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who writes only 'the plants would die' without a reason earns 1 mark.*

### Question 3 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- (i) **Root nodules** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Nitrogen-fixing bacteria** (accept Rhizobium) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Do not award (ii) for 'bacteria' alone without 'nitrogen-fixing'.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- The bacteria inside the nodules **fix nitrogen from the air into compounds (nitrates) that the plant can use** (1 mark).
- The plant in turn **supplies the bacteria with food and shelter**, so both gain from the relationship (1 mark).
- When the plant dies or its remains are ploughed in, the **nitrogen compounds are left in the soil**, making it more fertile for the next crop (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'symbiosis' or 'mutualism' as the name of the relationship, but the mark is for the explanation.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Mark out two similar plots** on the same soil, side by side (1 mark).
- **Grow a leguminous crop (cowpea or groundnut) on plot 1** and leave plot 2 without a legume; at the end of the season **dig the legume remains into plot 1**, then **plant maize on both plots and treat them in exactly the same way** (1 mark).
- **Expected result:** the maize on the plot that carried the legume grows **taller and greener and gives a better yield**, showing that the legume has enriched the soil with nitrogen (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A plan without a control plot earns at most 1 mark. Accept pots in place of plots where the pupil keeps the conditions the same.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Advice: he should not burn them**; he should **plough or dig the remains into the soil, or use them to make compost** (1 mark).
- **Reason:** burning **destroys the organic matter, drives off the nitrogen and kills the soil organisms**, while digging the remains in **returns nitrogen compounds and humus to the soil** for the next crop (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The advice must be clear; a candidate who merely lists the effects of burning without advising earns 1 mark.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- Nitrogen fixation - **nitrogen gas is changed into nitrogen compounds that plants can use** - **nitrogen-fixing bacteria** (in root nodules or free in the soil) **or lightning** (1 mark).
- Nitrification - **ammonium compounds are changed into nitrates** - **nitrifying bacteria** (1 mark).
- Ammonification - **the protein in dead plants, dead animals and animal waste is broken down into ammonium compounds** - **decomposers: bacteria and fungi** (1 mark).
- Denitrification - **nitrates in the soil are changed back into nitrogen gas, which escapes into the air** - **denitrifying bacteria** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Each row earns its mark only when both the change and the agent are correct.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- The **energy of the lightning flash makes nitrogen and oxygen of the air combine**, forming oxides of nitrogen (1 mark).
- These oxides **dissolve in the rain water** (forming weak acids) (1 mark).
- The rain **carries them into the soil, where they form nitrates** that the plants can absorb (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'nitric acid' for the dissolved product.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Denitrification** (1 mark).
- (ii) In the **waterlogged soil there is little air**, so **denitrifying bacteria change the nitrates into nitrogen gas**, which escapes; the crop is therefore short of nitrogen (1 mark).
- (iii) **Drain the field** (dig channels or make ridges and furrows) so that air can enter the soil, and apply the fertiliser after draining (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (ii) the loss of nitrate as nitrogen gas must be stated for the mark.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **Grow leguminous crops** (cowpea, groundnut, soya bean) or include them in a crop rotation (1 mark).
- **Apply well-rotted farmyard manure or compost** (1 mark).
- **Plough in crop remains or green manure** (1 mark).
- **Apply a nitrogen fertiliser** such as sulphate of ammonia, urea or NPK (1 mark, maximum 3).
  - *Marking note: Maximum 3 marks.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Different crops **take different nutrients, and in different amounts**, so changing the crop prevents one nutrient from being used up (1 mark).
- The **leguminous crop carries nitrogen-fixing bacteria in its root nodules**, which add nitrogen compounds to the soil (1 mark).
- When the **legume remains are ploughed in**, the nitrogen compounds and humus are left in the soil for the crop that follows (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'it also helps to control pests and diseases' as an extra point only after the three above.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Water plants and algae grow very quickly and cover the surface** of the water (1 mark).
- When they die and decay, the **oxygen dissolved in the water is used up, so fish and other water animals die** and the water becomes unfit for domestic use (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'the river becomes green and smells bad' as the first point.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Organic manure:** it is **cheap and available on the farm**, and it **adds humus**, which improves the structure, the water-holding and the life of the soil (1 mark).
- **Inorganic fertiliser:** it supplies a **known, concentrated amount of nitrogen that acts quickly**, and it is easy to carry and to apply (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: One advantage of each is required; two advantages of only one kind earn 1 mark.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) Burning **destroys the plant cover, the humus and the decomposers and nitrifying bacteria in the topsoil**, and the nitrogen in the burnt material escapes into the air (1 mark).
- (ii) Heavy rain **washes the dissolved nitrates down through the soil, beyond the reach of the roots**, so less nitrogen is available to the crop (1 mark).
- (iii) Clearing the legumes **removes the nitrogen-fixing bacteria of the root nodules**, so less nitrogen from the air is fixed into the soil (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Mark each of the three parts on its own.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Yields will fall season after season**, because the humus and the nitrogen compounds in the topsoil are destroyed by the fire and are not replaced (1 mark).
- The **bare soil will be eroded by rain and wind and its nitrates leached away**, so the topsoil becomes thin and poor (1 mark).
- The **soil organisms (decomposers and nitrifying bacteria) will die out**, so decay and nitrification slow down and the farmer must spend more on fertiliser, or abandon the land (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Each prediction earns its mark only when a reason is given with it.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- A clear, correct message on **not burning the bush**, for example 'Do not burn the bush - fire burns away the food in your soil' (1 mark).
- A clear, correct message on a **good practice**, for example 'Plant cowpea and groundnut - they feed your soil with nitrogen' or 'Cover your land after harvest; bare soil loses its riches to the rain' (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The message must be short, addressed to the community and scientifically correct. Do not award a mark for a message that merely repeats the question.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- This paper covers Sub-Strand 1: Earth Sciences for Basic 9, whose single content standard is the nitrogen cycle. Both indicators are examined: the process of the cycle itself and its importance to the environment.
- Candidates commonly confuse nitrification with nitrogen fixation. Teach the two side by side: fixation starts from nitrogen gas in the air; nitrification starts from ammonium compounds already in the soil.
- In Question 2(a) the arrows must be named with the scientific name of the process. 'Plants take in nitrates' is accepted for arrow 2 only because the syllabus exemplar uses the word assimilation in that sense.
- Question 3(c) is a project task taken directly from the syllabus exemplar. The control plot is the heart of the answer; candidates who grow cowpea on one plot and compare nothing with it cannot score full marks.
- Question 4(c) rewards candidates who connect waterlogging with the absence of air and the activity of denitrifying bacteria. Many will write 'the fertiliser was washed away', which is leaching, not denitrification; award no mark for that in (i).
- Question 6(c) asks for poster messages. Reward brevity and accuracy, as in a real community campaign; a long paragraph is not a poster message.
