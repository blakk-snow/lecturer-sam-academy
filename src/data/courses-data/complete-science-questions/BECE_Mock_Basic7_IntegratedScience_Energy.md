  - *Marking note: 'The ball moves faster' alone earns 1 mark only.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- Potential and kinetic energy are best taught with the body, not the board. A stone held up, dropped, and caught again makes the exchange obvious, and Fig. 1 then reads as a record of something the class has already seen.
- In every calculation insist on three lines: the formula, the substitution and the answer with its unit. Candidates who write only a number lose marks they could have had, since the method marks are given for the first two lines.
- Question 3(c) is the road-safety application of KE = half mv squared. The square is the whole point: doubling the speed multiplies the energy by four. Pupils who grasp that remember it long after the examination.
- The rod experiment in Question 4 needs no laboratory. A knitting needle, a plastic pen barrel and a stick of the same length, a tin of hot water and three blobs of candle wax are enough, and the result is seen in minutes.
- Keep conduction, convection and radiation tied to media: conduction mainly in solids, convection in liquids and gases, radiation through empty space. Candidates who learn the three words without the media interchange them under pressure.
- The three-card experiment in Question 5(a) is the standard demonstration of rectilinear propagation. The telling observation is the NEGATIVE one - the flame disappears when a card is moved out of line - and candidates should be trained to report it.
- Follow-up work: on a sunny morning, let pupils hold a glass prism or a basin of water with a mirror in it and throw a spectrum onto a white wall. Seeing the colours themselves settles the meaning of dispersion for good.
