- **No / the pupil is wrong** (1 mark).
- **Reason:** I = V/R, so at the same voltage doubling the resistance **halves the current** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The reason mark requires the relation I = V/R or a clear statement of the inverse relationship.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- J - **a resistor** (1 mark).
- K - **a light-emitting diode (LED)** (1 mark).
- L - **the cells (battery)** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'battery' for L; 'diode' alone for K earns the mark only if 'light-emitting' or LED is written.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Forward biased** (1 mark).
- (ii) The **positive terminal of the cells is connected to the anode of the LED** (the broad side of the triangle), so current flows in the direction in which the triangle points (1 mark).
- (ii) **The LED conducts and gives out light**, which happens only when it is forward biased (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept an answer argued from the observation (the LED lights, so it must be forward biased) for the second mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **The LED would not light (goes off)** (1 mark).
- Because it is now **reverse biased, and a diode conducts in one direction only**, so practically no current flows (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'It will burn out' earns no mark.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- (i) **To limit the current so that the LED is not damaged** (1 mark).
- (ii) **I = V/R = 1.2/60 = 0.02 A** (accept 20 mA; unit required) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (i) 'to increase the brightness' earns no mark.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **A component that allows current to flow in one direction only** (1 mark).
- (ii) **The positive of the supply is joined to the anode**, so the diode conducts (1 mark).
- (iii) **The positive of the supply is joined to the cathode**, so practically no current flows (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept answers written in terms of 'the way the cells are turned round' if the direction of conduction is clear.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Controls or limits the current** (opposes the flow of current) (1 mark).
- (ii) **Stores electric charge and releases it when needed** (1 mark).
- (iii) **Gives out light when current passes through it in the forward direction** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (ii) 'stores electricity' is accepted.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **LED lamps give the same light for much less electrical energy** (a filament lamp wastes most of its energy as heat), so the bill falls (1 mark).
- **They last much longer** (accept: they run cool and are safer, or they suit a solar supply) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Two distinct reasons are required.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **The LED lights very brightly for a moment and is then damaged (burns out)**, because the current is not limited (1 mark).
- **A resistor should be connected in series with the LED** to limit the current to about 20 mA (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who says only 'it will spoil' without the idea of the large current earns 1 mark.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Set-up:** the cell, switch, lamp and diode joined in series in a complete circuit (1 mark).
- **Method:** close the switch and note the lamp; then turn the diode round (or turn the cell round), leaving everything else unchanged, and note the lamp again (1 mark).
- **Observation:** the lamp lights in one connection only and does not light in the other (1 mark).
- **Conclusion:** the diode **allows current in one direction only - it conducts when forward biased** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A group that turns the cell round instead of the diode gains the same marks.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **Keep the switch open until the circuit has been checked, and never short-circuit the cell** (1 mark).
- **Use only the low-voltage cells supplied (never the mains), and disconnect the cell when the work is finished** (accept: include a series resistor with an LED; make clean tight connections) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Two distinct precautions are required.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **The circuit is not complete** - a loose or broken connection, or the switch left open (1 mark).
- **The cell is flat, the lamp's filament is broken, or the diode itself is damaged** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Two different reasons are required; 'the diode is reverse biased' cannot explain a failure in both connections and earns no mark.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- The curriculum limits the calculations in this sub-strand to the relation V = IR and to simple series and parallel reasoning. Nothing in this paper requires the formula for resistors in parallel, and candidates should not be expected to use it.
- In Question 1(b) the mark for a complete circuit is the one most often lost: many candidates draw the symbols correctly but leave a gap in the loop. Mark the loop first, then the symbols.
- Question 2 rewards reasoning rather than recall. In (b)(ii) and (c)(i) the statement and the reason carry separate marks, and a wrong statement cannot be rescued by a good reason.
- Candidates frequently reverse the relation in Question 3(c), writing R = VI. Award no marks for 6 ohms, but award the first mark wherever R = V/I is written, even if the division is then done wrongly.
- In Question 4(b) accept either line of argument: the physical one from the direction of the triangle and the positive terminal, or the observational one from the fact that the LED is alight. Both are sound at this level.
- The word 'bias' is new to many candidates. Encourage the plain form of words: forward bias is the way round that lets the current through, reverse bias is the way round that blocks it.
- Follow-up work: give each group an LED, a 100 ohm resistor, a switch and two cells, and let them build the circuit of Fig. 2, reverse the cells, and then add a large capacitor across the LED to see it fade slowly after the switch is opened. Each group should report its findings to the class, as the exemplar requires.
