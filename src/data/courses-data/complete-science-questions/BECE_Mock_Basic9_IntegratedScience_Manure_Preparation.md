### Question 1 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- (i) **Material of plant or animal origin added to the soil to supply nutrients and improve it** (1 mark).
- (ii) Manure is made from **plant and animal waste and adds humus, improving the structure and the water-holding of the soil**, while fertiliser is manufactured and supplies nutrients only (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'manure acts slowly, fertiliser acts quickly' in (ii).*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Farmyard manure** (1 mark).
- **Compost** (1 mark).
- **Green manure** (accept poultry manure, liquid manure or treated night soil) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Do not accept the names of inorganic fertilisers.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- (i) Two plant wastes, for example **dry leaves and kitchen vegetable peelings** (accept weeds, maize stalks, cocoa pod husks, rice husk) (1 mark).
- (i) Two animal wastes, for example **poultry droppings and cow dung** (accept pig dung, goat droppings, bone meal) (1 mark).
- (ii) **Nitrogen** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Both examples are needed in each half of (i).*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- A sandy soil **loses water and nutrients quickly**, since the particles are large and the soil is poor in humus (1 mark).
- The **humus in the manure holds water and nutrients in the topsoil** where the roots can reach them, and feeds the soil organisms (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A general answer such as 'manure is good for soil' earns 1 mark only.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- (i) To **test the condition inside the heap** from time to time (1 mark).
- (ii) The heap is **working well: the organisms are active (it is warm) and there is enough moisture** (1 mark).
- (iii) To **add water when the heap becomes dry**, since the organisms of decay need moisture (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (ii) accept 'decomposition is going on well'.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- The **micro-organisms respire as they break down the waste and release heat**, which is trapped inside the covered heap (1 mark).
- The heat **destroys many weed seeds, insect eggs and disease-causing organisms** (accept: it speeds up the rotting) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'The Sun heats the heap' earns no mark.*

### Question 3 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Collect and sort the waste**, removing plastics, glass, metal and stones, and **chop large materials into small pieces** (1 mark).
- **Choose a shady site away from classrooms and from any well or stream** and prepare the heap or pit (1 mark).
- **Build the heap in layers** - coarse material, green plant waste, animal dung, a thin layer of topsoil - **watering each layer** (1 mark).
- **Cover the heap and turn it every two or three weeks, watering when dry**, until the compost is ready (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Steps given out of order lose one mark overall.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Plastics such as water sachets and bags** (1 mark).
- **Broken glass or metal (tins, nails)** (1 mark).
- **Stones, rubber, batteries or chemicals** (accept badly diseased plants) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Maximum 3 marks.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- (i) It is **dark brown or black and crumbly, and the original materials can no longer be recognised** (1 mark).
- (i) It **smells like fresh earth, not foul, and the heap has cooled and shrunk** (1 mark).
- (ii) About **two to three months** (accept 6 to 12 weeks) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'about three months' in (ii).*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **18 kg** (1 mark).
- (ii) **7 kg** (1 mark).
- (iii) **18 kg - 7 kg = 11 kg** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The unit kg is required in all three parts.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Well-rotted poultry manure (plot Y)** (1 mark).
- (ii) Plot W **received no manure, so the soil could not supply enough nutrients**, especially nitrogen (1 mark).
- (iii) **Adding organic manure raises the yield of maize**, and in this trial poultry manure gave the biggest increase (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (iii) a conclusion that goes beyond the data earns no mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- The **same variety of maize, sown on the same day at the same spacing, on plots of the same size and soil** (1 mark).
- The **same weeding, watering, pest control and time of harvest** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Two distinct factors are required.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- Advise him to **compost the stalks, weeds and kitchen waste and use the compost on his field** (1 mark).
- **Reason:** plot Z shows that **compost gave almost as good a yield as the bought manure**, the materials cost nothing, and the compost improves the soil for later seasons (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The reason must refer to the data or to the benefit to the soil.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- Sandy soil - **work in plenty of well-rotted manure or compost, because the humus holds water and nutrients** (1 mark).
- Heavy clay - **work in manure to open up the soil so that air and water move through it** and the soil is easier to work (1 mark).
- Heavy rains - **work the manure into the soil rather than leave it on the surface, where it would be washed away** (1 mark).
- Fresh poultry droppings - **rot or compost them first, because fresh droppings are strong enough to scorch young plants** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Each mark needs both the action and the reason.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) So that it has time to **mix with the soil and break down, releasing its nutrients when the crop needs them** (1 mark).
- (ii) It is **too strong and may scorch the plants, and it may carry weed seeds and disease-causing organisms** (1 mark).
- (iii) The **humus added each year makes the soil crumbly and open, so it holds water and is easier to dig** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'it attracts flies' as part of the answer in (ii).*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- The practice is **poor**: burning **destroys the organic matter that would have formed humus and sends the nitrogen into the air** (1 mark).
- The fire also **kills useful soil organisms and leaves the soil bare to erosion**; the ash gives some potash but **no humus** (1 mark).
- Advice: he should **compost the residues or plough them into the soil** and apply the compost to the field (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who defends burning cannot earn the first two marks.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- A **suitable shaded site away from classrooms, the kitchen and any well or stream** (1 mark).
- **Separate containers or a sorting point** so that waste that rots is kept apart from plastics, glass and metal (1 mark).
- **Tools (cutlass, hoe, fork, watering can, gloves) and a duty roster with a record book** for turning and watering (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Maximum 3 marks; any three sensible arrangements are accepted.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **Wear gloves or use a tool, and wash the hands with soap and water afterwards and before eating** (1 mark).
- **Do not handle broken glass, sharp metal or chemicals; keep the heap away from drinking water and keep it covered** (accept: never use untreated night soil on vegetables eaten raw) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Two distinct rules are required.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **The waste becomes free manure that raises yields and saves the money spent on fertiliser** (1 mark).
- **The compound and gutters are kept clean**, so there are fewer flies, mosquitoes, smells and blocked drains (1 mark).
- **Burning pollutes the air and destroys humus, and dumping pollutes soil and water**, while compost improves the soil season after season (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Three distinct points are needed; repetition of one idea earns 1 mark.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- The three indicators of this sub-strand rise from listing the wastes, through describing the preparation, to actually preparing manure. A written paper can only sample the practical work, so Questions 2, 3 and 6 are built round a heap the class should have made before the examination.
- In Question 2(a) candidates are asked to SUGGEST materials for the layers. Any sensible material placed in the right position earns the mark: coarse and woody at the bottom, green waste next, animal waste above it and a cover on top.
- Candidates often confuse manure with fertiliser. Insist on the two differences that matter: the origin of the material, and the fact that manure adds humus and so improves the structure of the soil.
- In Question 4 the unit kg must be written. Readings within one small square are accepted, so 17 to 19 kg is correct for plot Y.
- Question 5(a) requires both an action and a reason in each row of the table. A row that gives only the action earns no mark.
- Night soil appears in the curriculum exemplar. Treat it factually: it may be used only after thorough composting or treatment, and never on vegetables that are eaten raw.
- Follow-up work: build two small heaps on the school garden, turn one every fortnight and leave the other untouched; record the temperature with a stick test and the appearance of each heap every two weeks, and compare the compost after three months.
