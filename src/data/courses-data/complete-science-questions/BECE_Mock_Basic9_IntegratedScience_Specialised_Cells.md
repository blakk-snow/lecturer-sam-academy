*General rules: (1) Accept any other correct and relevant answer. ### Question 1 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- **Specialised cell** - a cell whose shape and structure have changed so that it can carry out one particular job very well (1 mark).
- **Tissue** - a group of similar cells that work together to carry out the same job (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'a cell adapted to perform a special function'. Do not award the tissue mark for 'a part of the body' alone.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Cell -> tissue -> organ -> organ system (-> organism)** (1 mark; the order must be correct).
- (ii) Tissue: **muscle tissue**, **blood**, **nervous tissue** or **bone** (1 mark).
- (ii) Organ: **heart**, **lung**, **stomach**, **kidney**, **eye** or **brain** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (ii) do not accept 'skin cell' as a tissue or 'blood cell' as an organ.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Unspecialised cells divide** (by cell division) to produce new cells (1 mark).
- The new cells then **differentiate**, that is they change their shape, size and contents so that each can carry out one particular job (1 mark).
- In the plant this happens in the **meristems - the tips of roots and shoots and the cambium**; in the human being the dividing cells are the **stem cells**, and similar specialised cells then group together to form tissues (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The word differentiation, or a clear description of it, is needed for the second mark.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- Each kind of cell carries out its own job **more efficiently**, because its structure suits that job (1 mark).
- The **work of the body is shared out (division of labour)**, so many different activities go on at the same time (1 mark).
- It allows the organism to **grow larger and more complex** and to survive in its surroundings (1 mark, maximum 2).
  - *Marking note: Maximum 2 marks.*

### Question 2 (12 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- A - **epidermal cell** (1 mark).
- B - **guard cells** (a pair of guard cells with a stoma between them) (1 mark).
- C - **xylem vessel** (xylem) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In B accept 'guard cell'. Do not award the mark for 'stoma' alone, since the stoma is the opening and not the cell.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- A - **protects the tissues beneath and reduces the loss of water** from the leaf (1 mark).
- B - **opens and closes the stoma**, controlling the movement of gases into and out of the leaf and the loss of water vapour (1 mark).
- C - **carries water and dissolved mineral salts from the root to the rest of the plant** (and helps to support the plant) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Only one function is required for each cell; mark the first one given.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- It is a **long, narrow, hollow tube**, so water flows through it easily (1 mark).
- It has **no cross walls**, since the end walls have broken down, so the water column is not interrupted (1 mark).
- It is made of **dead, empty cells with no cytoplasm or nucleus**, leaving the whole space for water (1 mark).
- Its **walls are thickened with lignin**, which strengthens the tube and supports the plant (1 mark, maximum 3).
  - *Marking note: Maximum 3 marks, one for each correct feature.*

**(d)** [3 marks]

- **Water passes into the guard cells by osmosis** and they become **turgid** (1 mark).
- Because the **inner wall is thicker and less elastic than the outer wall**, the turgid cells **curve away from each other** and the stoma opens (1 mark).
- On a hot afternoon when water is short, the guard cells **lose water, become flaccid and close the stoma**, so that less water vapour is lost from the leaf (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The second mark depends on the idea of the cells curving apart; a candidate who writes only 'they open' does not earn it.*

### Question 3 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- The cambium is a **layer of thin-walled cells that keep dividing** (a meristem) (1 mark).
- It lies **between the xylem and the phloem** in the vascular bundle of the stem and the root (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'inside the bark, between the wood and the phloem'.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Epidermal cell - **forms a protective covering over the plant and reduces the loss of water** (1 mark).
- Cambium cell - **divides to produce new xylem and phloem cells, so that the stem grows thicker** (1 mark).
- Xylem vessel - **carries water and dissolved mineral salts upwards and gives support to the plant** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Mark each row on its own. A function given for the wrong cell scores nothing.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Water (and dissolved mineral salts) cannot reach the leaves**, so the leaves **wilt** and later dry up (1 mark).
- **Photosynthesis is reduced**, because water and minerals are short, so the tree makes less food and **grows poorly** (1 mark).
- The tree **loses support and may die** if the blockage is not cleared (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'the pods or fruits are small or do not form' as an alternative third point.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- The cocoa tree, a **dicotyledonous plant, has a cambium** whose cells keep dividing and add **new xylem and phloem** each season, so the stem becomes thicker (1 mark).
- The maize plant, a **monocotyledon, has no cambium** in its vascular bundles, so no new xylem and phloem are formed and the stem does not thicken in the same way (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Both halves of the comparison are needed for full marks; one correct half earns 1 mark.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- P - **nerve cell** (neurone) (1 mark).
- Q - **red blood cell** (erythrocyte) (1 mark).
- R - **muscle cell** (muscle fibre) (1 mark).
- S - **sperm cell** (spermatozoon) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'neurone' for P and 'muscle fibre' for R. Do not accept 'blood' alone for Q.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- P - **carries messages (nerve impulses)** from one part of the body to another, for example from the brain to a muscle (1 mark).
- Q - **carries oxygen** from the lungs to all the cells of the body (1 mark).
- S - **fertilises the egg cell (ovum)** of the female (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'carries carbon dioxide away' as part of the answer for Q.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- (i) It has **no nucleus**, so there is more room for the **haemoglobin** that carries the oxygen (1 mark).
- (i) Its **biconcave disc shape gives a large surface area** for taking in and giving out oxygen; it is also small and flexible enough to pass through narrow capillaries (1 mark).
- (ii) It is **very long**, so one cell can carry a message a long way, and it has **fine branched endings** for passing the message to the next cell (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (i) award one mark for each distinct feature with its reason. A feature without a reason earns the mark only where the reason is obvious.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- The red blood cell has **no nucleus** when mature; the white blood cell **has a nucleus** (1 mark).
- The red blood cell contains **haemoglobin and is red**; the white blood cell has **no haemoglobin and is colourless** (1 mark).
- The red blood cell is a **biconcave disc of fixed shape**; the white blood cell can **change its shape** to engulf germs (1 mark).
- Red blood cells are **far more numerous** than white blood cells, and they carry oxygen while white cells defend the body (1 mark, maximum 3).
  - *Marking note: Maximum 3 marks. Each difference must compare the two cells.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) With **fewer red blood cells there is less haemoglobin**, so **less oxygen is carried** to the cells of her body (1 mark).
- (i) With less oxygen, **less energy is released during respiration** in the cells, so she feels weak and tired (1 mark).
- (ii) A named iron-rich food - **kontomire (cocoyam leaves), liver, beans, dark green leafy vegetables, fish or meat** - together with the mineral **iron** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (ii) the food and the mineral are both needed for the mark. Accept any locally sold iron-rich food.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Muscle cells **contract (shorten) and so bring about movement** of the parts of the body (1 mark).
- Nerve cells **carry messages (impulses)** from the sense organs to the brain and from the brain to the muscles (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'cause movement' for the muscle cell.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- The **mitochondria are the parts of the cell where respiration takes place and energy is released** (1 mark).
- A muscle cell **needs a large supply of energy in order to contract again and again**, so it contains many mitochondria (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The link between respiration, energy and contraction must be clear for the second mark.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- Correct outline: an **oval or pear-shaped head, a short mid-piece and a long tail**, drawn large and in clear pencil lines (1 mark).
- **Head** correctly labelled (1 mark; the label line must touch the head).
- **Nucleus** shown inside the head and labelled (1 mark).
- **Mid-piece** and **tail** correctly labelled (1 mark for both).
  - *Marking note: Do not award marks for a small, sketchy or shaded drawing. Labels written without label lines may still earn the marks where the part meant is clear.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- It has a **long tail that lashes from side to side**, so that the cell can swim towards the egg (1 mark).
- Its **mid-piece contains many mitochondria**, which release the energy needed for swimming (1 mark).
- It is **small and streamlined**, and the **tip of its head carries enzymes** that help it to enter the egg (1 mark, maximum 2).
  - *Marking note: Maximum 2 marks.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **No, the pupil is wrong** (1 mark).
- A reason supported by an example: the cells **differ in shape and structure because each is suited to a different job** - for example, a **red blood cell has no nucleus and is a biconcave disc so that it can carry oxygen**, while a **nerve cell is long and branched so that it can carry messages** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The first mark is for taking a clear position; it is lost where the candidate agrees with the statement. The second mark requires a named cell and the job it does.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- This paper covers the whole of Sub-Strand 2: Living Cells for Basic 9. The syllabus names the cells to be studied, and only those cells are examined: epidermal cells, guard cells, cambium and xylem in the dicotyledonous plant, and nerve, blood, muscle and sperm cells in the human being.
- Candidates should be taught to write the function of a cell in terms of what the cell does for the whole organism. 'The xylem is in the stem' is a position, not a function, and earns nothing.
- In Question 2(a) many candidates will write 'stoma' for B. The stoma is the opening between the two guard cells; only the name of the cells earns the mark.
- Question 2(d) is the most demanding item in Section B. The marking scheme requires the thicker inner wall of the guard cell; teachers should demonstrate this with two long balloons stuck together along one side.
- Question 6(a) is a drawing task. Insist throughout the year on large, unshaded pencil drawings with ruled label lines that touch the part named; this is where marks are most often lost in the BECE.
- Question 6(c) asks the candidate to take a position and defend it. Reward a clear 'no' supported by one named cell and its job; do not expect a long essay.
- Follow-up practical work: prepare a leaf-peel slide from a hibiscus or waterleaf plant and let the pupils find the guard cells and stomata for themselves, and compare them with a prepared slide of human blood.
