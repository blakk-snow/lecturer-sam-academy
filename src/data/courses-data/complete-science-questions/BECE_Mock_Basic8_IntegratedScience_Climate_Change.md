### Question 1 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **P - energy, that is heat and light, travelling from the sun to the surface of the earth** (1 mark).
- **Q - heat given off by the warmed earth, escaping through the layer into space** (1 mark).
- **R - heat that the layer of gases sends back down to the surface of the earth** (accept 'heat trapped and returned by the gases') (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks, 1 mark each. For P accept 'sunlight', 'solar energy' or 'radiation from the sun'. For R the answer must show the heat coming BACK to the earth; 'heat in the air' is not enough.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **Carbon dioxide** (1 mark).
- **Methane** (1 mark).
- Also accept: **water vapour**, **nitrous oxide**, **chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO (2 marks). Do not accept oxygen or nitrogen; they are the commonest gases in the air but they do not trap heat in this way.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **The average temperature of the earth rises** - the earth becomes warmer. This is called global warming (1 mark).
- **The thicker layer absorbs more of the heat that is leaving the earth**, so less heat escapes into space along arrow Q (1 mark).
- **More of that heat is sent back down to the surface along arrow R**, so heat builds up at the surface and in the lower air (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks. The first mark is for the rise in temperature; the other two for the mechanism - less heat escaping and more heat returned. An answer that says only 'the earth becomes hot' earns 1 mark.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Burning fuels** - firewood, charcoal, petrol, diesel and gas - in kitchens, vehicles, generators and factories.
- **Cutting down and burning forests**, which both releases carbon dioxide and removes the trees that would have absorbed it.
- Also accept: **bush burning before the planting season**; **heaps of refuse left to rot, and landfill sites, which give off methane**; **keeping large herds of cattle**; **flooded rice fields**; **industry and mining**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO activities (2 marks). The activity must be one that people do; a volcano is natural and earns nothing.*

### Question 2 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- **1980 - 27.0 °C** (1 mark).
- **2020 - 28.4 °C** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 each. The unit °C must appear at least once in the answer; do not take the mark off twice for the same omission.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **28.4 − 27.0** (1 mark for the correct working).
- **= 1.4 °C** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks. Award the method mark for the correct subtraction even where the arithmetic slips. Insist on the unit in the final answer.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **The temperature rises steadily from one reading to the next**, by about 0.3 to 0.4 °C every ten years, and at no point does it fall (1 mark).
- **This suggests that the climate of the area is becoming warmer** - it is a sign of climate change, not merely a hot year (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 for describing the steady rise, 1 for the conclusion. 'The line goes up' alone earns the first mark only.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **The rains no longer begin and end when they used to**, and the rainy season has become shorter or less reliable.
- **Longer and hotter dry spells**, with streams, dams and wells drying up.
- Also accept: **heavier storms and more flooding when the rain does come**; **harvests failing, or crops that once grew well no longer doing so**; **grass and trees drying earlier**; **more dust and a longer Harmattan**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO signs (2 marks). The sign must be a long-term change, not a single storm or one hot afternoon.*

**(e)** [2 marks]

- **One station describes one place only**, and its reading can be affected by local changes - buildings, tarred roads or cleared trees round the station - rather than by the climate of the whole country (1 mark).
- **Collect and compare records from several stations and over as many years as possible**, set them beside the figures published by the Ghana Meteorological Agency or a world body, and add what the farmers and fishers of the area report about the seasons (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 for the weakness of a single record, 1 for a sensible way of strengthening the evidence. This part carries CP 5.1, combining information from several sources to reach a conclusion.*

### Question 3 (12 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **Climate change is a long-term change in the average pattern of temperature, rainfall and the seasons** of a place, or of the earth as a whole, observed over many years (2 marks).
- **Weather is the condition of the atmosphere at one place at one moment** - today's sunshine, this afternoon's storm - **while climate is the average of that weather taken over many years**, usually thirty or more (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks: 2 for the definition, which must contain the idea of a LONG-TERM change in the average, and 1 for the distinction. A candidate who says only 'the weather is changing' earns 1 mark.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- **Burning petrol, diesel, charcoal and firewood** in vehicles, generators and kitchens - releases **carbon dioxide**.
- **Cutting down forests (deforestation)** - the carbon stored in the wood is released, and the **absorption of carbon dioxide by the trees is lost**.
- **Bush burning before planting** - releases **carbon dioxide** and destroys the grass and young trees that would absorb it.
- **Heaping refuse in dumps where it rots** - releases **methane**.
- Also accept: **keeping large herds of cattle** (methane); **industry, mining and cement works** (carbon dioxide and other gases); **flooded rice fields** (methane).
  - *Marking note: 4 marks: 1 mark for each activity correctly paired with its gas or process. An activity with no gas named earns the mark only if the process, such as 'the trees that absorb carbon dioxide are lost', is clearly given.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **The rains no longer come when expected**, so seed planted at the usual time dies, or ripening crops are caught by an early dry spell, and harvests fail.
- **Longer and hotter dry seasons** dry up the streams, the dams and the pasture, so there is too little water for crops and animals.
- **Heavier storms and floods** wash away crops, seedlings and the topsoil itself.
- Also accept: **more pests and crop diseases** in the warmer conditions; **cocoa and other tree crops suffering where the dry season is longer and hotter**; **lower yields, so food prices rise and farmers earn less**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE effects (3 marks). The effect must be explained, not merely named: 'poor harvest' earns the mark only if the candidate says why.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **The rising sea floods the town and washes the land away**, destroying houses, roads and coconut farms, so that families have to move inland.
- **Salt water enters the wells, the lagoons and the farmland**, so the water is no longer fit to drink and crops will not grow.
- Also accept: **fish catches fall** as the warmer sea changes where the fish are found, so fishers earn less; **storms are stronger and the sea rougher**, so fishing is more dangerous.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO effects (2 marks).*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Adaptation is changing the way we live and work so that we can cope with the changes that are already happening** (1 mark).
- **Example of adaptation**: planting early-maturing or drought-resistant maize; building a sea defence wall at Keta; harvesting rain water; changing the planting calendar; storing part of the harvest (1 mark).
- **Mitigation is reducing the amount of greenhouse gas we release, or taking it back out of the air, so that the warming itself is slowed** (1 mark).
- **Example of mitigation**: using solar panels instead of a diesel generator; planting trees; cooking on an improved stove or on gas instead of an open wood fire; recycling instead of burning refuse (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 4 marks: 1 for each definition and 1 for each matching example. The commonest error is to give a mitigation example for adaptation; the test is simple - adaptation deals with the EFFECTS, mitigation deals with the CAUSE.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Plant early-maturing and drought-resistant varieties** of maize, millet or cowpea, so that the crop ripens before the rains stop.
- **Change the planting calendar and follow the weather forecast** from the Meteorological Agency and the agricultural extension officer instead of the old fixed dates.
- **Build small dams and dugouts, harvest rain water and irrigate** so that there is water in the dry months.
- Also accept: **improved storage so that part of the harvest is kept against a bad season**; **mixed cropping and keeping livestock as well as crops, so that one failure does not ruin the family**; **planting trees as windbreaks and for shade**; **joining a savings group or a crop insurance scheme**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE measures (3 marks). The measure must help the community COPE; 'stop using generators' is mitigation and earns nothing here.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Plant trees and protect those already standing**, so that carbon dioxide is taken out of the air and stored in the wood.
- **Stop bush burning** and use cleared weeds as compost or mulch instead of setting fire to them.
- **Change the way energy is used** - improved fuel-saving stoves, gas or solar instead of the open wood fire and the diesel generator.
- Also accept: **separate and compost organic waste instead of dumping or burning it**; **recycle plastic, metal and glass**; **walk, cycle or share transport**; **protect the forest reserve and the trees along the riverbank**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE actions (3 marks). The action must reduce the gases released or take them out of the air.*

### Question 5 (8 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- **A green economy is one in which people earn a living and the country develops without destroying the environment** (1 mark).
- **It releases little pollution and few greenhouse gases, uses natural resources carefully so that they are not used up, and still provides jobs and incomes** for the people (1 mark).
- Accept the short form: *growth that does not cost us the land, the water and the air.*
  - *Marking note: 2 marks. An answer about the colour green, or about growing vegetables only, earns nothing.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Installing and repairing solar panels and solar lanterns** - supplies electricity without burning fuel, so no greenhouse gas is released.
- **Raising seedlings in a nursery and planting trees** - the growing trees take carbon dioxide out of the air, hold the soil and bring back the forest.
- **Collecting and recycling plastic, metal and electronic waste** - keeps the waste out of the gutters, the land and the sea, and saves the energy that making new material would cost.
- Also accept: **organic farming and composting**; **making and selling improved fuel-saving stoves**; **beekeeping**; **eco-tourism and work in a forest reserve**; **repairing goods instead of replacing them**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE (3 marks). The mark is given only where the work AND its benefit to the environment are both stated.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Plant trees on the compound and care for them** - shade, cleaner air and carbon dioxide absorbed.
- **Separate the waste and compost the food and garden waste** - the compost feeds the school garden and the refuse that would rot in a dump and release methane is kept out of it.
- **Stop burning refuse on the compound** - no poisonous smoke for the pupils to breathe and no carbon dioxide released.
- Also accept: **switch off lights and fans when rooms are empty, and fit solar panels or solar lamps**; **harvest rain water from the roof**; **a walking, cycling or transport-sharing day**; **a science or environment club that teaches the community**; **use both sides of every sheet of paper**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE (3 marks), and the benefit must be stated. Reward a candidate who names something the school could really do tomorrow over one who names an expensive project.*

### Question 6 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- **Growing trees take carbon dioxide out of the air and store the carbon in their wood**, so less of the gas is left to trap heat.
- **Trees cool and shade the land, hold the soil against erosion, slow run-off and flooding, and help to keep rainfall steady**; they also restore the habitats of plants and animals.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO reasons (2 marks). The first reason is the central one; award the second mark for any genuine environmental benefit.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Plan**: decide where on the compound the trees will stand and how many are needed, and obtain the permission of the head teacher (1 mark).
- **Obtain the right seedlings**: choose species suited to the place - shade, fruit or timber - and collect them from the Forestry Commission, the agricultural office or a local nursery (1 mark).
- **Plant and care for them**: dig the holes, plant early in the rainy season, water, mulch and fence or stake each tree, and **assign a named pupil or class to each tree** so that it is watered and protected after the ceremony (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE steps (3 marks), provided they are in a sensible order. Give particular credit to any candidate who provides for the trees to be CARED FOR after planting; that is where most campaigns fail.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **One real difficulty** (1 mark): the seedlings die for want of water in the dry season; goats, cattle or careless people break them; there is no money for seedlings or tools; nobody cares for them during the long holidays.
- **A matching solution** (1 mark): a written watering rota with mulching round each tree; a stake and a wire or palm-frond guard; free seedlings requested from the Forestry Commission or the assembly; the caretaker, or families living near the school, asked to water during the holidays.
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 for the difficulty, 1 for a solution that actually answers it. A solution that does not match the difficulty earns nothing.*

**(d)** [3 marks]

- **Why the law is needed** (1 mark): bush fires destroy trees, grass, crops and wildlife, burn out the humus and leave the soil bare to erosion, and they release large amounts of carbon dioxide while killing the very plants that would have absorbed it; one careless fire can burn a whole district's farms.
- **Form a fire volunteer squad and cut fire belts** round the farms and the woodlot before the dry season, and agree with the chief on when, if ever, burning is allowed (1 mark).
- **Educate and warn those most likely to start fires** - hunters, herders, charcoal burners, children and farmers clearing land - and **report offenders to the chief, the assembly or the Fire Service** so that the law is actually applied (1 mark).
- Also accept: **use the cleared weeds as compost or mulch instead of burning them**, and **agree on by-laws and fines through the assembly**.
  - *Marking note: 3 marks: 1 for the reason, 1 each for TWO practical community actions. 'The government should arrest them' is not a community action; 'we report offenders so that they are arrested' is.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- This paper covers both indicators of the sub-strand, so it may be used as an end-of-topic test for the whole of Climate Change and Green Economy.
- Question 4(a) is the item that separates the class. Most candidates can define climate change; far fewer can keep adaptation and mitigation apart. Teach the simple test - adaptation deals with the effects, mitigation deals with the cause - and mark strictly against it.
- In Question 2(e) reward any candidate who sees that one station is not enough. That is the competency CP 5.1, combining information from several sources, and it is worth more than another list of signs.
- Award the method marks in Question 2(b) even where the arithmetic is wrong, but insist on the unit °C in the final answer.

- In Question 6(b), give particular credit for providing that the trees be cared for AFTER the planting day. A tree planted and abandoned is not a mitigation measure.
