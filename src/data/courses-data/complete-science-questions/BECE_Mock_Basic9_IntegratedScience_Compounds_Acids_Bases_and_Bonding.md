### Question 1 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- **Element** - a substance that cannot be split into two or more simpler substances by an ordinary chemical change; it is made of only one kind of atom (1 mark).
- **Compound** - a substance formed when two or more elements combine chemically in a fixed proportion by mass, and whose properties differ from those of the elements in it (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Award the mark for the idea, not for the exact wording. Do not award a mark where a candidate says a compound is 'two things mixed together'.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Iron - **Fe** (1 mark).
- Sodium - **Na** (1 mark).
- Copper - **Cu** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The second letter must be a small letter. Do not award the mark for FE, NA or CU, nor for the Latin name alone.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- The first dish holds a **mixture**; the black solid is a **compound** (1 mark).
- In the mixture the iron and the sulphur **keep their own properties**; in iron(II) sulphide the properties are **new and different** from those of iron and of sulphur (1 mark).
- The mixture can be made in **any proportion** and the parts **separated by physical means** (a magnet); the compound has a **fixed proportion** by mass and can be broken down only by a chemical change (1 mark).
- Any other correct difference, for example that **heat is given out** when the compound is formed but not when the two are merely stirred (1 mark, maximum 3).
  - *Marking note: Maximum 3 marks. A difference must be written as a comparison; a statement about only one of the two dishes scores nothing.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Test:** pass a magnet, wrapped in a piece of paper or cling film, over the material in the dish (1 mark).
- **Result:** the grey **iron filings are attracted** and are lifted away, leaving the yellow sulphur behind, showing that the two substances are only mixed and not chemically combined (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept adding carbon disulphide or warm water as alternative separation tests only where the expected result is correctly stated. The magnet test is the expected answer.*

- Any correct household example: **water**, **table salt (common salt)**, **quicklime**, **magnesium oxide**, **hydrogen chloride** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Do not award the example mark for marble, baking soda or a fertiliser, because each of these contains more than two elements.*

- Universal indicator gives a **different colour at each pH**, which is matched against a chart to read a **pH value** (1 mark).
- It therefore shows **how strong or how weak** the acid or alkali is, while litmus shows only **whether** the solution is acidic or alkaline (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The second mark depends on the comparison with litmus being stated.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- They have a **bitter taste** (1 mark).
- They **feel soapy or slippery** between the fingers (1 mark).
- They **turn red litmus paper blue** (1 mark).
- They **neutralise acids** to form a salt and water (1 mark).
- The soluble ones (alkalis) give solutions with a **pH greater than 7** (1 mark, maximum 3).
  - *Marking note: Maximum 3 marks, one for each correct property.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **Acid:** ethanoic acid (in vinegar) used in cooking and for preserving food, **or** citric acid (in lime, lemon or orange) used in drinks; the name and the use together earn 1 mark.
- **Salt:** sodium chloride (table salt) used to season food, to preserve fish or meat, or to make oral rehydration solution; the name and the use together earn 1 mark.
  - *Marking note: A name without a use, or a use without a name, scores nothing for that half of the question.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Neutralisation** (1 mark).
- (ii) **acid + base -> salt + water** (1 mark).
- (iii) **Shake a sample of the soil with water and test it with universal indicator** (or a soil-testing kit) and find that the pH has risen towards 7 (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (ii) accept 'alkali' for 'base'. In (iii) accept improved crop growth only where the pupil also mentions testing the soil.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- The burning feeling is caused by **too much acid (hydrochloric acid) in the stomach** (1 mark).
- The tablet contains a **base (for example magnesium hydroxide or a carbonate), which neutralises the excess acid**, forming a salt and water and so reducing the acidity (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The second mark requires the word neutralise or a clear statement that the base cancels the acid.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- X - **2, 8, 1** (1 mark).
- Y - **2, 8, 7** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 2.8.1 and 2.8.7. Award no mark where the shells are written in the wrong order.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- X has **1 electron** in its outermost shell; Y has **7 electrons** in its outermost shell (1 mark for both).
- **X loses its one outer electron** and is left with a complete outer shell, becoming a **positively charged ion** (1 mark).
- **Y gains one electron** to complete its outer shell of eight, becoming a **negatively charged ion** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept the ions written as X+ and Y-. The idea of a complete or stable outer shell must appear for the second and third marks.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Ionic bond** (electrovalent bond) (1 mark).
- (ii) **XY** (1 mark).
- (iii) One electron is **transferred from X to Y**, giving X+ and Y- ions, and the **opposite charges attract each other strongly**, holding the ions together (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (ii) do not accept X2Y or XY2. In (iii) the words transfer and attraction (or their clear equivalents) are both needed.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- It is a **crystalline solid at room temperature** (1 mark).
- It has a **high melting point and boiling point** (1 mark).
- It **dissolves in water** (1 mark).
- It **conducts electricity when molten or dissolved in water, but not when solid** (1 mark, maximum 2).
  - *Marking note: Maximum 2 marks, one for each correct property.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **Ionic:** formed when **electrons are transferred** from a metal atom to a non-metal atom; example **sodium chloride** (magnesium oxide, calcium chloride) (1 mark).
- **Covalent:** formed when **electrons are shared** between non-metal atoms; example **water** (carbon(IV) oxide, hydrogen chloride) (1 mark).
- **Metallic:** formed by the **attraction between the positive metal ions and the free (mobile) electrons** around them; example **copper** (iron, aluminium) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Each row earns its mark only when both the formation and a correct example are given.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Dry cells or a battery, connecting wires, a bulb in a holder (or an ammeter), two carbon electrodes and a beaker or dish** (1 mark for a workable list).
- (ii) **Connect the cell, the bulb and the two electrodes in series, leaving a gap between the electrodes**; place the dry salt in the dish and press the two electrodes into it, and observe the bulb (1 mark).
- (ii) **Clean the electrodes, dissolve the salt in water in the beaker, dip the two electrodes into the solution without letting them touch each other**, and again observe the bulb (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept an ammeter or a torch bulb as the detector. A plan that does not keep everything else the same in the two tests earns at most 1 mark in (ii).*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- In the **dry solid the ions are held firmly in fixed positions** in the crystal and cannot move, so no current flows (1 mark).
- When the salt **dissolves, the ions become free to move** and carry the charge through the solution, completing the circuit and lighting the bulb (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Do not award a mark for 'water conducts electricity'; the moving ions must be mentioned.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- This is the first paper of the Basic 9 series and it covers the whole of Strand 1, Sub-Strand 1: Materials, which the syllabus writes as two content standards - B9/JHS3.1.1.1 (binary compounds, acids, bases and salts) and B9/JHS3.1.1.2 (atomic bonding).
- Candidates should be reminded before the examination that a chemical symbol begins with a capital letter and that any second letter is small. Many marks are lost each year on NA, CU and MG.
- Question 2(d) is the most demanding item in Section B. Candidates who cross the valencies mechanically without checking the result will write NaO or MgCl; insist on the correct formula.
- In Question 3, Fig. 1 carries no words such as 'acid' or 'alkali', so candidates must read the pH numbers and apply what they know about the scale.
- In Question 5, the letters X and Y are not chemical symbols. Candidates who answer in terms of sodium and chlorine have reasoned correctly, but the formula asked for is XY; award the marks where the chemistry is right and the letters are used consistently.
- Question 6(b) is a design task. Reward a workable plan rather than a textbook description; a candidate who tests the dry salt and the solution with the same circuit has met the requirement.
- Teachers should follow up this paper with practical work: making a pH scale model with universal indicator and testing lime juice, soap solution, rainwater and well water from the pupils' own community.
