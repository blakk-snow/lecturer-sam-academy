## MARKING SCHEME - SECTION B (60 marks)

*General rules: (1) Accept any other correct and relevant answer. (2) An energy change must show both the starting form and the final form; a single form earns no mark. (3) Accept 'mechanical energy' for 'kinetic energy' where the body is clearly moving. (4) Where a candidate gives more answers than are asked for, mark only the first ones up to the number required. (5) Do not deduct marks for spelling unless the meaning is changed.)*

### Question 1 (9 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- Energy conversion is the **changing of energy from one form into another form** (1 mark),
- for example **electrical energy changing into heat energy in a kettle**; the energy is **not destroyed - it only changes its form** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. The second mark may be earned either by a correct example or by the point that the energy is not destroyed.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- (i) **Electrical energy to heat energy** (1 mark)
- (ii) **Chemical energy to electrical energy to light energy** (accept chemical to light) (1 mark)
- (iii) **Electrical energy to sound energy** (1 mark)
- (iv) **Light (solar) energy to chemical energy** (1 mark)
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each (4 marks). Both the starting form and the final form must be given; a single form earns no mark. In (ii) accept the two-step or the three-step chain.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Heat energy** - from the cooking stove or the coal pot.
- **Light energy** - from the electric lamp or the lantern.
- **Sound energy** - from the radio or the television (accept electrical energy at the socket, or chemical energy in food and dry cells).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for each correct pairing of a form of energy with a place where it is used or produced (3 marks). A form named without a place, or a place without a form, earns no mark.*

### Question 2 (12 marks)

**(a)** [1 marks]

- **Potential energy** (gravitational potential energy), because the water is stored at a high level
  - *Marking note: 1 mark. Do NOT accept kinetic energy; the stored water is not yet moving.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- The **dam wall holds back the river**, so that a large volume of water is **stored at a high level** in the reservoir (1 mark).
- The water is released and **rushes down the penstock**, so its potential energy changes into **kinetic energy** (1 mark).
- The fast-moving water **strikes the blades of the turbine and makes it spin** (1 mark).
- The spinning turbine **drives the generator through the shaft**, and the generator **produces electricity**, which is carried away by the transmission lines (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for each correct step in the right order (4 marks). This is the curriculum exemplar on the processes a dammed river goes through to produce electricity. A candidate who merely writes 'water turns turbine, turbine turns generator' earns a maximum of 2 marks.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- (i) **Kinetic energy of the moving water to mechanical (kinetic) energy of the turbine** (1 mark)
- (ii) **Mechanical (kinetic) energy to electrical energy** (1 mark)
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each (2 marks). Both forms must be stated in each case.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- The **higher the water is stored above the turbine, the greater its potential energy** (1 mark).
- As it falls, this becomes a **greater kinetic energy**, so the water **strikes the turbine with greater force and more electricity is produced** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. The link between the height, the energy and the output must be made; 'so that the water can flow' alone earns 1 mark at most.*

**(e)** [2 marks]

- The **water is renewable and free** once the dam has been built, so the **running cost is low** and no fuel has to be bought.
- **No fuel is burnt**, so there is **no smoke or air pollution** (accept: the reservoir also provides fish, water for irrigation and transport).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO (2 marks).*

**(f)** [1 marks]

- **People living in the valley lose their homes and farmland and have to be resettled** (accept: the still water breeds mosquitoes and bilharzia snails; the flow downstream is reduced, affecting farming and fishing).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for any one sound problem.*

  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. 'Sound' is not accepted; a lamp gives out negligible sound.*

**(e)** [1 marks]

- The village obtains **light without waiting to be connected to the national grid**; the sunshine is **free and plentiful**, there is **no fuel to buy and no smoke**, and it is safer than a kerosene lamp.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for any one sound advantage.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- The **moving air (wind) strikes the blades of the wind turbine and makes them spin** (1 mark).
- The spinning blades **turn a shaft that is connected to a generator** (1 mark).
- The generator **changes the mechanical energy into electrical energy**, which is stored in batteries or sent out along cables (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for each correct step in the right order (3 marks).*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Animal dung is mixed with water and fed into a closed tank (digester)** from which air is excluded (1 mark).
- **Bacteria break down the dung**, and the **gas produced collects at the top of the digester and is piped off** (1 mark).
- Use: the gas is **burnt for cooking or for lighting** (accept: to run a small generator; the slurry left behind is used as manure) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks: 2 marks for the process and 1 mark for a use. The exclusion of air or the action of bacteria must appear for the second process mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- It **puts natural energy into a form people can actually use** - for lighting, cooking, pumping water, transport and industry.
- It **saves money and fuel and supports schools, clinics and businesses**, and reduces the cutting of firewood and the pollution that comes from burning fuels.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO sound reasons (2 marks).*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- Our **lamps, machines and appliances are made to work on particular forms of energy**, usually electrical energy or heat energy (1 mark).
- Sunlight, wind and flowing water are **not in those forms**, so a device such as a solar panel, a turbine or a generator **must first convert them** before the energy can be used (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each.*

### Question 5 (9 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Kinetic (mechanical) energy to heat energy** (1 mark)
- (ii) **Potential energy to kinetic energy** (1 mark)
- (iii) **Chemical energy (in the boy) to kinetic energy to electrical energy to light energy** (accept kinetic to electrical to light) (1 mark)
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each (3 marks). Both forms must appear in each answer.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **No device changes all the energy supplied to it into the useful form**; some always escapes to the surroundings, mostly as **heat from friction or from the resistance of the wires** (1 mark).
- Example: a **filament bulb becomes very hot** while it is giving light, so part of the electrical energy supplied leaves as heat (accept any suitable example) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 mark for the explanation and 1 mark for a suitable example.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Switch off lights, fans and the television when nobody is using them**, and **use energy-saving (LED) bulbs**.
- **Cover pots while cooking** and use the correct size of saucepan, dry clothes in the sun, and keep appliances well maintained.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO practical ways (2 marks).*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- The LED **changes a much greater part of the electrical energy supplied into light** and **wastes far less of it as heat** (1 mark).
- For the **same brightness it therefore uses less electricity**, so the cells last longer and the electricity bill is lower (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. A candidate who writes only that the LED 'is better' or 'is modern' earns no mark.*

### Question 6 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Cut the cardboard into vanes (blades) and fix them evenly round a cork or a cut plastic bottle** to form the wheel.
- **Push the knitting needle through the centre to act as the axle** and rest its ends on two supports (for example two tins) so that the wheel spins freely.
- **Tie one end of the string to the axle and the small load to the other end**, so the string winds on to the axle as the wheel turns.
- **Pour water steadily on to the vanes from a height and observe** the wheel turning and the load being lifted; **repeat with a faster flow and record** what happens.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR workable steps in a sensible order (4 marks). The making of the wheel, the axle, the load and the testing must all appear for full marks.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- A clear sketch showing the **wheel with its vanes and the axle resting on the supports** (1 mark).
- The **string wound on the axle and carrying the load** shown correctly (1 mark).
- The **water being poured from above on to the vanes**, with **all the parts labelled** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks as shown. Award the marks for a workable arrangement, however roughly drawn; an unlabelled diagram scores a maximum of 1 mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- The **potential energy of the water held above changes into kinetic energy** as the water falls on to the vanes (1 mark).
- This **turns the wheel (mechanical energy)**, which **lifts the load, so the energy becomes potential energy stored in the raised load** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. Accept a correctly written chain such as 'potential to kinetic to mechanical to potential'.*

**(d)** [1 marks]

- **Handle the knitting needle and the cutting tool carefully, keeping the sharp ends away from others** (accept: mop up spilt water at once so that nobody slips; keep water away from electrical sockets).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for any one sound precaution. 'Be careful' on its own earns no mark.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- Sub-Strand 1 (Energy) has three content standards at Basic 8. This paper covers B8/JHS2.4.1.1 only. Renewable and non-renewable sources (B8/JHS2.4.1.2) and heat and temperature (B8/JHS2.4.1.3) are examined in the companion paper, so do not penalise candidates for omitting those topics here.
- Insist on the two-sided form of an energy change. 'Heat energy' alone is not an energy change; 'electrical energy to heat energy' is. This rule is printed in the instructions and applied throughout the marking scheme.
- Accept 'mechanical energy' wherever 'kinetic energy' is expected for a moving part such as the turbine or the water-wheel.
- Question 2(b) is the curriculum exemplar on the processes a dammed river goes through. Mark the four steps separately and in order; a two-line answer naming only the turbine and the generator cannot earn more than half the marks.
- In Question 3(a)(iii) the battery stores energy as chemical energy. Candidates who write 'electrical energy' have missed the storage step; award the mark only where they have shown that they understand the storage.
- Question 5(b) requires both an explanation and an example. Many candidates give one without the other.
- Question 6 is a practical task. If your class actually built the model, mark their own written account and drawing, and let the groups demonstrate the wheel lifting the load.
- The two figures are hand-drawn sketches, in the style pupils are expected to produce in their own answers; redraw them on the chalkboard if you wish.
