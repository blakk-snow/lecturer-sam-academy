- **Strain the liquid through a cloth before pouring it into the sprayer** (accept adding a little soap to make it stick) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The straining step is essential for the second mark.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- Roofing - **grass stems and palm fronds (leaves)** (1 mark).
- Stool - **the trunk (woody stem) of a tree** (1 mark).
- Basket - **stems, leaf strips or fibres of raffia, cane or palm** (1 mark).
- Herbal medicine - **bark, leaves or roots** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The table must be copied; four correct entries earn the marks.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **cheap and locally available**; **easily worked with simple tools and local skills**; **keep buildings cool**; **easily repaired or replaced**; **provide work and income locally**; **renewable and they rot away harmlessly** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: Three distinct reasons; repeated ideas score once.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- (i) **They are harvested faster than they grow back - whole trees felled, bark ringed, young plants cut** (accept bush fires or clearing of land) (1 mark).
- (ii) Any TWO of: **take only mature parts and never ring the bark**; **plant and care for new seedlings or keep a protected grove**; **protect the area from bush fire**; **agree community rules or a closed season** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: A measure must act on the cause given in (i).*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Banana or plantain leaves** (accept palm leaf strips) (1 mark); **softened, rolled into a cup, tied at the side and filled with soil** (1 mark).
- (ii) **It rots away, so the seedling can be planted without disturbing the roots and no plastic is left in the soil** (accept: it costs nothing) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Any workable description of making the pot earns the second mark.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- (i) Any ONE of **raffia palm, oil palm frond, sisal, baobab or kapok bark, banana or plantain stem** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Strip or beat out the long fibres** (1 mark); **soak, wash and dry them in the sun** (1 mark); **twist two or three strands together, adding fibre to lengthen the rope** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The three steps must be in a sensible order.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Hang a known load, heavier than the frame requires, from the rope** (1 mark); **it passes if it holds the load without breaking or stretching much** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Remake it with more or thicker strands, twist it more tightly or use a stronger fibre, and test it again** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A test without a stated result earns one mark only.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) Any ONE of **raffia, straw, palm leaf, screw pine leaf** (1 mark).
- (ii) Any TWO, each with a reason: **split into strips of even width** - the weave is regular (1 mark); **dried in the sun** - the material becomes tough and will not rot or shrink (1 mark); **soaked or softened** - the strips bend without cracking (1 mark). Maximum 2 marks.
  - *Marking note: No mark for a step given without its reason.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **the plant and part used and its source**; **the steps followed, with a labelled drawing**; **the tools used and the time taken**; **the difficulties met and how they were solved**; **how the article performed when it was used**; **the cost and how the material will be replaced** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: Three distinct items are required.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Reason: **felling a young tree kills it before it has produced seed, and the whole tree is lost for one small article** (1 mark).
- Instead: **take small patches of bark from a mature tree, or use fallen branches, dry fronds and leaves, and plant a seedling to replace what is taken** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The idea of sustainable harvesting must appear in the second mark.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- This sub-strand looks simple and is often answered carelessly. The marks are for linking a NAMED plant part to a NAMED use; 'the plant is useful' and 'we use the tree' earn nothing at all.
- Candidates must keep the two sides of the indicator apart. Planting material, tools, animal housing, animal feed, soil improvement and pest control are the agricultural uses; herbal medicine, building, furniture, artefacts, ceremonies and education are the non-agricultural ones.
- In Question 1 the lettering runs A to E and in Question 3 it runs W to Z, so that no letter is used twice in the paper. Candidates should answer with the letters given.
- Question 3(c) rewards classes that have actually made a neem extract. The straining step is the one most often forgotten, and a sprayer blocked by leaf pieces is the commonest failure in the field.
- The second indicator is a making indicator. A written paper can test only the plan, the materials, the steps, the test and the care of the plant, and those are what Questions 5 and 6 examine; the making itself must still be done in school.
- Questions 4(c) and 6(c) carry the conservation message of the whole strand. Harvesting mature parts, never ringing the bark of a tree and replacing what is taken are the three ideas candidates should be able to state without hesitation.
- Follow-up work: let each group collect, press and label the parts of five local plants, write beside each part one agricultural and one non-agricultural use, and then make one article - a rope, a set of leaf pots, a mat or a basket - test it in use and report on it. The display becomes a record of the whole sub-strand.
