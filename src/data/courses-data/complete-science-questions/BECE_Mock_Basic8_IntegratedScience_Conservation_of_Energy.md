### Question 1 (9 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- **Energy can neither be created nor destroyed** (1 mark);
- it can only be **changed from one form into another**, so the **total energy before a change equals the total energy after it** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. The first mark needs both 'not created' and 'not destroyed'; the second needs the change of form or the equality of the totals.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- Energy conservation means **using energy carefully and avoiding waste** in the home, the school and the workplace (1 mark);
- so that **less fuel and electricity are used, costs are lower and the supply lasts longer** for the future (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. Do NOT accept the law of conservation of energy as the answer here; the question asks for the everyday meaning.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Energy conversion** is the **changing of energy from one form into another**, for example electrical energy into light in a lamp (1 mark).
- **Energy conservation** is **using energy carefully so that none is wasted**, for example switching the lamp off when it is not needed (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark for each side of the difference. A candidate who explains only one of the two terms scores 1 mark.*

**(d)** [3 marks]

- It **puts energy into the form we actually need** - light for reading, heat for cooking, movement for transport and machines (1 mark).
- It makes the **free natural energy of the Sun, the wind and flowing water useful** to people (1 mark).
- It **powers industry, hospitals, schools and communication**, so it supports work, education, health and the economy (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE sound points (3 marks). This answers the curriculum exemplar on classifying the importance of energy conversion in daily life.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- Wasted form: **heat energy (90 J)**, which escapes to the surroundings (1 mark)
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **10 J + 90 J = 100 J**, which is **exactly the energy supplied** to the lamp (1 mark).
- This shows that **no energy has been created or destroyed**: all of it has simply been **changed into other forms**, as the law of conservation of energy states (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. The second mark needs the idea of conservation; simply writing '100 J' earns 1 mark.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **(10 ÷ 100) × 100** (1 mark)
- **= 10%** (1 mark)
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 mark for the method and 1 mark for the answer with the per cent sign.*

**(e)** [2 marks]

- An LED lamp **turns a much greater part of the electrical energy into light and wastes far less as heat** (1 mark).
- The family therefore gets the **same brightness for much less electricity**, so the **bill is lower and less fuel is burnt at the power station** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **Burning fuels releases smoke and harmful gases** that pollute the air and cause coughs and chest diseases (1 mark).
- The **carbon dioxide released adds to global warming and changes the climate** (1 mark).
- **Cutting trees for firewood and charcoal destroys forests and causes soil erosion** (accept: dams flood farmland and displace people; oil spills pollute rivers and kill fish; generators make noise) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE sound points (3 marks).*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Burning coal, petrol, diesel and gas **releases large amounts of carbon dioxide into the atmosphere** (1 mark).
- The carbon dioxide **traps heat that the Earth would otherwise send back into space** (1 mark).
- As more of the gas collects, the **Earth becomes warmer and the climate changes** - unreliable rains, floods and droughts (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks, 1 mark each. Candidates need not use the term 'greenhouse effect', but the trapping of heat must be described for the second mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- The **forest cover is lost, so the soil is exposed and washed away by the rain (erosion)**, and **animals lose their habitats**.
- There are **fewer trees to take in carbon dioxide and give out oxygen**, and the **rainfall of the area may fall, so streams dry up**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO effects (2 marks).*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Less fuel is burnt**, so there is **less air pollution and less carbon dioxide** released.
- **Fewer trees are cut for firewood and charcoal**, and **the fuels and other resources last longer for future generations** (accept: fewer new power stations and dams are needed).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO benefits (2 marks).*

### Question 5 (9 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Switch off lights, fans and computers when the classroom or office is empty**, and at the close of school.
- **Use daylight in the classrooms** instead of switching on lamps during the day.
- **Fit energy-saving (LED) bulbs** and repair faulty switches and appliances.
- **Cook for the whole school at one time in covered pots**, and use an improved stove that needs less firewood.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR practical ways (4 marks).*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **Less electricity, gas and charcoal are used, so the bills and the fuel bought each month cost less** (1 mark).
- **Appliances that are switched off and well maintained last longer**, so less money is spent on repairs and replacements (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **The habit:** leaving the **television, fan or security lights on when nobody needs them** (1 mark).
- **Why it wastes energy:** the appliance goes on **changing electrical energy into light, sound and heat that nobody uses**, and the bill rises (1 mark).
- **The correction:** **switch it off at the socket when it is not in use**, or fit a timer or sensor so that the lights come on only at night (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks as shown: 1 mark for naming a genuine habit, 1 mark for explaining the waste and 1 mark for a workable correction.*

  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR suitable items (4 marks). This answers the curriculum exemplar that asks pupils to make a poster presentation on their findings.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

  - *Marking note: 3 marks as shown. Award the marks for a sensible, well-planned layout; artistic quality is not being marked. A plan with no labels scores a maximum of 1 mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Two sources: **science textbooks and the school library**, and the **internet** (accept newspapers, radio and television, the school's own electricity bills, or an officer from the electricity company) (1 mark for any two).

  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 mark for naming two sources and 1 mark for a sound way of checking reliability. This answers the exemplar on searching multimedia sources for information.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- This paper is where the formal law of conservation of energy is examined. It was deliberately kept out of the paper on energy conversion (B8/JHS2.4.1.1), where it was referred to only in words.
- Keep the two senses of the word 'conservation' apart. Question 1(a) asks for the scientific law; Question 1(b) asks for the everyday meaning, which is the careful use of energy. Many candidates will give the law twice - mark 1(b) strictly.
- Question 3(c) is the key link between the two senses: the 10 J of light plus the 90 J of heat make exactly the 100 J supplied, which is the law in action.
