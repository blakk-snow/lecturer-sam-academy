### Question 1 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- (i) The **quantity of feed, made up of different ingredients, given to an animal in one day to meet its needs** (1 mark).
- (ii) The animal **grows fast and stays healthy and produces more meat, milk or eggs**; feed is not wasted and disease is reduced (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'the daily feed allowance of an animal' in (i).*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- Maize - **carbohydrate (energy)** (1 mark).
- Fish meal - **protein** (1 mark).
- Oyster shell - **minerals, chiefly calcium** (1 mark).
- Premix - **vitamins and trace minerals** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'energy food' for maize and 'body-building food' for fish meal.*

**(c)** [4 marks]

- **Weigh out each ingredient** in the correct proportion for 100 kg (1 mark).
- On a **clean, dry floor spread the ingredient of the largest quantity (the maize meal) first** and the smaller ones on top (1 mark).
- **Mix the premix and the salt with a little maize meal first** so that the small quantity spreads evenly through the heap (1 mark).
- **Turn the heap over with a shovel several times until the mixture is uniform**, then bag it, label it with the date and store it in a cool dry place on pallets (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Marks are for the steps, not for the order alone; a candidate who mixes the premix directly into the whole heap loses the third mark.*

**(a)** [3 marks]

**(d)** [3 marks]

- The pig has a **simple (one-chambered) stomach and no rumen with micro-organisms**, so it **cannot digest the cellulose** in the grass (1 mark).
- Most of the grass therefore **passes out undigested** and the animal gets too little energy and protein, so it remains thin (1 mark).
- He should feed a **concentrate ration of maize, bran and a protein source such as fish meal or soya bean meal** (with clean water), using greens only as a small part of the feed (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The first mark requires the idea of cellulose or fibre.*

### Question 3 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **2.4 kg** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Ration M** (1 mark).
- (iii) **2.4 kg - 1.2 kg = 1.2 kg** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The unit kg is required in (i) and (iii).*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) **The higher the protein content of the ration, the greater the average weight of the birds** (1 mark).
- (ii) Ration M supplied **too little protein, which is needed to build muscle and other body tissue**, so the birds grew slowly (1 mark).
- (iii) **Broilers should be fed a ration high in protein (about 20%) if they are to grow well** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: In (i) accept 'weight increases as protein increases'.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Whether the **extra weight gained is worth more than the extra money spent on the feed** - that is, the profit per bird (1 mark).
- The **price the market pays for heavier birds**, the **quantity of feed needed per kilogramme of meat**, or whether he can **mix a ration of the same quality more cheaply from local ingredients** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Two distinct considerations are needed.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- The **same breed, age and number of chicks** in each group (1 mark).
- The **same housing, space, water, vaccination and length of time** for all three groups (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Any two distinct factors earn the marks.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Young, growing and mature (adult)** animals (1 mark for all three).
- (ii) **Colostrum** (1 mark).
- (ii) It contains **antibodies that protect the young animal against disease** (accept: it is rich in protein and vitamins) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: No mark in (i) unless all three stages are given.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- Day-old to four weeks - **chick (starter) mash** (1 mark).
- Nutrient - **protein** (accept vitamins) for rapid growth (1 mark).
- Laying hen - **layer mash** (1 mark).
- Nutrient - **calcium** for the shells of the eggs (accept protein) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'chick starter' and 'layers' mash' as written.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- (i) The eggs will have **thin, soft shells that break easily**, because the ration is **low in calcium** (1 mark).
- (i) The **number of eggs laid will fall**, and the birds may peck the shells or become weak in the legs (1 mark).
- (ii) He should **change the birds to a layer ration** and may also offer **oyster shell or limestone grit** separately (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'he is wasting money' as an extra point only, not for the mark.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Wash the feeders and drinkers and provide clean, fresh water** first thing in the morning (1 mark).
- **Weigh out the day's ration (a grower or finisher mash) and share it among enough feeders** so that every bird can reach one (1 mark).
- **Observe the birds while they feed and remove any sick or weak bird**; remove wet, stale or soiled feed (1 mark).
- **Top up feed and water in the evening and record the quantity of feed given** in the farm record book (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who only writes 'give them feed and water' earns 1 mark.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **Wash the feeders and drinkers regularly** and keep the area around them clean (1 mark).
- **Give clean, fresh drinking water at all times** (1 mark).
- **Never feed mouldy, stale or soiled feed**, and store feed in a cool, dry, rat-proof store (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Maximum 3 marks; accept any three correct hygiene rules.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **No, the suggestion should not be accepted** (the position must be clear) (1 mark).
- **Reason 1:** growing broilers must be **fed every day (feed available for most of the day)**, otherwise growth stops, the birds lose weight and the flock becomes uneven (1 mark).
- **Reason 2:** feed that has been wet **grows mould, which produces poisons that can make the birds sick or kill them**, so it must be thrown away (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who agrees with the suggestion cannot earn the reason marks.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) **50 x 0.12 kg = 6 kg** per day (1 mark).
- (ii) **6 kg x 7 = 42 kg** per week (1 mark).
- (iii) **42 x GH¢ 6.50 = GH¢ 273.00** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Award the later marks on a wrong figure carried forward, provided the working is correct.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- To **know how much feed is used, work out the cost of production and see whether the farm is making a profit** (1 mark).
- To **notice at once when the animals eat less (an early sign of disease)**, to order feed in good time and to compare one ration or batch with another (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Two distinct reasons are required.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Mix their own ration from local ingredients** (maize, bran, palm kernel cake, fish meal) instead of buying ready-made feed (1 mark).
- **Buy ingredients in bulk at harvest time, when prices are low, and store them dry and rat-proof** so that none is lost (1 mark).
- **Reduce wastage: use well-designed feeders, fill them only part full and raise them to the level of the birds' backs**, and control rats and wild birds (1 mark).

### EXAMINER NOTES

- Sub-Strand 4 for Basic 9 is a practical sub-strand: the four indicators ask pupils to prepare feed, to select feed for ruminants, monogastrics and poultry, to match feed to the stage of growth, and actually to feed animals on a farm. Questions 1, 5 and 6 should therefore follow real work on the school farm.
- In Question 2 the examiner is looking for the link between the structure of the stomach and the kind of feed the animal can use. Candidates who name the four compartments but cannot say why a ruminant copes with grass have missed the point of the indicator.
- In Question 3 the unit kg must be written. Readings within one small square are accepted, so 2.3 to 2.5 kg is correct for ration L.
- Question 4(b) is a table item. A ration name without the nutrient (or the nutrient without the ration) earns only the mark for the part given.
- In Question 6(a) the method marks stand even where a candidate makes an arithmetic slip, provided the working is shown. The prices used are illustrative; teachers should replace them with current local prices when re-using the question.
- Feed names vary from district to district. Accept 'chick starter', 'chick mash' or 'broiler starter' for the first four weeks, and 'layers' mash' or 'layer ration' for hens in lay.
- Follow-up work: let the class formulate and mix a small quantity of feed (about 10 kg) for the school's birds, weigh it out, record what the birds eat for two weeks and work out the cost per kilogramme of weight gained.
