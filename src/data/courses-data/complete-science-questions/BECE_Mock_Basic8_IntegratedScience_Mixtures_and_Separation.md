### Question 1 (12 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- A mixture is a combination of **two or more substances** that are **not chemically joined together**.
- The substances in a mixture **keep their own properties** and can be **separated by physical means** (filtration, evaporation, sieving, etc.).
- The substances of a mixture can be mixed in **any ratio**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct points (2 marks). Award 1 mark for ‘two or more substances mixed together’ only if the idea that they are not chemically combined is present. Do NOT accept ‘two things put together’ on its own.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- In a **mixture** the substances are **not chemically joined**; in a **compound** the elements are **chemically joined**.
- A mixture can be **separated by physical means**; a compound can only be separated by **chemical means**.
- The parts of a mixture **keep their own properties**; a compound has **new properties** different from its elements.
- The parts of a mixture can be mixed in **any ratio**; the elements of a compound combine in a **fixed ratio**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct differences (2 marks). The difference must be stated as a comparison, not as a description of only one of them. Do NOT accept ‘a compound is harder’ or answers that describe the appearance alone.*

- B - **colloid**
- C - **suspension**
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each (3 marks). All three must be correctly matched to A, B and C; a correct list in the wrong order earns no mark.*

**(d)** [1 marks]

- The **Tyndall effect** (accept ‘the scattering of light’).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for the correct name.*

**(e)** [1 marks]

  - *Marking note: 1 mark for any correct example. Do NOT accept salt solution, sea water or muddy water - these are not colloids.*

### Question 4 (12 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- I - **thermometer**
- II - **round-bottom flask**
- III - **Liebig condenser**
- IV - **beaker** (accept ‘receiver’ or ‘conical flask’)
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each (4 marks). Accept ‘flask’ for II and ‘condenser’ for III. Do NOT accept ‘flask’ for IV or ‘test tube’ for IV.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- I (thermometer): **measures the temperature** of the vapour or liquid so that the boiling point can be checked (1 mark).
- III (condenser): **cools the vapour** and changes it back into a liquid - it condenses the vapour (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. The function must be linked to the correct part; a correct statement attached to the wrong letter earns no mark.*

**(c)** [1 marks]

- **Evaporation** (accept ‘vaporisation’).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark. If a candidate writes ‘boiling’, award 0 here but allow the mark in (d) if the reason given refers to the boiling point.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Ethanol** is collected first in IV (1 mark).
- Reason: ethanol has a **lower boiling point** (about 78 °C) than water (100 °C), so it vapourises first (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks. Award the second mark only if a comparison of boiling points is given. Do NOT accept ‘because ethanol is lighter’.*

**(e)** [1 marks]

- To **cool (condense) the vapour** into a liquid; also to prevent the vapour from escaping into the air.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for any one correct reason.*

**(f)** [1 marks]

- The mixture in the flask contains **two liquids**; the liquid in IV contains **only one** (pure) liquid.
- The mixture boils over a **range of temperatures**; the liquid in IV boils at a **fixed temperature**.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for any one correct difference. The answer must be a comparison, not a description of only one of them.*

**(g)** [1 marks]

- The water would **evaporate and escape** into the air instead of being collected, and the **salt would be left behind** in the flask.
- Distillation is used to recover the **liquid** from a mixture, not the solid; to obtain salt, **evaporation to dryness** should be used instead.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for any answer that identifies that the salt would be left in the flask and that evaporation is the correct method.*

### Question 5 (12 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- (i) Iron filings and sand - **magnetic separation**
- (ii) Ground pepper and groundnut soup - **filtration**
- (iii) Salt and water - **evaporation**
- (iv) Salt, sand and water - **filtration, then evaporation** (both must be named)
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each (4 marks). In (iv) both processes must be named for the mark. Do NOT accept ‘distillation’ for (iii) unless the candidate shows that the water is to be collected.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- (i) **Iron is magnetic** (attracted by a magnet) while sand is not, so the iron filings are picked out and the sand remains.
- (ii) **Pepper is insoluble** in the soup and its particles are large enough to be caught on the filter paper as the residue, while the soup passes through as the filtrate.
- (iii) **Salt is dissolved** in the water and cannot be caught by a filter; on heating, the water escapes as vapour and the salt is left behind.
- (iv) The sand is **insoluble**, so filtration removes it first; the salt is **dissolved**, so the filtrate must then be **evaporated** to recover the salt.
  - *Marking note: 2 marks each for any TWO correctly explained choices (4 marks): 1 mark for the correct reason and 1 mark for linking it clearly to the method chosen. A reason that would fit any method earns no mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Filtration:** pour the mixture through **filter paper in a funnel**. The **sand** is trapped as the residue and the salt solution passes through as the filtrate.
- **Evaporation:** pour the filtrate into an **evaporating dish** and heat gently (or leave it in the sun). The water evaporates and **salt crystals** are left behind. Dry the sand and the salt.
  - *Marking note: 2 marks: 1 mark for each stage correctly described, and the order must be filtration first. Accept ‘leave the filtrate in the sun’ instead of heating. Maximum 2 marks.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- Pour a little of the drink into a **clean, transparent glass** and shine a **torch or beam of light** through it (1 mark).
- In a **true solution** the path of the light is **not visible** and the drink stays clear; if it were a **suspension**, the light path would be **visible (scattered)** and **particles would settle** on standing (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks. Accept a ‘leave it to stand and observe whether particles settle’ test if it is fully described. Look for the comparison between what is seen in a solution and in a suspension.*
