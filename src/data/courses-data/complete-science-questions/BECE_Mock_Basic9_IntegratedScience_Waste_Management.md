### EXAMINER NOTES

- This sub-strand is easy to answer loosely. Marks are lost when candidates write 'it is bad' or 'it is dirty' instead of naming the effect - flooding, malaria, cholera, polluted water, dead fish. Train classes to finish every sentence with the consequence.
- Question 2 rewards careful reading of the figure. The lining, the daily soil cover, the gas vent and the leachate pipe are the four features that make a landfill 'sanitary', and each has a scientific reason behind it.
- In Question 3 the arithmetic is simple but the unit and the percentage sign are part of the answer. Candidates should also be reminded that the audit ran for six days, not seven, so the daily average is 10 kg and not about 8.6 kg.
- Question 4(c) tests the single most important principle in composting: decay with air is sweet and fast, decay without air is slow and foul. A class that has built and turned a heap answers this at once.
- Biogas is a mixture, but methane is the gas that burns and the one asked for. Accept 'biogas, which is mainly methane'; do not accept 'carbon dioxide'.
- Question 6 is a critique, and marks are given for balance. Candidates who can see both what works and what fails in their own town, and who then propose a practical improvement with a reason, are showing exactly the thinking the indicator asks for.
- Follow-up work: let the class run its own one-week waste audit, draw the bar chart, calculate the percentages, start a compost pit with the food waste and weigh the compost produced. Repeating the audit a term later gives a direct measure of how effective the school's new methods have been.
