### Question 1 (9 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- Electricity transmission is the **carrying of electrical energy from the power station where it is generated** (1 mark)
- **through cables, pylons, transformers and substations to the places where it is used** - homes, schools and factories (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- (i) **Step-up transformer** - it **raises the voltage** of the electricity leaving the power station (1 mark) **before it is sent along the transmission lines**, so that less energy is lost on the way (1 mark).
- (ii) **Pylon** - the **tall steel tower that carries the high-voltage cables high above the ground**, out of reach (1 mark).
- (iii) **Insulator** - it **holds the cable away from the metal pylon** so that the current cannot pass into the tower and down to earth (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 4 marks as shown. In (i), 'it changes the voltage' earns 1 mark only; the direction of the change must be stated.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- The cables have **resistance**, so some electrical energy is always **turned into heat in them** as the current flows (1 mark).
- For the same amount of power, a **higher voltage means a smaller current** flowing in the cables (1 mark).
- The heat lost depends on the size of the current, so the **smaller current wastes far less energy** over the long distance (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks, 1 mark each. No formula is required; award full marks for the reasoning 'higher voltage - smaller current - less heat lost'.*

  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each.*

**(c)** [1 marks]

- The current **falls steadily to zero**, so **no further current flows** in that branch of the circuit
  - *Marking note: 1 mark. 'The current stops' is acceptable.*

**(d)** [3 marks]

- The **LED glows brightly for a moment and then fades out** (1 mark).
- The **charged capacitor now acts as the source** and **drives a current through the LED as it discharges** (1 mark).
- As the **stored charge is used up the current falls**, so the light grows dimmer and finally goes out (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks, 1 mark each. This answers the curriculum exemplar on the discharging action of a capacitor. A candidate who says the LED stays on permanently earns no mark.*

### Question 5 (9 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- **Two metal plates placed close together but not touching** (1 mark),
- **separated by an insulating material (air, waxed paper, mica or plastic)**, with a connecting lead joined to each plate (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each. The insulator between the plates must be mentioned for the second mark.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- When the switch is closed, **charge flows on to one plate and away from the other**, so one plate becomes **negatively charged and the other positively charged** (1 mark).
- The **current is large at first and falls steadily as the charge builds up** on the plates (1 mark).
- Charging stops when the **voltage across the capacitor is equal to the voltage of the supply**; the current then falls to zero (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 3 marks, 1 mark each. No charge actually crosses the insulator between the plates; do not penalise a candidate who does not mention this, but credit it as an alternative point.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- When the supply is removed and the plates are **joined through a conducting path, the stored charge flows back round the circuit** (in the opposite direction) (1 mark).
- This **gives a current that is large at first and falls to zero** as the capacitor empties, lighting an LED briefly if one is in the path (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- **Storing charge to give the flash in a camera**, and **smoothing the supply in a radio, television or phone charger**.
- **Keeping the clock or memory of a device running briefly when the power goes off**, **starting the motor of a fan or refrigerator**, or **timing the flashing of a lamp** (accept any two).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct uses (2 marks).*

### Question 6 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- **Collect the components** - cell, switch, resistor, capacitor, LED and connecting wires - and **check that the cell is good**.
- **Connect the cell, the switch, the resistor and the capacitor in a loop**, joining the capacitor the correct way round (positive lead to the positive side).
- **Connect the LED across the capacitor**, with its longer leg towards the positive side, and **check every joint**.
- **Close the switch and observe the LED; then open the switch and observe it again**, recording what is seen each time.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR workable steps in a sensible order (4 marks). The correct way round of the LED or the capacitor, and the testing, should appear for full marks.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **On closing the switch**: the LED **flashes briefly and then goes out** as the capacitor charges up (1 mark).
- **On opening the switch**: the LED **glows again for a moment and fades away** as the capacitor discharges through it (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 2 marks, 1 mark each.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **Use only the low-voltage cell provided - never the mains supply** - and **do not short-circuit the cell**.
- **Connect the capacitor and the LED the correct way round**, **discharge the capacitor before handling the leads**, and switch off when the work is finished.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO sound precautions (2 marks). 'Be careful' on its own earns no mark.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- The **LED is connected the wrong way round**, so it cannot conduct (accept: the LED is damaged).
- The **cell is flat or wrongly connected, or a joint is loose so that the circuit is not complete** (accept: the resistor used is far too large).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO sensible faults (2 marks).*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- This paper covers both content standards of Sub-Strand 2, so it completes Electricity and Electronics at Basic 8. The blueprint shows the indicator against every item.
- In Question 1(b)(i) and in the objective items on transformers, insist on the direction of the change: a step-up transformer RAISES the voltage; 'it changes the voltage' is worth half the marks at most.
- Question 2(d) is the curriculum exemplar that asks for a flow chart from the point of generation to the point of consumption. Mark the stages and the direction of the arrows; the shape and neatness of the boxes do not matter.
- Questions 4 and 5 are the heart of the capacitor standard. The expected sequence is: switch closed - charging current large at first - LED flashes - current falls to zero as the capacitor becomes fully charged - cell removed - capacitor discharges through the LED, which glows and fades.
- A common error is to write that the capacitor 'produces' electricity like a cell. Credit only answers that make clear that a capacitor stores charge given to it, while a cell produces electrical energy from chemicals.
- In Question 6, if your class actually built the circuit, mark their own observations. A large capacitor (1000 microfarads or more) and a resistor of about 1 kilohm with a 1.5 V or 3 V supply give a flash that is easy to see.
- Safety: pupils must use only low-voltage cells for this work. Never allow mains voltage in a classroom circuit activity.
- The two figures are hand-drawn sketches, in the style pupils are expected to produce in their own answers; redraw them on the chalkboard if you wish.
