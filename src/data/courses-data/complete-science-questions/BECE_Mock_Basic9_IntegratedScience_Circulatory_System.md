- The sacs are **very many, giving a large surface area**, and each is **richly supplied with capillaries** carrying blood away all the time (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Any two distinct features earn the marks.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- The **oxygenated blood is carried to the heart and pumped through the arteries and capillaries to every cell**, where the oxygen is used in respiration (1 mark).
- The blood then **collects the carbon dioxide made in the cells and carries it back through the veins and the heart to the lungs** to be breathed out (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Both the outward and the return journey are needed for full marks.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) The **chemical breakdown of glucose in the cells, using oxygen, to release energy** (1 mark).
- (ii) **glucose + oxygen** on the left of the arrow (1 mark).
- (ii) **carbon dioxide + water + energy** on the right of the arrow (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'food' for glucose in (i) but not in the equation.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) Breathing is the **physical movement of air into and out of the lungs**; respiration is a **chemical reaction that releases energy** (1 mark).
- (i) Breathing takes place in the **breathing organs only**, while respiration takes place in **every living cell** (1 mark).
- (ii) For **movement of the muscles** (accept growth and repair, keeping the body warm, or the working of the organs) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Two distinct differences are needed in (i).*

**(c)** [4 marks]

- The **muscles were working hard and needed much more energy, so they respired faster** (1 mark).
- **Breathing became faster and deeper to take in more oxygen** (and to remove carbon dioxide at the lungs) (1 mark).
- The **heart beat faster to carry oxygen and glucose to the muscles and to carry carbon dioxide away** more quickly (1 mark).
- After the race the **muscles needed less energy and the extra carbon dioxide was removed**, so breathing and heart beat slowly returned to normal (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A candidate who writes only 'he was tired' earns no mark.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- (i) The **force with which the blood presses on the walls of the blood vessels** as the heart pumps (1 mark).
- (ii) A **sphygmomanometer** (1 mark).
- (iii) **Hypertension (high blood pressure)** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'BP apparatus' only where the candidate also writes sphygmomanometer.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- **Stroke** or bursting of a blood vessel in the brain (1 mark).
- **Heart attack, heart failure, kidney damage or damage to the eyes** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Two distinct problems are required; 'death' alone earns no mark.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- **Use less salt in food** - too much salt **raises the blood pressure** (1 mark).
- **Eat less fried and fatty food and more fruit and vegetables** - fat is **deposited in the arteries and narrows them** (1 mark).
- **Exercise regularly (about thirty minutes on most days), avoid smoking and heavy drinking, and check the blood pressure at the clinic** - this **strengthens the heart and finds trouble early** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Each mark requires both a piece of advice and a reason; advice without a reason earns no mark.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- This paper examines the whole of Sub-Strand 1 for Basic 9: the composition and functions of blood, the parts of the circulatory system, the diseases of the system, blood pressure, and the way the respiratory and circulatory systems complement each other.
- Question 2 is built on the curriculum exemplar that pupils should draw and label a longitudinal section of the mammalian heart. Teachers should let pupils draw the section themselves, in pencil, before the examination; a candidate who has drawn it finds the lettering easy.
- The letters on Fig. 1 follow the convention that the section is seen from the front, so the right side of the heart appears on the left of the drawing. Candidates who reverse A and C have not read the caption.
- In Question 4 the two gas arrows are numbered, not named, so that the figure does not give away the answer. Accept 'alveolus' or 'air sac' interchangeably.
- Question 5(a)(ii) is marked in two halves: one mark for the reactants and one for the products. An equation written with chemical formulae in place of words is accepted if correct.
- Question 6 deals with hypertension, which is common in Ghanaian communities. Marks in 6(c) are given only where advice is supported by a reason; this is what the curriculum means by describing ways of managing blood pressure.
- Follow-up work: invite a nurse from the nearest clinic to take the blood pressure and pulse rate of volunteers in the class, and let the pupils record and discuss the readings, taking care not to alarm or label any pupil.
