### Question 1 (10 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- (i) The **stage at which the crop, or the part of it that is wanted, is fully developed and ready to be harvested** (1 mark).
- (ii) The **husks and silk are dry and brown**, the leaves have turned yellow, and the **grains are hard** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'the grain is hard and dry' alone in (ii).*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- (i) **Germination -> seedling -> vegetative growth -> flowering -> maturity** (2 marks for the whole order correct; 1 mark where only one pair is out of place).
- (ii) The plant **produces leaves, stems and roots and grows larger**, making and storing food, but it does not yet flower (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: No mark in (i) where two or more pairs are out of order.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- Early maturing - **okro, cowpea, garden egg, lettuce or other leafy vegetable** (1 mark).
- Medium maturing - **maize, rice, groundnut or tomato** (1 mark).
- Late maturing - **cassava, yam, plantain, oil palm or cocoa** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept any locally grown crop placed in the right group; the grouping, not the particular crop, earns the mark.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- She should **count back the ten weeks from the date she needs the maize** and sow at that time - about the **first or second week of October** (1 mark).
- She should also make sure the crop will have **enough water (rain or irrigation) during those ten weeks**, and may sow in batches so that some maize is ready even if the weather changes (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Accept 'early October' or 'about the beginning of October' for the first mark.*

- A (flat bed) - **easy and quick to prepare** and suitable for small seeds sown close together on well-drained land (1 mark).
- B (ridges) - **give a deeper layer of loose soil and allow excess water to drain away**, and make weeding and earthing up easier (1 mark).
- C (mounds) - **give the deepest loose soil for large tubers such as yam** and drain very well on heavy or wet land (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: One advantage only for each bed; mark the first given.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- (i) **C, the mounds** (accept B, the ridges, where the reason is correct) (1 mark).
- (i) **Reason:** the crop is **raised above the wet soil, so excess water drains away and air can reach the roots** (1 mark).
- (ii) **Yam** (accept cassava or sweet potato) (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: The reason must mention drainage or air for the roots.*

**(d)** [3 marks]

- A bed of **deep, loose, well-drained soil lets the roots spread quickly** and take in water, air and nutrients, so the crop grows fast and **matures earlier** (1 mark).
- A **hard, compacted or waterlogged bed keeps air from the roots**, growth is slowed and the crop **matures late** or gives a poor yield (1 mark).
- A well-prepared bed gives **even germination and even moisture**, so the plants grow at the same rate and the whole crop is ready at about the same time (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: Award the third mark for any other correct point, such as the effect of the manure worked into the bed.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- This paper covers Sub-Strand 3: Crop Production for Basic 9, which the syllabus writes as two content standards: differences in the maturity of crops on different soils and seed beds, and the uses of crops at the different maturity stages.
- The syllabus expects the work to be done in the school or community garden. Questions 3 and 4 are therefore built around a class investigation rather than recall, and teachers should run such a study with the class before the examination.
- In Question 3(a) the unit cm must be written. Readings within one small square of the scheme are accepted, so 40 to 42 cm is correct for week 4 on soil Q.
- Question 4(a) is the design task. The three things that must appear are two comparable plots, identical treatment apart from the seed bed, and a record of the date of flowering. Neatness of expression is not marked.
- Question 5 draws on dishes the pupils know. Accept any local dish that fits the stage named; a candidate from the north may write tuo zaafi where the scheme says banku.
- Question 6(b) asks for a judgement. Candidates who agree with the advice have misread the length of the rains and cannot earn the reason marks; teach pupils to compare the length of the growing period with the length of the rains before choosing a variety.
- Follow-up work: keep a planting calendar on the classroom wall for the school farm, with the sowing date, the expected date of flowering and the expected date of harvest for every crop the class plants.
