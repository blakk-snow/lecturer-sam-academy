## MARKING SCHEME - SECTION B (60 marks)

*General rules: (1) Accept any other correct and relevant answer. (2) The names of the components must be used correctly: cell or battery, switch, resistor, diode, LED, capacitor and inductor. 'Bulb' written for LED earns no mark. (3) In a circuit diagram, the recognised symbols must be used and the circuit must be complete; a broken circuit loses the completeness mark. (4) 'Thermal' is accepted for a station that burns fuel and 'nuclear' for one that splits atoms; the two must not be interchanged. (5) Where a candidate gives more answers than are asked for, mark only the first ones up to the number required. (6) Do not deduct marks for spelling unless the meaning is changed.)*

  - *Marking note: Accept 'positive region' for P and 'negative region' for N.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- Any THREE of: **unplug the gadget and remove the cells first**; **never open a gadget connected to the mains; leave mains parts to the teacher**; **work under supervision with the proper tools**; **keep the parts safely and sketch the arrangement before removing anything**; **handle sharp edges with care and wash hands afterwards** (1 mark each).
  - *Marking note: 'Be careful' alone is too vague for a mark.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- **The LED becomes dimmer** (1 mark).
- **The total resistance of the circuit increases** (1 mark).
- **So the current falls, and the brightness depends on the current** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: No reason marks if the candidate says the LED becomes brighter.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- **The LED did not light** (1 mark).
- **The diode was reverse biased and a diode conducts in one direction only** (1 mark).
- **So no current flowed round the circuit** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: 'The diode was spoilt' earns no mark.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- **The components depend on one another; a change in one affects the working of the others** (1 mark).
- **Example: the resistor fixes the current, and the current decides the brightness of the LED (or protects it from damage)** (1 mark).
  - *Marking note: A definition with no example earns 1 mark only.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- Thermal and nuclear stations differ only in HOW the heat is obtained. Teach the common chain once - heat, steam, turbine, generator - and then show that one burns fuel while the other splits uranium nuclei. Question 2(c) rewards exactly that distinction, and points true of both stations earn nothing.
- The chimney is not the boiler. Candidates who label A in Fig. 2 'chimney' have seen a picture but have not followed the process.
- A real LED, two dry cells in a holder and a 220-ohm resistor cost very little and turn this whole sub-strand into a practical lesson. Once pupils have reversed an LED themselves and seen it refuse to light, Questions 4(b) and 6(b) answer themselves.
- Insist on correct circuit symbols and on a CLOSED loop. The commonest loss of marks in Question 4(a) is a diagram that leaves a gap or that shows the components side by side instead of one after another.
- Brightness follows current. Candidates should reason in three steps - more resistance, less current, dimmer LED - rather than asserting the answer.
- On safety: no class should open any gadget that can be plugged into the mains, and large capacitors can hold a charge long after a set is switched off. Make the safety rules of Question 5(c) part of the lesson, not an afterthought.
- Follow-up work: let groups prepare the posters and leaflets named in the curriculum, one group arguing for thermal and another for nuclear generation, and display them for the whole school during Science Week.
