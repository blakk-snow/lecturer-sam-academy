# PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT DOCUMENT (PDD)

## NUMBER ACADEMY

### A Free Interactive Number and Algebra Learning Web App

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Development Specification
**Platform:** Web / Progressive Web App (PWA)
**Primary Audience:** Students
**Secondary Audience:** Teachers / Course Administrators
**Content Basis:** *Learning, Teaching and Applying Number and Algebra* course handout

---

# 1. PRODUCT OVERVIEW

## 1.1 Product Name

**Number & Algebra Academy**

### Proposed tagline

> **Learn. Practise. Apply. Master.**

The product is a free, interactive web-based learning platform designed to help students learn, practise and apply Number and Algebra concepts through structured lessons, worked examples, interactive mathematics tools, practice activities and assessments.

The application will transform the educational structure of the supplied Number and Algebra course handout into an **interactive learning experience**, rather than simply reproducing the handout as an electronic textbook.

---

# 2. PRODUCT VISION

The vision is to create a simple, accessible and engaging mathematics learning environment where students can:

* learn concepts step by step;
* visualise mathematical ideas;
* practise immediately after learning;
* receive instant feedback;
* identify and correct misconceptions;
* apply mathematics to real-life problems;
* monitor their own progress;
* revisit difficult concepts;
* complete assessments;
* learn using a phone, tablet or computer;
* continue learning even when internet access is unavailable.

The application should feel like a **digital mathematics course**, not a PDF reader.

---

# 3. PROBLEM STATEMENT

Students often encounter difficulty with Number and Algebra because mathematical concepts can be abstract, interconnected and easily misunderstood.

The course handout itself places significant emphasis on:

* meanings of errors and misconceptions;
* barriers to learning Number and Algebra;
* conceptual understanding;
* appropriate teaching and learning methods;
* use of manipulatives and ICT;
* real-life applications;
* visualization;
* problem solving;
* functions and algebraic representations.

A conventional PDF or static website does not take full advantage of these possibilities.

The proposed application will therefore convert the course structure into:

**Content → Explanation → Visualization → Example → Practice → Feedback → Assessment → Progress**

# 14. LESSON DESIGN MODEL

The application should adapt the existing teaching structure into a digital learning cycle.

## Stage 1 — Think Back

Equivalent to:

**EOPT — Exercise on Previous Topic**

Purpose:

* activate prior knowledge;
* identify misconceptions;
* prepare the learner for the new topic.

---

## Stage 2 — Correction

The student reviews previous answers and receives explanations.

The system should not simply say:

> Wrong.

Instead:

> Your answer suggests that you may have confused the sign when subtracting an integer.

The exact feedback will depend on the question and identified misconception.

---

## Stage 3 — Learning Objectives

Each lesson should clearly display what students should be able to do.

Example:

### By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

* identify natural numbers;
* distinguish natural numbers from whole numbers;
* classify given numbers.

---

## Stage 4 — Observe

Present:

* image;
* diagram;
* animation;
* number line;
* interactive model;
* short video where appropriate.

---

## Stage 5 — Reading / Learning

The content should be divided into short sections rather than presenting large blocks of text.

Possible structure:

**Concept → Explanation → Example → Key idea**

---

## Stage 6 — Demonstration

Show a worked example step by step.

Students should be able to reveal each step rather than receiving the entire solution immediately.

---

## Stage 7 — Guided Practice

The system gives a problem and provides structured support.

---

## Stage 8 — Independent Practice

The student solves a new problem independently.

---

## Stage 9 — Immediate Feedback

Feedback should explain:

* whether the answer is correct;
* why it is correct;
* why an incorrect answer is incorrect;
* what misconception may have caused the error;
* how to try again.

---

## Stage 10 — Reflection

Students answer short prompts such as:

* What did you learn?
* Which part was difficult?
* Can you explain this concept in your own words?

---

## Stage 11 — Mastery Check

A short quiz determines whether the student has demonstrated sufficient understanding.

# 17. MISCONCEPTION ENGINE

This is one of the application's key differentiating features.

Instead of merely recording:

```text
Question: Wrong
```

the system should attempt to classify the error.

Example:

```javascript
{
  id: "integer-sign-001",

  question: "Calculate -3 + 5",

  correctAnswer: 2,

  misconceptions: [
    {
      answer: "-8",
      type: "sign-confusion",
      feedback:
        "You may have treated both numbers as negative. Remember that -3 + 5 means moving five units to the right from -3."
    }
  ]
}
```

This allows the application to become more instructional.

# 26. CONTENT ARCHITECTURE

Course content should be data-driven.

Do not hard-code lessons directly into React components.

Example:

```javascript
const lesson = {
  id: "u1-s3-l1",
  unitId: "unit-1",
  sectionId: "u1-s3",

  title: "Natural Numbers",

  objectives: [
    "Identify natural numbers",
    "Explain the meaning of natural numbers",
    "Classify given numbers"
  ],

  sections: [
    {
      type: "explanation",
      title: "What are Natural Numbers?",
      content: "..."
    },

    {
      type: "example",
      question: "...",
      steps: [
        "...",
        "...",
        "..."
      ]
    },

    {
      type: "interactive",
      component: "number-line"
    }
  ],

  practice: [
    "question-001",
    "question-002"
  ],

  quizId: "quiz-u1-s3"
};
```

This makes it possible to expand the course without rewriting application components.

The MVP is considered complete when a student can:

### Start

* open the website;
* create a simple local profile;
* enter the course.

### Learn

* select Unit 1;
* select a topic;
* read an interactive lesson;
* view examples;
* interact with a mathematical visualization.

### Practise

* answer practice questions;
* receive immediate feedback;
* retry incorrect questions.

### Assess

* complete a topic quiz;
* receive a score;
* see explanations.

### Progress

* see lesson completion;
* see topic mastery;
* see unit progress;
* resume where they stopped.

### Offline

* install the PWA;
* disconnect from the internet;
* continue accessing previously available course content;
* retain progress.

# 46. DEVELOPMENT PRINCIPLES

The development team should follow these principles.

## Principle 1 — Learning first

Every feature must answer:

> Does this help the student learn mathematics?

---

## Principle 2 — Interaction over decoration

An interactive number line is more valuable than an elaborate animation that teaches nothing.

---

## Principle 3 — Feedback over scoring

The system should explain mistakes, not merely mark them.

---

## Principle 4 — Mastery over completion

Finishing a lesson does not necessarily mean understanding it.

---

## Principle 5 — Offline first

The core learning experience should not depend on continuous internet access.

---

## Principle 6 — Data-driven content

Lessons and questions should be stored as structured data.

---

## Principle 7 — Reusable components

A single lesson renderer should support hundreds of lessons.

---

## Principle 8 — Mobile first

Design for the student's phone first, then expand to larger screens.

---

# 47. FUTURE AI FEATURES

AI should not be part of the MVP.

Potential later features include:

### AI Mathematics Tutor

Students could ask:

> "Why is my answer wrong?"

The AI could explain the relevant concept.

### AI Practice Generator

Generate additional questions based on weak topics.

### AI Misconception Analysis

Analyse repeated incorrect answers and recommend remedial lessons.

### AI Study Assistant

Create:

* summaries;
* revision questions;
* explanations;
* personalized practice.

However, AI should supplement—not replace—the carefully designed course content.

# 48. FUTURE COURSE EXPANSION

The architecture should allow additional courses to be added later.

For example:

```text
Academy
│
├── Number & Algebra
├── Geometry
├── Statistics
├── Measurement

Therefore, avoid naming database structures in a way that permanently assumes only one course.

Instead of:

```

prefer:

```javascript
courses
units
sections
lessons
questions
```

  ↓

  ↓
